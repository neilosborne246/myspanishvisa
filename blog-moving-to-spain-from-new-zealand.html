Moving to Spain from New Zealand: Complete 2026 Guide

A practical guide for New Zealanders planning to relocate to Spain — from the Working Holiday Visa option for under-30s to the Non-Lucrative Visa for longer-term residents, plus IRD tax rules, KiwiSaver, and everything else you need before you go.

Spain tends to resonate with New Zealanders in a way that few European destinations do. The outdoor lifestyle, the food culture, the sunshine — there is a recognisable quality of life that feels familiar to Kiwis, even across the other side of the planet. And in practical terms, Spain has become increasingly accessible: the visa pathways are clear, the country's growing English-speaking expat community makes settling in easier than it once was, and Spain's cost of living is significantly lower than Auckland or Wellington. Whether you are a working holidaymaker in your twenties, a remote worker, or planning for full retirement, this guide covers the key steps from Wellington to wherever in Spain you have in mind.

Do New Zealanders Need a Visa to Move to Spain?

New Zealand citizens can visit Spain — and the wider Schengen Area — for up to 90 days in any 180-day rolling period without a visa. This covers short holidays and exploratory trips. For longer residency, you need a Spanish long-stay Type D visa before you leave New Zealand. There is no in-country pathway to legal residency if you arrive on a visitor entry. Applications for all long-stay visa types are made at the Spanish Consulate General in Wellington.

Which Spanish Visa Is Right for You?

Working Holiday Visa — for New Zealanders aged 18 to 30

New Zealand and Spain have a bilateral Working Holiday Agreement, which means New Zealanders aged 18 to 30 (inclusive at the time of application) can apply for a Spain Working Holiday Visa. This visa allows you to live and work in Spain for up to one year, with the flexibility to take on employment, travel, and explore the country while you decide whether you want to stay long-term. It is issued for a single 12-month period and cannot be renewed in the same form — but it is an excellent starting point for younger Kiwis who want to experience Spain before committing to a longer visa route. Applications are made at the Spanish Consulate in Wellington.

Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) — for retirees and those with sufficient passive income

The NLV is Spain's long-stay residency route for people who do not need to work locally. It suits New Zealanders who are retired or who have sufficient income from investments, property, New Zealand Superannuation, KiwiSaver drawdowns, or other passive sources. For 2026, the income requirement is approximately €28,800 per year for the primary applicant, plus around €7,200 per year per dependent. You must hold comprehensive private health insurance valid in Spain and must not work for Spanish employers or Spanish clients. The NLV is issued for one year and is renewable in two-year blocks for up to five years, after which long-term residency can be applied for.

Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) — for remote workers serving NZ or international clients

New Zealanders who work remotely for New Zealand-based employers or for international clients can use Spain's Digital Nomad Visa to live in Spain legally while their professional life stays anchored outside Spain. The DNV is valid for one year and renewable for up to five years. It also provides optional access to Spain's Beckham Law, a flat-rate income tax of 24% on Spanish-source income, which may be attractive for higher earners during the initial residency period.

Student Visa

Kiwis enrolling at accredited Spanish universities, language schools, or vocational programmes can apply for a Student Visa covering their course duration. Part-time work is permitted; full-time employment is not.

Where New Zealanders Apply: Spanish Consulate in Wellington

All long-stay visa applications from New Zealand residents must be submitted in person at the Spanish Consulate General in Wellington. This is the only Spanish consular office in New Zealand. Processing times typically run four to eight weeks, though Working Holiday Visa processing can be faster. Book your appointment as early as possible once your documents are complete.

Documents New Zealand Applicants Need

  • Valid New Zealand passport — at least six months of validity remaining beyond your planned entry into Spain
  • New Zealand Police Vetting Service report (criminal record check) — apostilled through the New Zealand Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade (MFAT) and accompanied by a certified Spanish translation
  • Medical certificate from a registered New Zealand physician confirming no communicable diseases, apostilled and translated into Spanish
  • Proof of sufficient financial means — bank statements, investment account records, pension income evidence, or a combination meeting the NLV or DNV threshold
  • Comprehensive private health insurance valid in Spain — no copayments and with adequate coverage levels
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain — signed lease, property deed, or accommodation letter
  • Completed visa application form and passport photographs

The Working Holiday Visa application has a slightly simplified document list — but you still need proof of onward funds, return travel evidence, and the fee payment. Confirm the exact requirements with the Wellington consulate when you book, as specific requirements for Working Holiday applicants can differ from the standard NLV checklist.

New Zealand Taxes When You Move to Spain

New Zealand, like Canada and the UK, taxes on residency — not citizenship. Once you become a New Zealand tax non-resident (generally by leaving New Zealand permanently and spending fewer than 183 days in New Zealand per year), Inland Revenue (IRD) will no longer tax your worldwide income. This is a significant benefit compared to US citizens, who face ongoing worldwide taxation regardless of where they live.

Notifying Inland Revenue

When you leave New Zealand permanently, you should notify the IRD of your change in tax residency. You remain a NZ tax resident for the income year you depart until either the day you leave (if you are clearly leaving permanently) or until you have been absent for more than 325 days in any 12-month period. The IRD can provide guidance on your specific departure date position. Failing to notify the IRD creates ongoing filing obligations and potential back-tax exposure.

New Zealand-Source Income After Departure

Even as a non-resident, you may have New Zealand-source income that remains taxable in New Zealand — rental income from New Zealand property, dividends from New Zealand companies, and interest from New Zealand bank accounts are all subject to New Zealand non-resident withholding tax (NRWT). Rates depend on the type of income and are often reduced under the NZ-Spain double tax agreement. These income streams will also be reportable in Spain once you are a Spanish tax resident.

KiwiSaver: What Happens When You Emigrate

KiwiSaver is New Zealand's retirement savings scheme and it operates differently to pension funds in most other countries. If you emigrate permanently from New Zealand to Spain, you can apply to withdraw your entire KiwiSaver balance under the permanent emigration provisions, after at least one year has passed since you left New Zealand and can demonstrate permanent residency in Spain. The withdrawal is a one-time option — once taken, you cannot contribute to KiwiSaver again. Tax implications apply (government contributions have specific rules), and the NZD/EUR exchange rate at the time of withdrawal will significantly affect what you receive in euros. Get financial advice before making any withdrawal decision.

New Zealand Superannuation in Spain

New Zealand Superannuation (NZ Super) can be paid to you while you are living in Spain, though you must meet the usual eligibility criteria (age 65 and New Zealand resident for at least 10 years, with at least five of those years after age 50). NZ Super is paid in NZD and converted to euros by your bank at prevailing exchange rates. You must notify Work and Income New Zealand of your move and that you are living permanently overseas. Depending on your circumstances, any Spanish pension or equivalent income may be deducted from your NZ Super entitlement under the applicable rules — check with Work and Income before you depart.

After You Arrive in Spain

TIE Card (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero)

Within 30 days of arriving in Spain on a long-stay visa, apply for your TIE at the local Oficina de Extranjeros or Comisaría de Policía. Bring your passport, visa, proof of Spanish address, application forms (Modelo EX-17 for most cases), and the government fee payment (Modelo 790). A fingerprint appointment is part of the TIE process.

Empadronamiento (Municipal Registration)

Register your address at your local Ayuntamiento (town hall) to obtain your padrón certificate. This record of your Spanish address is required for your TIE, healthcare access, school enrolment, and a wide range of administrative tasks. Bring your passport and rental contract or property deed.

NIE Number

Your NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) is your Spanish identification number, required for all financial and legal transactions — opening a bank account, signing leases, buying property, and filing Spanish taxes. It is typically assigned as part of your visa and TIE process — confirm the timeline with the Wellington consulate before you depart.

Spanish Bank Account

Open a Spanish current account once your NIE is confirmed. You will need your passport, NIE, and proof of Spanish address. International transfers from New Zealand to your Spanish account via specialist forex providers (rather than your NZ bank's telegraphic transfer) typically offer significantly better exchange rates and lower fees — worth setting up before you go.

Practical Checklist for New Zealanders Moving to Spain

  • Determine your visa route: Working Holiday (under 30), NLV, DNV, or Student
  • Obtain your NZ Police vetting report and apostille it through MFAT
  • Get a medical certificate from your GP and apostille it
  • Gather financial evidence: 12 months of bank statements, investment records, or pension income letters
  • Purchase comprehensive private health insurance valid in Spain
  • Secure proof of Spanish accommodation
  • Book your appointment at the Spanish Consulate in Wellington early — waiting times can be several weeks
  • Notify IRD of your change in tax residency before or shortly after departure
  • Contact Work and Income NZ if you draw or will shortly draw NZ Super
  • Get KiwiSaver withdrawal advice if you intend to emigrate permanently
  • Set up a forex account for NZD-to-EUR transfers at competitive rates
  • Within 30 days of arrival: register at your Ayuntamiento and book your TIE appointment

Common Mistakes Kiwis Make When Moving to Spain

  • Arriving without a long-stay visa and expecting to regularise status from inside Spain — this is not possible
  • Missing the 30-day window to apply for the TIE after arrival
  • Submitting police clearance without apostille or without certified Spanish translation
  • Not notifying IRD before or on departure, creating ongoing NZ tax filing obligations
  • Making KiwiSaver withdrawal decisions without financial advice on timing and exchange rates
  • Purchasing health insurance with copayments that Spanish consulates will reject
  • Converting NZD to EUR through a New Zealand bank at unfavourable rates rather than using a specialist forex provider

Frequently Asked Questions

Do New Zealand citizens need a visa to move to Spain?
Yes. New Zealanders can visit Spain visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. For longer residency — retirement, remote work, or a permanent move — you must apply for a Spanish long-stay visa before leaving New Zealand at the Spanish Consulate General in Wellington. The Non-Lucrative Visa and Digital Nomad Visa are the most popular routes for longer-term moves.
Can New Zealanders under 30 use the Working Holiday Visa to live in Spain?
Yes. New Zealand has a bilateral Working Holiday Agreement with Spain. New Zealanders aged 18–30 (inclusive at the time of application) can apply for a Spain Working Holiday Visa, which allows you to live and work in Spain for up to one year. Applications are made at the Spanish Consulate in Wellington before departure.
What happens to my New Zealand taxes when I move to Spain?
New Zealand taxes on residency, not citizenship. Once you permanently leave NZ and become a non-resident, the IRD no longer taxes your worldwide income. You should notify the IRD of your change in residency status. NZ-source income (rental income, investments) may still attract New Zealand non-resident withholding tax.
What happens to my KiwiSaver when I move to Spain?
If you emigrate permanently to Spain, you may be eligible to withdraw your entire KiwiSaver balance after at least one year abroad and proof of permanent overseas residency. This is a one-time withdrawal. Get financial advice on timing and the NZD/EUR exchange rate before making any withdrawal decision.
Where do New Zealanders apply for a Spanish visa?
New Zealanders apply at the Spanish Consulate General in Wellington, which is the only Spanish consular office in New Zealand. All long-stay visa applications are submitted in person there. Processing typically takes four to eight weeks.
Can I receive NZ Superannuation while living in Spain?
Yes. NZ Super can be paid while you are living in Spain, subject to meeting standard eligibility criteria. You must notify Work and Income New Zealand of your overseas move. Payments are made in NZD and exchange rate fluctuations will affect the EUR value of your payments. Any Spanish pension income may be offset against your NZ Super entitlement depending on applicable rules.
