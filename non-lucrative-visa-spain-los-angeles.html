The Spanish Consulate in Los Angeles

The Consulate General of Spain in Los Angeles serves the largest geographic footprint of any Spanish consulate in the US — covering all of California, Arizona, Nevada, and Hawaii.

🏛️

Consulate General of Spain — Los Angeles
5055 Wilshire Blvd, Suite 860, Los Angeles, CA 90036
Covers: California, Arizona, Nevada, Hawaii
Website: exteriores.gob.es/losangeles

California alone generates one of the highest volumes of NLV applications in the United States. Appointment slots at this consulate are in high demand — expect 6–10 weeks to secure a slot. Start your application preparation well in advance, as delays in getting an appointment are often the biggest obstacle for West Coast applicants.

There is no operational Spanish consulate in San Francisco, Sacramento, San Diego, or elsewhere in California for NLV purposes. All California residents — regardless of which part of the state they live in — must apply in Los Angeles. The same applies to residents of Arizona, Nevada, and Hawaii.

California Applicants The high cost of living in California means many applicants are pleasantly surprised by how far their income stretches in Spain. A $4,000/month retirement income that feels modest in San Francisco is genuinely comfortable in cities like Valencia, Seville, or Granada.

Who Applies for the NLV from California?

California sends a diverse range of applicants to the LA consulate each year. The state's high cost of living makes Spain's lifestyle and NLV income thresholds particularly attractive.

Retirees from Southern & Northern California

From the retirement communities of San Diego and Orange County to Bay Area professionals ending long tech or finance careers, California retirees are the largest demographic of West Coast NLV applicants. Social Security combined with a 401(k) or pension typically exceeds the threshold comfortably.

Tech Workers Transitioning to Passive Income

Silicon Valley and LA's tech and entertainment industries produce applicants with vested RSUs, stock portfolios, and rental income who are transitioning out of active employment. The NLV suits those moving fully to passive income — but it's important to ensure your income structure qualifies as non-active before applying.

Hawaii residents — a smaller but consistent group — also apply through Los Angeles and often find Spain's Mediterranean climate and cost of living an appealing alternative. Arizona retirees with pension income from government or military careers are another strong applicant group for this consulate.

Income Requirements & Accepted Sources

The NLV financial threshold is based on Spain's IPREM index. For 2026, a single applicant needs approximately $2,600/month; a couple approximately $4,000/month (USD equivalents — confirm the current rate at time of application).

Common Income Sources for West Coast Applicants

  • Social Security: SSA benefit verification letter plus 3–6 months of bank statements confirming deposits.
  • California state pension / CalPERS / CalSTRS: Pension award letter plus bank statements.
  • 401(k) / IRA distributions: Regular scheduled distributions documented with account statements and bank confirmation.
  • Investment dividends & interest: Brokerage statements from Schwab, Fidelity, Vanguard, or similar.
  • California rental income: Schedule E from Form 1040 plus current lease agreements.
  • Vested RSU dividends / stock dividends: Must be passive distributions — not active compensation.
  • Military retirement pay: DFAS statements confirming monthly amounts.
⚠️

Active income does not qualify. If you're still receiving a salary, active freelance income, or compensation from a US employer, those sources cannot be used to meet the NLV threshold. The visa is specifically for non-active, passive income. Consulting a specialist before applying is strongly advised if your income mix includes active elements.

The 8-Step Application Process from California

Plan for 7–10 months from starting your preparation to arriving in Spain. Appointment availability at the LA consulate is the most common bottleneck for West Coast applicants.

01

Order Your FBI Identity History Summary

Start here, immediately. Use an FBI-approved channeler service for fast turnaround (3–5 business days for the check itself). Standard direct processing takes 12–18 weeks and is not recommended for NLV timelines.

02

Apostille the FBI Check (US State Department)

Mail the FBI check to the US State Department Authentications Office in Washington D.C. California cannot apostille federal documents — only the State Department can. Standard processing: 6–8 weeks. Use an expedited service if needed.

03

Complete Your Medical Certificate

Visit any licensed physician in California for your NLV medical certificate. It must be translated into Spanish by a certified translator and is valid for 90 days. Time it so it remains valid on your consulate appointment date.

04

Purchase Spanish Private Health Insurance

Buy a policy from a Spain-registered insurer (established private health insurers in Spain, or similar). The policy must have no copays, no exclusions for pre-existing conditions, and cover you for the full visa period. Medicare does not count.

05

Assemble Financial Documentation

Collect 6 months of bank statements, income award letters, your most recent Form 1040, brokerage statements, and any other relevant proof. Have certified Spanish translations prepared. A letter from a CPA or financial advisor summarizing your income can strengthen borderline cases.

06

Complete Form EX-01 (Visa Application)

Download Spain's national long-stay visa application (Form EX-01). Complete it in full — in Spanish or with certified translation. Print two originals and sign both. Do not date the form more than a few weeks before your appointment.

07

Attend In-Person Appointment in Los Angeles

Appear in person at the Consulate General of Spain, 5055 Wilshire Blvd, Suite 860, Los Angeles, CA 90036. Bring originals and copies of all documents. Pay the visa fee (approximately €80–€100 per applicant). Biometrics may be collected at this stage.

08

Await Decision & Collect Visa

Processing takes 10–16 weeks. Upon approval, collect your visa in person or via an authorized representative. Travel to Spain within the visa's validity window and register at your local Foreigners' Office within 30 days of arrival.

Document Checklist for LA Consulate Applicants

Identity & Legal

  • Valid US passport (6+ months remaining, 2 blank pages)
  • 2"×2" passport photos (white background, recent)
  • Form EX-01 (2 signed copies)
  • FBI Identity History Summary + US State Dept apostille
  • Certified Spanish translation of FBI check

Health & Insurance

  • Medical certificate from US-licensed physician
  • Certified Spanish translation of medical certificate
  • Spain-registered private health insurance policy
  • Insurance certificate showing no copays, full coverage

Financial Proof

  • 6 months bank statements (all accounts)
  • Social Security / pension award letters
  • 401(k) / IRA distribution schedule or statements
  • Most recent Form 1040 US tax return
  • Brokerage / investment account statements
  • Rental income: Schedule E + lease agreements
  • Certified Spanish translations of key docs

Accommodation & Fees

  • Proof of accommodation in Spain (lease, deed, hotel)
  • Visa fee (approx €80–€100, typically money order)

Frequently Asked Questions — Los Angeles / California Applicants

Do I need to go to the LA consulate if I live in San Francisco?

Yes. There is currently no Spanish consulate in San Francisco processing Non-Lucrative Visa applications. The Consulate General of Spain in Los Angeles (5055 Wilshire Blvd, Suite 860, CA 90036) is the only consulate with jurisdiction over all of California. Whether you live in San Francisco, Sacramento, San Diego, or anywhere else in the state, you must travel to Los Angeles for your in-person appointment.

Does California have its own apostille service for the FBI check?

No. The California Secretary of State's office apostilles California state-issued documents — things like California state police checks, birth certificates, and court records. But the FBI Identity History Summary is a federal document. Only the US State Department Authentications Office in Washington D.C. can apostille it. You'll need to mail the original FBI check to the State Department, or use a private apostille handling service.

Can tech workers or people with stock income qualify for the NLV?

It depends on whether the income is truly passive. Dividends from vested RSUs, dividends from stock holdings, and capital gains from investment accounts can qualify. But active employment income, active consulting fees, or compensation from a US employer does not — the NLV prohibits work activity. If you've fully transitioned to passive income, the NLV can work. If your income is mixed, you need specialist guidance on how to structure your application.

Do Arizona, Nevada, and Hawaii residents apply at the LA consulate?

Yes. The Consulate General of Spain in Los Angeles has jurisdiction over California, Arizona, Nevada, and Hawaii. Applicants from Phoenix, Tucson, Las Vegas, and Honolulu all apply in Los Angeles. There are no Spanish consulates in those states for this purpose. You'll need to make the trip to LA for your appointment.

Can I combine rental income from a California property with investment income?

Yes. Multiple passive income sources are added together for the NLV threshold. California rental income (documented via Schedule E on your Form 1040 and current lease agreements) can be combined with dividends, Social Security, pension payments, or any other recurring passive income. The total must consistently exceed approximately $2,600/month for a single applicant in 2026.

How early should I book my consulate appointment?

As early as possible — ideally as soon as you start assembling your documents. The LA consulate is one of the busiest Spanish consulates in the US. Appointment slots regularly open 6–10 weeks in advance. Check the portal frequently, including early mornings, as cancellations do appear. Do not wait until all your documents are ready before looking for appointment dates — many applicants are caught with completed documents but no available appointment for months.
