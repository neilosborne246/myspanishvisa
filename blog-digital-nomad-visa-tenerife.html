Digital Nomad Visa Tenerife: The Canary Islands Option for Remote Workers

Tenerife gets considered by digital nomads for obvious reasons — year-round sun, affordable living, good flights from Europe, and it's in Spain so the Digital Nomad Visa applies. But there's an honest conversation to have before you book your one-way ticket.

Tenerife is genuinely not Spain's top nomad destination. That title belongs to Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, which has official government support, a deeper coworking infrastructure, and a more established nomad community. That said, Tenerife works for the right kind of nomad — and this guide will tell you whether that's you.

Is Tenerife Good for Digital Nomads? (The Honest Answer)

Yes and no, depending on what you need. If you value climate, cost, and a relaxed lifestyle over a buzzing nomad community and unlimited coworking options, Tenerife can be excellent. If you need hot desks, networking events, and daily contact with other remote workers, you'll find Las Palmas de Gran Canaria more rewarding.

The south of Tenerife — the resort zone — is gorgeous but thin on proper work infrastructure. Santa Cruz de Tenerife (the capital, north-east) has better coworking options and faster fibre connections, but has a less tropical feel than the sun-bleached south. Be honest with yourself about what you're prioritising. The island can deliver on climate and cost. It cannot yet deliver on community and coworking in the way Las Palmas can.

Best Areas for Remote Working in Tenerife

Santa Cruz de Tenerife

The capital, situated in the north-east of the island, offers the best infrastructure for remote workers. This is a real city with proper fibre internet, coworking spaces, business services, and a genuine working environment. It lacks the sun-drenched beach lifestyle of the south, but for nomads who need reliable daily working conditions, Santa Cruz is the obvious base.

La Laguna

Adjacent to Santa Cruz, La Laguna is a UNESCO-listed university town with some café culture suited to laptop working. The pace is slower, the architecture beautiful, and the presence of the university adds a certain intellectual energy. Dedicated coworking is thin on the ground, but the town has a genuine character that appeals to nomads who work independently.

Puerto de la Cruz

Located on the north coast, Puerto de la Cruz has good fibre availability, a genuine town feel rather than a resort strip, and reasonable café options for working. Dedicated coworking spaces are sparse, but some nomads make it work through café-hopping. The climate is slightly cooler and wetter than the south — which is still very good by most standards.

South Resort Zone (Costa Adeje, Los Cristianos)

Beautiful, reliably warm, and well-connected to the airport. Coworking infrastructure is genuinely limited, though, and this needs to be said plainly. Most nomads who base themselves in the resort south work from their apartments or from café tables. This works fine if you're disciplined, self-contained, and don't need coworking for structure or community — but if you do, the south will fall short of your expectations.

Coworking Spaces in Tenerife

The coworking scene in Tenerife is smaller than Las Palmas, and it's important to go in with that expectation set correctly.

In Santa Cruz de Tenerife, there are genuine options — The Cluster is among the established coworking providers, and several business centres offer hot desks and day passes. The sector is growing but not extensive: you're not going to find the density of spaces that exists in a major mainland city or in Las Palmas.

In the south — Costa Adeje and Los Cristianos — dedicated coworking is very limited. Some serviced office providers operate in Adeje and the situation has improved marginally in recent years, but it remains patchy. If you arrive expecting a thriving coworking ecosystem in the resort zone, you will be disappointed.

Cafés with decent wifi are scattered across the island. Tenerife has a good café culture, and working from a café is a viable day-to-day option in most towns. But a café is a different proposition to a structured coworking environment — there's no community, no reliable private call space, and no sense of working infrastructure around you.

Honest summary: if coworking community matters to your work or your lifestyle, Tenerife will disappoint relative to Las Palmas. If you work independently and want a quality-of-life base with great weather, the island delivers.

Internet Connectivity

Fibre coverage is solid in Tenerife's urban areas and increasingly available across the island:

  • Santa Cruz de Tenerife, La Laguna, and Puerto de la Cruz all have good fibre infrastructure from major providers
  • The south resort areas (Adeje, Los Cristianos) have improved significantly — most modern apartment buildings now have fibre access
  • Major providers: Movistar, Orange, Vodafone, and DIGI (which offers competitive pricing)
  • Typical speeds in a fibre-connected apartment: 300Mbps to 1Gbps symmetric
  • Coworking spaces — where they exist — generally offer solid, reliable connections
  • Rural and interior areas can still have connectivity gaps; always verify the specific property's connection before signing a rental agreement
  • 5G and 4G coverage is strong enough in resort areas that phone tethering serves as a viable backup if a building's fibre setup is poor

For most nomads based in urban Tenerife, internet connectivity is not the issue it once was. Check the specific address — not the area — before committing.

Cost of Living for Nomads vs Mainland Spain

The Canary Islands operate under their own tax regime, which translates into meaningfully lower everyday costs. The islands use IGIC (a local tax) at 7% rather than mainland Spain's VAT at 21%. That difference shows up in restaurant bills, services, utilities, and much else.

  • 1-bedroom apartment (north, Santa Cruz / Puerto de la Cruz area): €650–€900/month
  • 1-bedroom apartment (south resort zones): €750–€1,000/month
  • Coworking hot desk (where available): €150–€300/month
  • Menú del día lunch: €10–€12
  • Groceries for one: €200–€280/month
  • Utilities: €70–€110/month (the mild climate keeps heating/cooling costs low)
  • Monthly all-in for a working nomad living reasonably: €1,300–€1,900

This is significantly cheaper than Lisbon, Barcelona, or Madrid for an equivalent lifestyle. It's broadly comparable to — or slightly above — Las Palmas de Gran Canaria depending on the area. Nomads with a lean budget who work independently will find Tenerife very comfortable at the lower end of these numbers.

The Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) — Requirements Overview

Spain's Digital Nomad Visa allows non-EU citizens to live in Spain while working remotely for employers or clients based outside Spain. The Canary Islands are fully covered by the DNV — choosing Tenerife as your base does not change the requirements or the process.

Key requirements for 2026:

  • Evidence of remote work — either an employment contract with a non-Spanish employer, or self-employment with at least 80% of income coming from non-Spanish sources
  • Minimum income of approximately €2,646/month (equivalent to three times the Spanish minimum wage, 2026 figures)
  • Private health insurance with full coverage in Spain, no exclusions, no co-payments
  • Clean criminal record certificate from all countries of residence in the past five years
  • No Spanish tax residency in the last five years
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain

The DNV is applied for at your Spanish consulate before you travel. It is initially granted for one year (or three years if you enter Spain on a tourist visa and apply in-country). The Beckham Law tax regime is available to DNV holders — a flat 24% income tax rate instead of Spain's progressive rates, applicable for up to six years. This is a significant financial benefit worth understanding before you apply.

The Canary Islands' special economic zone status and lower tax burden make the islands a financially attractive DNV base, even before the Beckham Law benefit is factored in. To start your DNV application, visit our Digital Nomad Visa portal.

The Nomad Community in Tenerife

The nomad community in Tenerife is smaller and less organised than Las Palmas — and it's worth being upfront about that. The island is increasingly on nomads' radar, and the community is growing, but it has not yet reached the critical mass that gives Las Palmas its character.

Santa Cruz has more genuine community than the south. The north of the island attracts longer-term residents rather than the transient visitors who cycle through the resort zones. Online communities — Facebook groups, Nomad List, Reddit — show growing Tenerife representation, and in-person meetups do happen.

Where Tenerife genuinely excels for nomads who care about it is outdoor connection. Hiking Mount Teide, surfing the north coast, water sports, cycling, and spectacular walking routes are connectors that bring people together in a different way to coworking events. The island's outdoor offering is world-class, and for nomads whose identity involves active outdoor life alongside remote work, Tenerife can be enormously appealing.

Honest verdict: if community is a priority for your remote working life, Las Palmas wins. If climate, personal productivity, and lifestyle — including outdoors — matter more, Tenerife is a solid choice that rewards the right kind of nomad.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Tenerife good for digital nomads?
It depends on your working style. Tenerife offers excellent climate, affordable living, and good internet in urban areas. However, its coworking infrastructure is limited compared to Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. Nomads who work independently and value lifestyle over networking will thrive here; those who rely on coworking community and events will find Las Palmas more rewarding.
What coworking spaces are in Tenerife?
Santa Cruz de Tenerife has the most options, including The Cluster and several serviced office providers with hot desk facilities. The south resort zone — Costa Adeje and Los Cristianos — has very limited dedicated coworking. Cafés with good wifi are available across the island but are a different proposition to a structured coworking environment.
How fast is the internet in Tenerife?
Excellent in urban areas. Fibre connections of 300Mbps to 1Gbps are common in Santa Cruz, La Laguna, and Puerto de la Cruz. The south resort areas have improved significantly. Rural and interior areas are patchier — always verify the connection at a specific property before signing a rental agreement.
Can I apply for the Digital Nomad Visa to live in Tenerife?
Yes. The Spanish Digital Nomad Visa applies equally across all Spanish territory, including the Canary Islands. You apply through your Spanish consulate before arrival. The requirements are the same as for mainland Spain: remote work evidence, minimum income, health insurance, and clean background. The Canary Islands' lower tax rates and special economic zone status make Tenerife a financially attractive DNV destination.
How does Tenerife compare to Las Palmas for nomads?
Honestly, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria has the edge for most nomads. It has an official government-backed remote work programme, more coworking spaces including the popular El Cubo hub, a larger and more organised nomad community, and is a proper city rather than a resort island. Tenerife offers better beach resort lifestyle and arguably more dramatic scenery, but nomads who need coworking infrastructure and community should look seriously at Las Palmas first.
What is the cost of living in Tenerife for a digital nomad?
Affordable. A digital nomad in Tenerife can live comfortably on €1,300–€1,900 per month including rent, food, utilities, and leisure. The Canary Islands have their own lower tax rate — IGIC at 7% instead of mainland VAT at 21% — which reduces everyday costs. Compared to Lisbon, Barcelona, or Madrid, Tenerife offers strong value for money.
