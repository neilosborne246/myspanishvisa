Cost of Living in Spain 2026: Monthly Budgets for Expats

What does life in Spain actually cost? We break down realistic monthly budgets across different cities and lifestyles — and compare directly to what you'd spend in the UK and US.

Cost of living is the single most common question we get from people considering a move to Spain. The answer is more nuanced than a simple headline figure: Spain is genuinely cheaper than the UK, US, Canada, or Australia in most categories, but the gap varies considerably depending on which city you choose, how you live, and what you're comparing against.

This guide gives you concrete monthly budget estimates across three lifestyle tiers and three representative cities, with direct comparisons to London and New York so the numbers land in context.

The Big Picture: Spain vs UK vs US

Before getting into city-by-city detail, here's how Spain compares at a macro level for the categories that matter most to incoming expats:

  • Rent: Typically 40–65% cheaper than equivalent London zones; 30–55% cheaper than major US cities
  • Groceries: Around 25–35% cheaper than the UK; 20–30% cheaper than mid-range US cities
  • Eating out: Dramatically cheaper — a three-course lunch at a local restaurant costs €10–€15 in Spain, with nothing remotely equivalent at that price in London or New York
  • Private health insurance: A comprehensive policy for a healthy 60-year-old runs €150–€300 per month in Spain; equivalent US private coverage is typically $600–$1,200+ per month
  • Public transport: Spain's metro and bus systems are cheap and functional — monthly passes in Madrid and Barcelona cost around €55–€75, versus £165+ for a London Zone 1-2 Travelcard
  • Utilities: Gas and electricity are comparable to or slightly cheaper than UK rates; internet is fast and affordable at €30–€50 per month for fibre

What the Non-Lucrative Visa Income Requirement Means in Practice

The Non-Lucrative Visa requires you to demonstrate income of approximately €2,400 per month for a single applicant (2026 figures — reviewed annually against the Spanish IPREM). Each additional family member adds roughly €600 per month to the requirement.

Looking at the budgets above, this income level comfortably covers a good life in mid-tier Spanish cities and a careful but enjoyable life in Madrid or Barcelona. It is set at this level to ensure residents can support themselves without needing Spanish employment — and in practice, most NLV holders find they can live well within it.

For couples with a combined income of €3,000–€4,000 per month, sharing accommodation costs makes Spain feel genuinely comfortable almost anywhere in the country.

Rent: The Biggest Variable

Rent is the single biggest swing factor in any budget. A few important considerations:

  • Rental costs have risen significantly in Barcelona, Madrid, and Valencia over the past five years and continue to increase
  • Furnished rentals often command a premium over unfurnished; most Spanish long-term rentals are unfurnished
  • Short-term (less than one year) rentals cost considerably more than long-term contracts
  • Seasonal rental markets in tourist areas can be unpredictable — coastal towns have less supply of year-round rentals because owners can earn more from summer tourist lets
  • Buying property eliminates rental cost uncertainty but requires significant capital and adds purchase costs of 10–12% above the purchase price

Food and Dining

Spanish food costs are one of the most genuinely pleasant surprises for newly arrived expats. Spanish supermarkets — Mercadona, Lidl, Carrefour, Día, and Alcampo are the main chains — are well-stocked and affordable. Fresh produce, particularly fruit, vegetables, olive oil, and fish, is exceptional quality and very reasonably priced.

Eating out is where the difference from the UK and US is most dramatic. The menú del día — a set three-course lunch offered at almost every local restaurant — typically costs €10–€15 and includes water or wine. This is not fast food: it is a proper sit-down meal with a starter, main, dessert, and coffee. Nothing like it exists at that price point in London or New York. For expats who enjoy lunch as a social occasion, this transforms the economics of daily life.

Transport

Spain's public transport in cities is good and cheap. Monthly passes in Madrid (€56), Barcelona (€60–€80 depending on zones), and Valencia (€40) compare very favourably with London or major US cities.

Outside cities, a car becomes considerably more useful — particularly in coastal and rural areas. Fuel costs in Spain are lower than in the UK (around €1.50–€1.65 per litre in 2026). Car insurance is also generally cheaper than UK equivalent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Spain cheap to live in compared to the UK?
Yes, significantly. Rent in Spain's major cities runs at roughly 40–60% of equivalent London costs. Food is cheaper both in supermarkets and eating out. Healthcare private insurance is far lower than comparable UK private cover. Most expats who move from the UK to Spain find their monthly outgoings fall meaningfully even if they maintain a similar lifestyle standard.
How much do I need to live comfortably in Spain?
It depends on where you live. In a mid-sized city like Valencia or Seville, a single person can live comfortably for €1,400–€1,900 per month. In Barcelona or Madrid, expect €2,000–€2,800 for the same lifestyle. In smaller towns or rural areas, €1,100–€1,500 is often sufficient. Couples can share many costs and often live well for €2,000–€3,200 combined in mid-tier cities.
What is the minimum income required for a Non-Lucrative Visa?
For 2026, the Non-Lucrative Visa income requirement is set at approximately 400% of the Spanish IPREM. For a single applicant this works out to approximately €2,400 per month, or roughly €28,800 per year. Each additional family member requires around an additional €600 per month. These figures are reviewed annually.
Is it cheaper to rent or buy in Spain as an expat?
For most newly arrived expats, renting first is the sensible approach — it gives you time to understand local property markets and find the right neighbourhood. Purchase costs in Spain add approximately 10–12% on top of the purchase price (transfer tax, notary, registry). If you intend to stay long-term, buying can make strong financial sense given Spain's lower price-to-rent ratios compared to the UK.
How does food cost compare in Spain versus the US?
Spain is generally cheaper for food than major US cities. A weekly grocery shop for one person costs approximately €50–€75 in a standard supermarket. Restaurant meals are significantly cheaper — a three-course menú del día (set lunch) at a local restaurant typically costs €10–€15 including wine or water, with no US equivalent at that price point.
Do I need private health insurance to live in Spain?
Non-EU expats on a Non-Lucrative Visa or Digital Nomad Visa are required to hold private health insurance — it is a mandatory visa condition. Policies from Spanish insurers typically cost €80–€200 per month depending on your age and coverage level, which is considerably less than equivalent private health insurance in the US, UK, or Australia.
