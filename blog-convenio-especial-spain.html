Convenio Especial Spain: Access Public Healthcare as a Resident

Spain's voluntary pay-in healthcare scheme lets residents access the public health system without automatic entitlement. Here is exactly how it works, what it costs, and who can apply.

Home / Blog / Convenio Especial Spain: Access Public Healthcare as a Resident

One of the first questions expats ask after arriving in Spain is how to access the healthcare system. If you are not working for a Spanish employer or registered as self-employed, you do not automatically qualify for public healthcare through Social Security contributions. That is where the Convenio Especial comes in — a voluntary agreement that lets qualifying residents pay a fixed monthly fee in exchange for full access to Spain's public health network.

This scheme is particularly relevant for people on the Non-Lucrative Visa, retirees living off pension income, and anyone who has settled in Spain outside of the employment system. Understanding how it works could save you significant money compared to private health insurance, especially if you have pre-existing health conditions.

What Is the Convenio Especial?

The Convenio Especial de Prestación de Asistencia Sanitaria is a voluntary agreement between an individual and the Spanish Social Security system (or regional health authority, depending on where you live). In plain terms: you pay a fixed monthly contribution, and in return you receive the same access to Spain's public healthcare network — the Sistema Nacional de Salud (SNS) — as a worker who contributes through their salary.

This is not a private insurance product. It plugs you into the public system directly, which means you get a GP, access to specialists, hospital care, and emergency treatment just as any other public healthcare beneficiary would. The critical distinction from private health insurance is that the Convenio Especial does not exclude pre-existing conditions. If you have a long-term health condition that makes private insurance expensive or simply unavailable, this scheme can be genuinely life-changing.

The Convenio Especial is also an individual agreement. It does not cover your spouse, partner, or children automatically. Each family member who wants public healthcare access must apply and pay separately.

Who Can Apply?

Not everyone is eligible. The Spanish government has set clear qualifying criteria, and you must satisfy all of them before submitting an application:

  • Twelve months of continuous legal residency: You must have been registered on the local municipal census (the padrón) for at least one uninterrupted year. This requirement makes the Convenio Especial particularly popular with NLV holders approaching their first renewal — by that point, the 12-month residency threshold is typically met.
  • No existing entitlement to public healthcare: The scheme is specifically for people who fall outside the Social Security contribution system. If you are employed in Spain, registered as autónomo, or already have healthcare access through another route, you cannot use the Convenio Especial.
  • No S1 form or bilateral agreement coverage: UK retirees drawing a state pension can often access Spanish public healthcare through an S1 form under the UK-EU Withdrawal Agreement, provided they registered before Brexit deadlines. If that applies to you, you do not need — and cannot use — the Convenio Especial. The same logic applies to EU citizens whose home country health system covers them under European reciprocal arrangements.
  • Individual applications only: As noted above, each person must apply independently. There is no family membership option.

What Does It Cost?

The cost is fixed nationally and does not vary based on your income, wealth, or where in Spain you live. The 2026 rates are:

  • Under 65 years old: €60 per month
  • 65 or older: €157 per month

These fees are reviewed periodically but have remained stable in recent years. Unlike private insurance, there is no medical underwriting, no premium loading for health history, and no renewal surprises. You pay the flat rate, and your access to the SNS is maintained for as long as you keep paying.

For context, a comprehensive private health insurance policy in Spain for a person with any significant medical history can easily run to €200–€400 per month or more. For healthier individuals under 65, private insurance may be cheaper — but it will likely exclude pre-existing conditions. The Convenio Especial does not.

What Is (and Is Not) Covered?

The Convenio Especial gives you access to the same broad range of services available to all SNS beneficiaries:

  • Primary care — GP appointments, nurse consultations, and referrals
  • Specialist consultations through the public system
  • Hospital treatment, including planned surgery and overnight stays
  • Emergency care at any public hospital
  • Pre-existing conditions — covered from day one with no waiting periods
  • Mental health services through the public network

However, there are meaningful gaps you need to plan for:

  • Prescription medicines: Convenio Especial holders pay 100% of the cost of prescription medications at the pharmacy. Standard public healthcare beneficiaries receive subsidised prescriptions; this scheme does not carry that benefit.
  • Non-urgent patient transport: Scheduled ambulance journeys for non-emergency appointments are not covered.
  • Dental care: The SNS provides very limited dental services (primarily emergency extractions). For routine dental care, fillings, or any significant dental work, you will need either a separate dental plan or to pay out of pocket.
  • Optical care: Eye tests and glasses are not covered through the public system and are not included in the Convenio Especial.

Many expats using the Convenio Especial add a low-cost supplementary plan to cover dental and prescription top-ups. This combination can still work out considerably cheaper than a full private health policy.

How to Apply: Step by Step

Because Spain's health system is decentralised, the exact process varies depending on which autonomous community you live in. The broad steps are as follows:

Step 1: Confirm you meet the eligibility criteria

Check that you have 12 months of continuous padrón registration, that you are not entitled to public healthcare through any other route, and that you do not hold an S1 form or equivalent bilateral coverage.

Step 2: Gather your documents

You will generally need: your valid passport; your NIE number and TIE residency card; your certificado de empadronamiento (the official certificate from your town hall confirming your registration address and how long you have been registered); and a certificate of non-exportation of health rights — a formal document from your home country's health authority confirming that they are not providing healthcare coverage for you in Spain. This last document can take time to obtain, so request it early.

Step 3: Find the correct regional authority

Depending on where you live, you will apply through either your regional health authority (consejería de salud) or your local Social Security office (INSS). The Spanish Ministry of Health maintains a directory of regional health authorities on its website. Each region has its own application form — download the one for your autonomous community.

Step 4: Submit and set up payment

Submit your completed application with all supporting documents, either in person at the relevant office or online if your region supports digital applications (a Spanish digital certificate or Cl@ve account is usually required for online submissions). Once approved, you will sign the agreement, set up a direct debit for the monthly fee, and receive your SIP card — the public healthcare card that identifies you as an SNS beneficiary.

The Convenio Especial and Your NLV

One common question is whether the Convenio Especial can replace private health insurance when renewing a Non-Lucrative Visa. The NLV requires you to maintain health insurance coverage in Spain, and the initial application must be supported by a comprehensive private policy with no co-payments or deductibles.

At renewal stage, some consulates accept the Convenio Especial as satisfactory proof of healthcare coverage, since it provides genuine, funded access to the public health system. However, practice varies between consulates, and this is an area where getting specific advice before your renewal is strongly recommended. Do not cancel your private policy on the assumption that the Convenio Especial will automatically be accepted — confirm the position first with a specialist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is eligible for the Convenio Especial in Spain?
You must have been legally resident and registered on the padrón for at least 12 continuous months, have no entitlement to public healthcare through employment, social security contributions, or an S1 form, and not be covered under a bilateral health agreement. Each applicant must apply individually — dependents are not automatically included.
How much does the Convenio Especial cost per month?
The flat monthly fee is €60 if you are under 65, and €157 if you are 65 or older. These rates are set nationally and do not vary by income or region, though the application process itself is managed by regional health authorities.
Does the Convenio Especial cover prescription medicines?
No. Prescription medication costs are excluded from the Convenio Especial. Unlike standard public healthcare beneficiaries who receive subsidised prescriptions, Convenio Especial holders pay the full retail price at the pharmacy.
Can I use the Convenio Especial to satisfy the health insurance requirement for an NLV renewal?
Potentially yes — but this depends on the consulate processing your renewal. Some consulates accept the Convenio Especial as sufficient proof of health coverage for NLV renewals, while others continue to require a comprehensive private policy. Always confirm the current position with a qualified immigration specialist before cancelling your private insurance.
Does the Convenio Especial cover pre-existing conditions?
Yes, and this is one of its biggest advantages over private health insurance. The Convenio Especial gives you access to the full public healthcare system (SNS), which does not exclude pre-existing conditions. If you have a medical history that makes private insurance expensive or impossible to obtain, this scheme can be a practical alternative.
How do I apply for the Convenio Especial?
The application is submitted to your regional health authority or local Social Security office (INSS). You will need your passport, NIE or TIE card, certificado de empadronamiento proving 12 months of continuous registration, and a certificate of non-exportation of health rights from your home country. The exact form and process varies by autonomous community.
