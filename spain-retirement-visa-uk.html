The Spain Retirement Visa for UK Citizens: Post-Brexit Reality

Since January 2021, UK citizens need a visa to live in Spain. The Non-Lucrative Visa is the primary route for British retirees.

Before Brexit, British nationals could live in Spain as EU citizens — registering locally with no visa required. That changed on 1 January 2021. UK nationals are now treated as third-country nationals and must apply for a specific Spanish visa to live in Spain long-term.

The Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) is Spain's retirement and passive income visa. It allows you to live in Spain without working, provided you can demonstrate sufficient passive income — UK State Pension, private pension, savings, rental income from UK property, or investment income. Despite the extra bureaucracy, British nationals continue to move to Spain in large numbers. The lifestyle, climate, and healthcare are consistently worth it.

💡

Key post-Brexit change: UK nationals no longer have free movement. You cannot simply arrive and register. You must apply through BLS International (the authorised visa application service for Spain in the UK), submit a full document dossier, demonstrate income, and obtain a visa before moving to Spain permanently.

Eligibility for British Nationals: What You Need

UK applicants face the standard NLV requirements, plus specific UK-only documentation.

  • Valid UK passport: Must have at least 6 months' validity beyond your intended stay. Plus two recent passport photographs.
  • Passive income proof: Approximately €2,400/month (around £2,050) for a single applicant. UK State Pension alone is rarely sufficient — most applicants supplement with savings, private pension, or rental income.
  • ACRO criminal record certificate: The ACRO Standard Disclosure is the specific criminal record check Spain requires. A DBS check is NOT accepted. Apply at acro.police.uk (£45 standard, £98 fast-track).
  • Medical certificate: Signed by a GMC-registered doctor confirming you have no public health risk conditions. Must be on the correct template, apostilled, and translated.
  • Private health insurance: A Spanish-compliant private policy with comprehensive coverage — no copayments, no coverage gaps. NHS EHIC/GHIC cards do not satisfy this requirement. Annual cost: £600–£2,200 depending on age.
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain: Signed rental contract, property purchase deed, or formal invitation.
  • All documents apostilled (FCDO) and translated by sworn translator

Income Requirements for British Applicants (2026)

Meeting the income threshold is the most common challenge for UK retirees. Here's exactly what counts — and how to present it.

2026 Income Thresholds

Single applicant: €2,400/month (approximately £2,050)

Each dependent (spouse, child): ~€600/month extra

GBP/EUR reference rate: 1 EUR = 0.85 GBP (April 2026). Check current rates when calculating.

Acceptable UK income sources:

  • UK State Pension: Counts. Request a DWP pension statement or benefits letter. Note: the full new State Pension (~£11,500/year) equates to under £1,000/month — typically below the threshold alone.
  • Private pension / final salary scheme: Statement from provider on official letterhead showing monthly income.
  • Annuity income: Statement from annuity provider showing guaranteed monthly payments.
  • UK rental income: Tenancy agreements plus 6 months of bank statements showing deposits.
  • Investment income (ISAs, shares, dividends): Note — ISAs lose their tax-free wrapper status under Spanish tax law once you are a Spanish resident. Underlying income is still valid for income purposes.
  • Savings lump sum: Liquid savings in UK bank accounts — consulates typically require €28,800+ per single applicant (12-month equivalent).
⚠️

UK pension gap: Many British applicants are surprised to find their State Pension alone doesn't meet the threshold. This is one of the most common reasons UK NLV applications are delayed or rejected. Plan to combine income sources and start gathering documentation early.

Documents Required: ACRO, FCDO Apostille, and BLS Submission

All UK documents must be apostilled by the FCDO and translated by a sworn Spanish translator before submission to BLS International.

  • Completed visa application form (EX-01): Available from the BLS/Spanish consulate website.
  • Valid UK passport + certified photocopies + 2 recent passport photos
  • ACRO Standard Disclosure certificate: Apostilled by FCDO (£75) and translated into Spanish. NOT a DBS check.
  • Medical certificate: From a GMC-registered physician, apostilled, and translated by a sworn translator.
  • Income proof: DWP pension letter, private pension statement, bank statements (3–6 months), rental agreements — all apostilled and translated.
  • Private health insurance policy: Certificate covering all of Spain, no copayments, minimum 1-year duration.
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain: Rental contract or property deed.
  • Birth certificate (apostilled and translated): Required by some consulates — check with BLS for current requirements.
📌

FCDO apostille service: The FCDO Legalisation Office in Milton Keynes handles apostilles for UK documents. Cost: £75 per document. Processing: 4–8 weeks standard. Apply by post. Then send to a sworn Spanish translator (€50–€150 per document). Allow 8–12 weeks total from document gathering to complete dossier.

Applying Through BLS International: Step by Step

UK nationals do not apply directly at a Spanish consulate. BLS International submits your application on your behalf from London or Manchester.

BLS London

Covers: South England, Wales, Northern Ireland. Processing: 6–10 weeks. Higher demand — book early.

BLS Manchester

Covers: North England, Scotland, Midlands. Processing: 4–8 weeks. Often faster than London.

Consulate Fee

Approximately €80–€150, non-refundable. Paid at BLS appointment. BLS may charge an additional service fee.

The process in order:

  • Step 1: Apply for ACRO certificate immediately (£45 standard / £98 fast-track). This is on the critical path — start it first.
  • Step 2: Gather income documents, obtain medical certificate, purchase health insurance.
  • Step 3: Submit all UK documents to FCDO for apostille (4–8 weeks).
  • Step 4: Send apostilled documents to sworn Spanish translator (1–2 weeks).
  • Step 5: Book BLS appointment online. Attend with originals and complete dossier.
  • Step 6: Wait for consulate decision (4–10 weeks). Collect approved passport at BLS.
  • Step 7: Travel to Spain and register at Extranjería within 30 days for TIE residence card.

Tax Implications for British Retirees in Spain

Moving to Spain affects your tax status with both HMRC and Spain's Agencia Tributaria. Here's what British retirees need to understand.

  • File P85 with HMRC: Notify HMRC you are leaving the UK and becoming non-resident for tax purposes. This stops UK tax being deducted at source on income where the treaty applies.
  • Spanish tax residency: If you spend more than 183 days in Spain in a calendar year, you are a Spanish tax resident and must file a declaración de la renta (annual tax return) with Agencia Tributaria, declaring worldwide income.
  • UK-Spain double tax treaty: Prevents the same income being taxed in both countries. UK-source income (pension, rental income) taxed in the UK receives a credit against Spanish tax liability. Essential to use the treaty correctly.
  • ISAs: Spain does not recognise UK ISAs as tax-free savings vehicles. Investment income within ISAs is taxable in Spain once you are a Spanish resident.
  • UK pension income: State Pension and most private pensions are taxable in Spain as a resident. The UK-Spain treaty allocation between countries depends on the pension type — occupational vs. private.
  • UK rental income: Taxed in the UK under the treaty. You may also need to declare it in Spain, but a credit is given for UK tax already paid.
⚠️

Don't ignore HMRC or Agencia Tributaria: Many British expats in Spain underestimate their Spanish tax filing obligations. Once resident, Spain requires you to report worldwide income. Non-compliance can result in fines and complications at renewal. Seek advice from a cross-border tax specialist with UK-Spain experience.

Where British Retirees Settle in Spain

UK nationals form the largest single expat group in Spain. Here are the most popular areas.

Costa del Sol

The classic British heartland in Spain. Marbella, Nerja, Fuengirola, and Torremolinos have large established British communities with English-language services, shops, and social clubs.

Costa Blanca

Alicante, Torrevieja, Javea, and Altea are extremely popular with British retirees from the Midlands and North. Affordable, sunny, and English is widely spoken.

Mallorca & Balearics

Popular with wealthier British retirees — particularly from South England and London. Beautiful scenery, excellent healthcare, and strong property market.

Canary Islands

Tenerife and Gran Canaria attract British retirees seeking guaranteed winter sun. Year-round mild climate, large UK expat community, and lower cost of living.

Valencia City

Growing in popularity with younger British retirees. Vibrant city life, excellent healthcare, beaches nearby, and significantly cheaper than Barcelona.

Rural Andalusia

British retirees seeking authentic Spain and lower property costs are increasingly settling in the Axarquia region east of Malaga, the Ronda area, and rural Alicante province.

Frequently Asked Questions: Spain Retirement Visa for UK Citizens

Can British citizens still retire to Spain after Brexit?

Yes — but the process is now more involved than before January 2021. UK citizens are now third-country nationals and must apply for the Non-Lucrative Visa through BLS International in London or Manchester. You need income of approximately €2,400/month (around £2,050), private health insurance, and an ACRO criminal record certificate. Despite Brexit, Spain remains the top retirement destination for British nationals.

What is the ACRO certificate and why does Spain require it?

The ACRO Standard Disclosure is the UK criminal record check required for the Spanish NLV. It is different from a DBS check — Spanish consulates specifically require ACRO, not DBS. Apply at acro.police.uk. Standard cost: £45 (10–15 working days) or £98 fast-track (3–5 working days). The certificate must then be apostilled by the FCDO (£75) and translated into Spanish by a sworn translator.

Does my UK State Pension count as income for the Spain retirement visa?

Yes. UK State Pension counts as qualifying passive income. However, the full new State Pension (around £11,500/year) is approximately £958/month — below the €2,400/month (£2,050) threshold for 2026. Most UK applicants combine State Pension with private pension income, savings, investment income, or rental income from UK property to meet the threshold.

Which BLS centre do I apply through?

BLS International operates two visa application centres: London (South England, Wales, Northern Ireland) and Manchester (North England, Scotland, Midlands). You apply through the centre serving your region — you do not visit the Spanish consulate directly. Book appointments at the BLS International website. Consulate fee: approximately €80–€150, non-refundable.

How does the UK-Spain double tax treaty work for British retirees?

The UK-Spain double taxation treaty prevents the same income from being taxed in both countries. Once you are a Spanish tax resident (more than 183 days/year in Spain), you file a Spanish tax return declaring worldwide income. UK-source income already taxed in the UK is given relief under the treaty. File a P85 form with HMRC when you become non-resident. Note that ISAs lose their tax-free status under Spanish law. Always consult a cross-border tax specialist.
