What the Non-Lucrative Visa Requires

Spain’s immigration regulations require Non-Lucrative Visa applicants to hold health insurance (Royal Decree 1155/2024, art. 61.2(b)). The regulation itself is short, so the detail comes from the consulate that handles your application.

In practice, consulates ask for comprehensive private health insurance with an insurer authorised to operate in Spain, with no co-payments and no waiting periods, starting when you arrive and covering at least the first year. Each consulate publishes its own checklist, and that checklist is the one to follow.

ℹ️
Travel insurance and policies that only refund medical bills are not accepted as NLV health insurance. If you are unsure whether a policy will be accepted, check the certificate wording before you pay.

What Your Insurance Certificate Should Show

Consulates review the certificate, not the marketing brochure. Before you submit, check that it states:

✓The insurer is authorised to operate in Spain
✓Comprehensive cover: GP, specialists, hospital and emergencies
✓No co-payments (sin copagos)
✓No waiting periods (sin carencias)
✓Policy dates that match your planned arrival and the visa period
✓The name of every applicant covered

Some consulates also ask to see that the premium is paid for the full year, or that repatriation is included. Check your consulate’s list before you buy.

Price, Pre-Existing Conditions and Families

What affects the price

The premium depends mainly on each applicant’s age, where in Spain you will live and your medical history. A personalised quote is the only reliable figure.

Pre-existing conditions

Insurers assess your health questionnaire before offering cover. A condition may be accepted, excluded or affect the premium, and no applicant is guaranteed acceptance. Answer the questionnaire fully, and ask how any exclusion will appear on your certificate before you apply for the visa.

Couples and families

Every person on the application needs their own cover, including children. A single family policy can cover the whole household, as long as the certificate names each person.

Applying at 60, 65 or Older

Many Non-Lucrative applicants are retiring. Age affects the premium more than anything else, and older applicants are assessed through the same health questionnaire, so:

  • Ask for your quote as soon as your appointment is likely, so any medical questions don’t hold up the certificate.
  • Declare every condition, and ask how any exclusion will appear on the certificate.
  • Whether a plan accepts new applicants at your age is confirmed in your quote.
  • Plan to keep the policy for the long term: Non-Lucrative Visa holders are not automatically entitled to Spain’s public health system, and each renewal checks continuous cover.

Applying from your home country

Your consulate’s checklist is the one to follow, and home public cover doesn’t count. Country guides: United States · Canada · United Kingdom · Australia · New Zealand · moving as a family.

Keeping Your Cover at Renewal

When you renew your Non-Lucrative residence, you must show that your health insurance has continued without a break. Keep the policy active, renew it before it lapses and keep each year’s certificate. Our NLV renewal health insurance guide covers the renewal stage in detail.

How Buying Your Policy Works

What happens between asking for a quote and handing your certificate to the consulate:

  1. Get a quote. Tell our insurance partner who is applying, each person’s age and where you will live in Spain.
  2. Answer the health questionnaire. Each applicant declares their medical history. The insurer decides whether to accept, exclude or price any condition, so declare everything — an undisclosed condition can affect your cover later.
  3. Choose the start date — normally your planned arrival date, running for at least the first year.
  4. Accept the offer and pay. Payment terms are set by the policy rather than chosen freely: some visa policies must be paid annually upfront, and some consulates expect to see the year paid. Your quote states the terms that apply to you.
  5. Receive your certificate. Once your application is accepted and paid, the certificate is issued. It can show a future start date agreed with you, so you can have it for your appointment before your cover begins. It should be in Spanish and name each applicant, the policy dates, full cover in Spain, no co-payments and no waiting periods. It goes in with your visa application.

When to buy: once your appointment date is known. Because the certificate can show a future start date, you can have it for the appointment without paying for cover months before you travel.
