Moving to Spain from the UK: Complete 2026 Guide

Brexit changed the rules. British citizens wanting to live in Spain now need a visa — but the process is more straightforward than many people assume. Here is everything you need to know before you make the move.

Spain remains the most popular destination for British emigrants — the climate, the food, the people, and the cost of living all continue to pull people south. What has changed since 1 January 2021 is that British citizens are no longer EU nationals. That means the old right to simply move to Spain and register has gone. In its place is a formal visa process, a document checklist, and a set of administrative steps that need completing before and after you arrive. If you understand the system, though, it is very manageable — and thousands of British nationals successfully make the move every year.

What Brexit Means in Practice

Prior to the end of the Brexit transition period, British citizens exercised EU freedom of movement rights in Spain. They could move without a visa, register as EU residents, and access the Spanish healthcare system on broadly the same terms as Spanish nationals. All of that changed on 31 December 2020.

From 1 January 2021, British citizens wanting to live in Spain are treated as third-country nationals, the same legal category as Americans, Canadians, and Australians. You can visit for up to 90 days in any 180-day rolling period without a visa — useful for extended holidays and reconnaissance trips — but for actual residency, you need a Type D long-stay visa issued by a Spanish Consulate before you leave the UK. Arriving in Spain on a visitor entry and attempting to "sort it out on the ground" is not a legal route and leaves you in an irregular position.

Which Visa Route Is Right for You?

Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) — the most popular option for British retirees and those with passive income

The Non-Lucrative Visa is designed for people who can support themselves in Spain without needing to work for a Spanish employer or Spanish clients. It suits retirees drawing UK State Pension or private pension income, people with rental or investment returns, and anyone with substantial savings. For 2026, the income requirement is approximately €28,800 per year for the primary applicant, with an additional €7,200 per year required for each dependent joining you. You must hold comprehensive private health insurance in Spain, and you must not work for Spanish entities during the initial visa period. The NLV is issued for one year and renewable in two-year blocks up to five years, after which you can apply for long-term residency.

Digital Nomad Visa — for remote workers and UK freelancers

If you work remotely for UK employers or serve UK clients, the Digital Nomad Visa was created with you in mind. It allows you to live in Spain while your work and income remain anchored outside Spain. The visa is valid for one year initially and renewable for up to five years. It also provides access to the Beckham Law, a flat-rate income tax option at 24% on Spanish-source income, which is considerably lower than standard Spanish progressive rates and can represent a meaningful saving for people earning in GBP at higher income levels.

Student Visa — for those enrolling in Spanish courses or universities

British citizens enrolling at accredited Spanish institutions — universities, language schools, or professional training programmes — can apply for a Student Visa valid for the duration of their course. Limited part-time work is permitted; you cannot work full-time on a Student Visa.

Where Do British Citizens Apply?

All long-stay visa applications must be submitted in person at a Spanish consular office. The UK has three: the Consulate General of Spain in London (which covers England and Wales), the Spanish Consulate in Edinburgh (Scotland), and the Spanish Consulate in Manchester (the North of England). You must apply at the office that covers your region of residence — you cannot choose freely between them. Processing times vary but typically run between four and eight weeks, depending on application volumes and the time of year.

Documents British Applicants Need

The specific document list depends on which visa you are applying for, but most British applicants will need the following:

  • Valid UK passport — at minimum six months of remaining validity beyond your entry date
  • ACRO Police Certificate (UK criminal record check) — apostilled and accompanied by a certified Spanish translation; the ACRO check is the standard route for British applicants rather than the FBI check used by Americans
  • Medical certificate from a registered GP or physician confirming no contagious diseases — typically needs to be apostilled and translated
  • Proof of sufficient financial means — bank statements, pension award letters, investment account statements, or a combination showing income above the threshold
  • Comprehensive private health insurance covering Spain — no copayments or significant exclusions; the policy must meet minimum coverage standards
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain — signed rental contract, property deed, or similar
  • Completed national visa application form and recent passport photographs

Documents issued in the UK must bear the Hague Apostille, available through the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) via post or through a document apostille service. Certified Spanish translations of key documents (criminal record check, medical certificate) are required by most consulates. Build in four to six weeks for document preparation.

HMRC Tax Residency and the UK-Spain Double Tax Treaty

For most British nationals, the tax situation when moving to Spain is considerably simpler than for Americans — primarily because the UK taxes on residency, not citizenship. Once you establish tax residency in Spain (generally triggered by spending more than 183 days in Spain in a calendar year), you will ordinarily cease to be UK tax resident and become primarily liable to Spanish income tax.

Notifying HMRC

When you move, you should complete HMRC form P85 (or the online equivalent) to notify HMRC that you are leaving the UK and becoming non-resident. HMRC will assess your UK tax residency status under the Statutory Residence Test (SRT), which looks at the number of days spent in the UK and various qualifying factors. Most people who physically move to Spain and spend fewer than 16 days in the UK will be treated as non-resident in the UK from the date of departure.

UK State Pension and Private Pensions

You can continue to receive your UK State Pension in Spain — it is paid directly to your bank account and continues to be uprated annually (check current rules with the UK Government Pension Service, as Brexit affected uprating agreements). Under the UK-Spain Double Tax Convention, UK pension income is generally taxable in Spain once you are a Spanish tax resident. Some Government pensions (civil service, NHS, military, police, teaching) remain primarily taxable in the UK regardless of where you live — this is a specific carve-out in the treaty.

ISAs and UK Investments

ISA tax-free status applies only for UK tax purposes. Once you become a Spanish tax resident, the income and gains within a UK ISA are not sheltered from Spanish tax, even though your ISA retains its UK tax-advantaged status. Investment income — dividends, interest, capital gains — from UK stocks and shares becomes reportable in Spain under Modelo 100. Get cross-border tax advice on restructuring any investments before you move.

QROPS — Pension Transfers

Some British nationals consider transferring UK pension funds to a Qualifying Recognised Overseas Pension Scheme (QROPS) before moving abroad. QROPS can offer flexibility around drawdown and currency, but the rules and tax treatment are complex, fees can be high, and not all QROPS arrangements are beneficial. The HMRC Overseas Transfer Charge (25%) applies in some circumstances. Get specialist pension advice from an IFA authorised in both the UK and Spain before making any transfer decision.

Healthcare After You Move

Brexit ended the ability to use an EHIC or GHIC card for ongoing healthcare in Spain as a resident. The current Global Health Insurance Card (GHIC) covers temporary visitors, not residents. Once you are living in Spain as a legal resident:

  • You will need private health insurance as a condition of your NLV or DNV application, and this is what covers you during the initial residency period
  • Once you have held legal residency for a period and are contributing to the Spanish social security system (applicable mainly to DNV holders with Spanish freelance clients), you may be able to register with the state health system (sanidad)
  • Retirees on the NLV typically remain on private insurance for the duration of their non-lucrative status; switching to public healthcare requires meeting specific contribution conditions

After You Arrive: In-Country Steps

TIE Card (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero)

Within 30 days of arriving in Spain, you must apply for your TIE at the Oficina de Extranjeros or your local Comisaría de Policía. The TIE is your physical residency card. Bring your passport, visa, proof of address, application form, and the government fee payment. A fingerprint appointment is required.

Empadronamiento (Municipal Registration)

Register your address at your local Ayuntamiento (town hall) to get your padrón certificate. This is required for your TIE application, healthcare access, school enrolment for children, and countless administrative procedures. Bring your passport and rental contract or property deed.

NIE Number

Your NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) is your Spanish identification number, needed for every financial transaction — bank accounts, property purchases, utility contracts, and tax filings. In most cases your NIE is assigned during the visa and TIE process, but confirm the timeline with your consulate before departure.

Common Mistakes British Applicants Make

  • Believing the 90-day rule starts fresh each time you enter Spain — it does not; it rolls over a 180-day window
  • Applying at the wrong consulate for their UK region
  • Submitting documents without an apostille or without certified Spanish translations
  • Assuming UK ISA sheltering continues under Spanish tax law — it does not
  • Failing to notify HMRC via P85 before or shortly after departure
  • Purchasing health insurance with copayments, which many Spanish consulates reject
  • Not getting cross-border financial and pension advice before making transfers or drawing down assets

Frequently Asked Questions

Do British citizens still need a visa to live in Spain after Brexit?
Yes. Since 1 January 2021, British citizens are third-country nationals in Spain. You can visit for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa, but to live there long-term you must apply for a long-stay visa before leaving the UK. The Non-Lucrative Visa and Digital Nomad Visa are the most popular routes for British nationals.
What is the Non-Lucrative Visa income requirement for UK applicants in 2026?
For 2026, the Spanish Non-Lucrative Visa requires approximately €28,800 per year for the main applicant, plus around €7,200 per year for each dependent. Evidence typically includes UK bank statements, pension award letters, and investment account records covering the preceding 12 months.
What happens to my HMRC tax residency when I move to Spain?
Once you establish Spanish tax residency (generally over 183 days per year in Spain), you should notify HMRC using form P85. Under the UK-Spain double tax treaty, you will typically become primarily liable to Spanish income tax on worldwide income, with specific carve-outs for certain Government pensions and treaty-allocated income streams that remain taxable in the UK.
Can I receive my UK State Pension while living in Spain?
Yes. Your UK State Pension is paid directly to your bank account wherever you live. Once you are a Spanish tax resident, it is generally taxable in Spain under the bilateral treaty. Annual uprating applies — check the current rules with the UK Government Pension Service as Brexit affected some previous arrangements.
Where do British citizens apply for a Spanish visa?
You must apply at the Spanish consular office covering your UK region: the Consulate General in London covers England and Wales; the Spanish Consulate in Edinburgh covers Scotland; the Spanish Consulate in Manchester covers the North of England. You cannot freely choose between offices.
Can British remote workers use the Digital Nomad Visa to live in Spain?
Yes. The Digital Nomad Visa is available to British nationals working remotely for UK employers or clients. It lasts one year and is renewable for up to five years. It also provides access to the Beckham Law flat-rate tax option — potentially beneficial for higher earners working in GBP during their first period of Spanish residency.
