If you're considering moving to Spain but aren't sure which visa route fits your situation, this comparison breaks it down. Student visa, DNV, NLV, or tourist — each has different eligibility, work rights, costs, and pathways. Let's help you choose the right one.

Student Visa vs Digital Nomad Visa

Two very different visas for two different profiles. Here's when to choose each.

Choose Student Visa If:

You Want to Study

Your primary goal is genuine education — language immersion, university degree, professional course.

  • You're genuinely interested in learning Spanish or another skill
  • You don't have stable remote work income yet
  • You're younger (gap year, career change, retraining)
  • You want the language immersion and student community
  • Lower financial threshold (vs. DNV income requirement)
  • You want part-time work flexibility alongside study
Choose Digital Nomad Visa If:

You're Already Working Remotely

Your primary goal is to live and work in Spain — not study.

  • You have stable remote work with a foreign employer
  • You earn €2,000+ per month (meets DNV requirement)
  • Your focus is living in Spain, not studying
  • You want full-time work flexibility
  • You may study informally but it's not the visa requirement
  • You're building a long-term life and career in Spain
The Reality:

They're Often Sequential

Many people start on a student visa, then transition later.

  • Study for 1-2 years on student visa
  • Gain remote work income during / after studies
  • Switch to DNV once income threshold is met
  • Or pursue a work permit with a Spanish employer
  • Or continue as student if pursuing degree
  • Student visa is often the entry point to Spain
💡

Key insight: Student visa is not a "lesser" visa. It's often the strategic entry point for people who want to build a long-term life in Spain but don't yet have the remote work income for a DNV. Many DNV holders began on student visas.

Student Visa vs Non-Lucrative Visa

Both allow long-term residence in Spain, but serve very different life stages and financial situations.

Choose Student Visa If:

You Want to Study

Education is your primary goal or enabler.

  • You want to genuinely learn Spanish
  • You want part-time work rights (income flexibility)
  • You're younger or in a life transition
  • Lower financial bar to entry
  • You value the student community and experience
  • You want a stepping stone to work/career later
Choose Non-Lucrative Visa If:

You Don't Need to Work

You have passive income or savings covering living costs.

  • You're retired or semi-retired
  • You have passive income (pensions, investments, property)
  • You don't want to work or need work permission
  • You have sufficient capital (€16,000+ liquid + monthly income)
  • You want the simplicity of a non-work visa
  • You may study casually, but it's not required
The Difference:

Work Rights & Life Stage

The core difference is work permission and age.

  • Student: Assumes you're building your career
  • NLV: Assumes you've already built your wealth
  • Student: You can work part-time and earn
  • NLV: No work allowed — must live on passive income
  • Student: Shorter initial duration (per course)
  • NLV: Longer initial duration (1 year, renewable)
⚠️

Important: Retirees and people with passive income can still study on a student visa — there's no age limit. However, if you have sufficient passive income and don't need work rights, the NLV is often simpler from an administrative perspective.

Can You Study in Spain on a Tourist Visa?

Yes, but with significant limitations. Let's be clear on what works and what doesn't.

What You Can Do on Tourist Entry:

  • Enroll in short courses (intensive Spanish, workshops, certifications)
  • Take language classes at a school for under 90 days
  • Attend seminars, conferences, or training
  • Complete non-degree study within the 90-day Schengen window

What You Cannot Do on Tourist Entry:

  • Renew your tourist visa after 90 days — you must leave
  • Work, even part-time (no work rights on tourist entry)
  • Enroll in long-term university programs
  • Have a path to residency (tourist entry doesn't lead to permanent stay)
  • Stay beyond 90 days in the Schengen area
✈️

The Reality: Tourist entry with study is a "trial run" at best. If you want a proper, long-term study stay with the option to work part-time and build a residency pathway, the student visa is the legal route. After 90 days, you're required to leave the Schengen area unless you hold a valid residence visa.

Student Visa as Your Entry Point to Spain

Many people use the student visa strategically — not necessarily because they want to be a student forever, but because it's the cleanest entry point to Spain with built-in work flexibility.

Common Pathways After Student Visa

Student → Digital Nomad Visa

During or after your studies, you build remote work income. Once you hit €2,000+/month, you can transition to a DNV for full-time remote work rights.

Student → Work Permit (Spanish Employer)

After graduation, a Spanish company sponsors you for a work permit. Your student visa becomes the foundation for skilled worker pathways.

Student → Entrepreneur/Self-Employed Visa

Start a business in Spain while on student visa (part-time), then transition to self-employed visa once registered and generating income.

Student → Non-Lucrative Visa

If you develop passive income streams during your stay, you can transition to an NLV for a longer, renewable residency.

Student → Long-Term Residence (5+ Years)

After 5+ years of legal residence (student visa counts), you may be eligible for long-term residence or permanent residency regardless of visa type.

🎯

Strategic advantage: The student visa gives you legal entry, work flexibility (part-time), and the freedom to explore your options. It's one of the most flexible visas precisely because it doesn't lock you into a single pathway. Many successful long-term residents started this way.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I study in Spain on a tourist visa?

Yes, you can enroll in short courses under 90 days while on a tourist entry (Schengen visa-free for most nationalities). However, you cannot renew a tourist visa in Spain — you must leave after 90 days. You have no work rights and no path to residency. For a proper study stay of any length, the student visa is the legal, straightforward route.

Is the student visa easier to get than the Digital Nomad Visa?

Both have different requirements, so "easier" depends on your situation. The student visa requires proof of enrolment in a course and funds to cover living costs and tuition. The DNV requires proof of income (typically €2,000+/month) from a non-Spanish employer. If you already have the income, DNV might be faster. If you're genuinely wanting to study, student visa is simpler to qualify for.

Which Spain visa is cheapest?

The tourist/Schengen visa is free (for visa-exempt nationalities). The student, DNV, and NLV visas all cost around €100-150 for the visa processing itself. Total cost depends heavily on living expenses, course fees (for student visa), and health insurance (mandatory for NLV). The student visa may have additional course costs; the DNV and NLV have minimal added costs beyond visa fees.

Can I switch from a student visa to a Digital Nomad Visa?

Yes, it is possible if you meet the DNV's income requirement and can prove you are working remotely for a non-Spanish employer. You would apply for a new visa. Many people follow this exact pathway: start on a student visa, build remote work income over 1-2 years, then transition to DNV once the €2,000+/month requirement is met. It's a common and intentional progression.

Which visa lets me work in Spain?

The Digital Nomad Visa is specifically for remote work for non-Spanish employers. The student visa allows part-time work (typically 20-30 hours/week depending on region and academic agreement). The Non-Lucrative Visa does not permit any work. The traditional Work Permit allows employment with a Spanish company. The DNV is most flexible for full-time remote work.

Is there an age limit for the student visa?

No — there is no formal age limit for the student visa. You can study at any age: university students, career-changers, mature learners, retirees pursuing language schools, and professionals taking certifications can all hold student visas. Age is not a barrier. What matters is proof of enrollment in a legitimate course.

Should I use a student visa to live in Spain cheaply?

Using a student visa without genuine study intent is visa fraud and risks cancellation and future application problems. However, if you genuinely want to learn Spanish, the student visa offers legal residence at lower financial thresholds than the NLV. Many people combine genuine study with enjoying Spanish life.

Can I combine a student visa with the Non-Lucrative Visa?

You cannot hold both simultaneously. However, you can transition from one to the other — study in Spain on a student visa, then switch to the NLV if you meet the financial requirements and want to remain without continuing formal studies.

Is the student visa or DNV better for a digital nomad who also wants to study Spanish?

If your primary purpose is remote work and the DNV threshold, the DNV is correct. Many DNV holders also study Spanish — this is perfectly compatible with the DNV (you're not prohibited from studying; you're just not limited to studying). The student visa is only appropriate if studying is genuinely your primary activity.

What's the difference between a student visa and an au pair arrangement in Spain?

An au pair arrangement in Spain has its own specific visa category — not the standard student visa. Au pairs live with a Spanish family, help with childcare and household, and typically attend language classes. The specific terms (hours, accommodation, stipend) are regulated. Contact the Spanish consulate for au pair visa requirements.
