Rejections Happen—And They're Often Avoidable

A Digital Nomad Visa rejection is frustrating, but it doesn't mean your application was fundamentally ineligible. In most cases, rejections result from fixable issues with documentation, translation, or procedural compliance. Professional preparation dramatically reduces rejection risk.

ℹ️

Good news: You can reapply after a rejection. Most applicants who fix the underlying issue succeed on their second or third attempt. The key is understanding exactly what went wrong.

This guide covers the 8 most common rejection reasons, why they happen, and how to avoid them. Use this to prepare a stronger reapplication.

The Most Common Rejection Reasons

Understanding these reasons helps you identify what went wrong in your application and fix it before reapplying.

1

Incomplete or Incorrect Documents

The #1 reason for Digital Nomad Visa rejections. This includes missing translations, incorrect apostille format, expired certificates, forms filled out with errors, or documents submitted in the wrong language. Authorities are strict about documentation standards. Common culprits: driver's license not apostilled and translated, bank statements missing dates or signatures, employment contracts without all required information, or tax returns in the wrong language.

2

Insufficient Income Evidence

You must demonstrate consistent income of at least €2,850/month. Rejections occur when: income is below threshold, there's inconsistency month-to-month (gaps or drops), missing months of statements, income provided in different currency without proper conversion documentation, or the income source cannot be clearly identified. Authorities want 12 months of bank statements showing regular deposits from your employer or clients.

3

Non-Compliant Health Insurance

Your health insurance policy must meet specific Spanish requirements. Common rejections happen when: the policy includes a co-payment clause (not allowed), has waiting periods, isn't from a Spanish-authorized insurer, travel insurance was submitted instead of proper private coverage, or the policy doesn't explicitly cover Spain. The policy must be valid for the entire visa period and cover healthcare in Spain without user contribution.

4

Employer Letter Issues

The employment verification letter is critical. It gets rejected when: the letter doesn't explicitly confirm remote work arrangements, doesn't mention Spain or the ability to work from there, doesn't confirm you've worked there for 3+ months, lacks specific details about your role, or the employer appears unfamiliar with visa requirements (vague or generic language). The letter must be on company letterhead, signed by an authorized officer, and include specific details about your position and remote status.

5

Criminal Record Certificate Problems

You must provide clean criminal record certificates from all countries where you've lived in the past 5 years. Rejections occur when: certificates are missing from any country where you resided, they're not apostilled, they're not officially translated, they're expired (usually 3-6 month validity), or the wrong type of certificate is submitted. Authorities conduct thorough background checks and need properly authenticated documents from your home country and any other residences.

6

Exceeding the 20% Spanish Income Rule

The Digital Nomad Visa requires that no more than 20% of your income comes from Spanish sources. If your application documents show excessive income from Spain-based clients or employers, you'll be rejected. This rule prevents the visa from becoming a substitute for regular work authorization in Spain. Be clear about your client base and ensure the majority of your work is international, not Spain-based.

7

Not Demonstrating Genuine Remote Work

You must prove your work can genuinely be performed remotely. Rejections happen when: your job role appears location-dependent (chef, construction worker, etc.), you can't demonstrate flexibility in work location, your employer letter is vague about remote capabilities, or your work arrangement seems informal. Provide clear evidence that your role is inherently remote and doesn't require physical presence in any specific location.

8

Previous Visa Violations or Irregular Status

If you have a history of visa overstays, previous visa refusals, irregular immigration status, or other compliance issues, you'll likely be rejected. Spanish immigration authorities conduct thorough background checks. Resolve any previous immigration issues before reapplying. This may require consulting with an immigration attorney if you've had prior compliance problems.

What to Do If Your DNV Has Been Rejected

A rejection doesn't mean the end of your Spain plans. Most applicants successfully reapply when they understand and fix the underlying issue.

1

Review Your Rejection Notice Carefully

Read the official rejection letter from the Spanish authorities. It should specify why you were denied. Common phrases indicate the issue: "incomplete documentation," "insufficient income," "non-compliant insurance," etc. The more specific the reason, the easier it is to fix. Keep this letter—you'll reference it when reapplying.

2

Diagnose the Specific Issue

Match your rejection reason against the 8 common reasons above. Identify exactly what went wrong: Was it a translation issue? Missing documents? Insurance not compliant? This clarity is essential. If the rejection notice is vague, you may need to contact the Spanish consulate for clarification or seek professional guidance.

3

Can You Reapply?

Yes—in most cases you can reapply immediately. The exception is if you've violated Spanish visa terms or immigration law. If your rejection is due to documentation issues, income verification, or insurance compliance, you can fix these and submit a new application. There's no official waiting period, though some consulates prefer you wait 1-3 months to show you've resolved the issue.

4

Consider Your Options: Appeal or Reapply

Reapply (simpler): Fix the issue and submit a new application with corrected documents. This is the fastest and most successful route for most applicants.

Appeal: You can file a "recurso de reposición" (administrative review) or "recurso contencioso-administrativo" (court appeal) within 30 days of rejection. These are complex legal processes that usually require professional legal help. Most applicants find reapplying with fixes is simpler and faster.

5

Fix the Specific Issue and Gather New Documents

Based on your diagnosis, take action: Get proper apostilles and translations, obtain additional bank statements showing consistent income, switch to compliant health insurance, get an updated employer letter addressing the issue, obtain missing criminal record certificates, or clarify your income sources and remote work arrangement. Document everything.

6

Get Professional Help for Your Second Attempt

After a rejection, professional support dramatically increases success. Have documents reviewed by specialists before resubmission. Ensure everything meets Spanish standards. A small investment in professional preparation prevents another rejection and the associated delays and costs.

⚠️

Important: Appeals are complex legal matters. Unless you have strong legal grounds to contest the decision, reapplying with corrections is almost always faster and more successful. Consulate decisions are rarely overturned on appeal.

How to Avoid Rejection in the First Place

Prevention is always better than reapplying. Here's what professionals do to ensure approval:

  • Use a professional service: Specialists understand exactly what authorities expect. They catch issues others miss.
  • Have documents reviewed before submission: Don't submit anything without professional review. A €50 review prevents a rejection costing you months.
  • Use sworn translators: Not all translations are equal. Use translators certified for legal documents. Spanish authorities reject unofficial translations.
  • Get apostilles early: Apostilles can take weeks. Start this process immediately—don't wait until the last minute.
  • Confirm insurance compliance in advance: Contact your insurance provider directly. Get written confirmation that your policy meets Spanish DNV requirements.
  • Provide consistent income documentation: 12 months of clean, consistent bank statements. No gaps, no surprises.
  • Clarify remote work clearly: Your employer letter must be unambiguous about your remote arrangement and ability to work from Spain.
  • Anticipate questions: Cover all bases. If something might be questioned, explain it proactively.

Frequently Asked Questions About DNV Rejections

How long do I have to wait after rejection before reapplying?

There's no official waiting period. You can technically reapply immediately after receiving a rejection. However, some consulates prefer you wait 1-3 months to demonstrate you've resolved the issue. More importantly, take time to identify and fix the problem, gather corrected documents, and prepare a stronger application.

Can I appeal a Digital Nomad Visa rejection?

Yes, you have two appeal options: (1) Recurso de Reposición—an administrative review filed within 30 days, and (2) Recurso Contencioso-Administrativo—a court appeal. Both are complex legal processes requiring professional legal help. Appeals rarely overturn rejections. Reapplying with corrections is almost always faster and more likely to succeed.

What's the most common reason for DNV rejection?

Incomplete or incorrect documentation is the #1 reason. This includes missing translations, incorrect apostille format, expired certificates, or forms filled out incorrectly. Spanish authorities are strict about documentation standards. Proper preparation prevents this issue entirely.

How long does a reapplication take after rejection?

A reapplication takes the same amount of time as an initial application: typically 3-6 months. There's no expedited process for reapplications. The timeline is the same whether you're applying for the first time or the third time.

What if I was rejected for exceeding the 20% Spanish income rule?

You must diversify your client base or reduce Spanish income to below 20% before reapplying. If your work can't be reorganized to meet this requirement, the Digital Nomad Visa may not be the right option. Consider other visa categories like the Self-Employed visa if you want to work primarily for Spanish clients.

Can I get my application fee back if I'm rejected?

No, consulate fees are non-refundable regardless of the outcome. However, the fees are relatively modest (typically €100-300 depending on the consulate). Professional help (€1,899) prevents rejections and multiple fee payments.

Do I need legal representation for a reapplication?

Not necessarily. If you clearly understand what went wrong and can fix it (better translations, additional income documentation, insurance switch), you may reapply independently. However, professional support increases success significantly and prevents another rejection.

What if my rejection was due to health insurance?

Switch to compliant insurance and get written confirmation from the provider that your policy meets Spanish DNV requirements. Include this confirmation letter with your reapplication. Make sure the new policy is in effect before you resubmit.

Can I reapply through a different consulate if rejected?

You typically apply through the consulate serving your country of residence. However, if you change residence, you can apply through a different consulate. Standards are consistent across Spanish consulates, so a different consulate won't change requirements—only the specific issue preventing approval.

How many times can I reapply for the Digital Nomad Visa?

There's no official limit on reapplications. However, each rejection should address a specific issue. If you keep getting rejected for the same reason, you're missing something fundamental. After 2-3 rejections, consult a professional immigration attorney to identify deeper issues.

What is the most common reason the Digital Nomad Visa gets rejected?

Insufficient or incorrectly documented income is the leading cause. This includes: income just at or below the threshold, inconsistent documentation between stated income and bank statements, self-employed applicants without adequate client contract evidence, or freelancers unable to demonstrate the 20% Spanish client restriction is met.

Can I appeal a Digital Nomad Visa rejection?

Yes. You can file an administrative appeal (recurso de reposición or recurso de alzada) against a consulate rejection. The appeal must address the specific grounds cited in the refusal letter. Alternatively, correcting the issues and reapplying is often more practical and faster than appealing.

How soon can I reapply after a DNV rejection?

There is no mandatory waiting period before reapplying. However, reapplying immediately without addressing the reasons for rejection will result in another refusal. Take time to understand the refusal grounds, correct the specific issues, and then submit a stronger application.

Does a rejected DNV affect future visa applications?

A single rejection on good-faith grounds, properly addressed, does not prevent future applications. Spanish immigration authorities review each application on its merits. However, multiple rejections or applications based on false documentation can affect future assessments. Always address rejection grounds honestly.

Should I use a specialist after a DNV rejection?

Using a visa specialist after a rejection is strongly advisable. They can identify the specific issue, advise whether to appeal or reapply, ensure the documentation package fully addresses the refusal reasons, and help avoid repeating the same mistakes.
