Spain vs Italy for Expats: Which Country Should You Choose?

Spain and Italy are two of Europe's most popular expat destinations — Mediterranean lifestyle, excellent food, great weather, and rich culture. But they differ significantly in practical ways that matter to expats. Here's a direct comparison to help you decide.

Visas and Residency

Spain: the Non-Lucrative Visa is well-established, with clear (if demanding) income requirements and a mature process. The Digital Nomad Visa was introduced in 2023. Processing times vary by consulate but are generally reasonably predictable.

Italy: Italy has the Elective Residency Visa (similar to Spain's NLV), the Flat Tax Regime attracting high-net-worth individuals, and more recently a Digital Nomad Visa for non-EU citizens. Italian bureaucracy is notoriously complex and the residency application process is generally considered harder to navigate than Spain's.

Cost of Living

Both countries have significant internal variation. Southern Spain and Southern Italy (Puglia, Calabria) offer very low costs; Rome, Milan, Barcelona, and Madrid are considerably more expensive.

In comparable areas, costs are broadly similar. Italy's food culture means eating out can be extraordinary value in the south. Spain's rental market in mid-sized cities (Seville, Valencia, Murcia) is often slightly cheaper than Italian equivalents.

Healthcare

Both countries have strong public healthcare systems, consistently rated among Europe's best. Both have good private healthcare sectors. The key difference for expats is access: in Spain, NLV holders need private insurance; in Italy, some visa types can access public healthcare. Bureaucratic processes for healthcare registration are complex in both countries.

Bureaucracy

This is a notable difference. Spanish bureaucracy is complex but has improved in recent years. Italy's bureaucratic processes are widely regarded as more opaque, slower, and harder to navigate — even by European standards. For expats without Italian language skills, Spain's processes are generally considered more manageable.

Language and Integration

Spanish (Castilian) is a global language with over 500 million speakers — learning it opens doors far beyond Spain. Italian, while beautiful, is less widely useful for international communication. Both languages require genuine effort to achieve fluency. In expat-heavy areas of both countries, you can get by with English — but integration into local life requires the local language.
