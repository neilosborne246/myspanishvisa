Spain Digital Nomad Visa from Chicago: Complete Consulate Guide for Midwest Applicants

Everything Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, Minnesota, and 8 other Midwest states need to know about applying for Spain's Digital Nomad Visa through the Chicago consulate — appointments, documents, income requirements, and timelines.

Updated April 2026 Chicago Consulate: 180 N Michigan Ave Processing: 4–10 weeks after submission 13 Midwest states covered

The Chicago Consulate and Its Midwest Jurisdiction

If you live in Illinois or any of the twelve other Midwest and Great Plains states covered by the Chicago consulate, the Consulate General of Spain in Chicago is where you will apply for Spain's Digital Nomad Visa. With jurisdiction over 13 states — the largest geographic footprint of any Spanish consulate in the US — the Chicago consulate is a major hub for Spain visa applicants across the American heartland.

States Covered by the Chicago Consulate

Illinois Indiana Iowa Kansas Kentucky Michigan Minnesota Missouri Nebraska North Dakota Ohio South Dakota Wisconsin

You must apply at the consulate covering your state of legal residence. Residents of states not listed here should check which consulate serves their state. Michigan applicants use Chicago, not the New York or Washington DC consulate.

The consulate is located at 180 N Michigan Avenue, Suite 1500, Chicago, IL 60601, in the heart of the Chicago Loop. The building is steps from Millennium Park and accessible by multiple CTA lines (Red/Blue line at Washington; Brown/Orange/Green/Purple at Randolph/Wabash). Street parking is limited; the Millennium Park garage and several paid lots are nearby.

Appointment Booking at the Chicago Consulate

All Digital Nomad Visa appointments are booked through the consulate's online portal at cgespanachicago.org. There is no phone booking. Navigate to the Visa Services section, select National Visa (long-stay), and choose the Digital Nomad category.

The FBI Background Check: Getting It Right

US applicants must obtain a federal FBI Identity History Summary — not any state-level police check. An Illinois State Police background check, while useful for domestic purposes, is not accepted for the Spain Digital Nomad Visa.

The FBI Check Process

  1. Choose a channeler (recommended): FBI-approved channelers process your fingerprints electronically and return results within 1–3 business days. Find the current list at fbi.gov. Cost is typically $50–$75 including the FBI fee. Alternatively, submit directly to the FBI (allows 8–14 weeks).
  2. US Department of State Apostille: Mail your original FBI result to the State Department's Authentications Office with the apostille application form and fee. Standard processing: 6–8 weeks. Expedited options are available.
  3. Sworn Spanish Translation: The apostilled document must then be translated by a certified sworn translator.
Start the FBI Check on Day One

The FBI check with apostille is the document with the longest lead time in the entire application — typically 8–12 weeks from start to finish. Start it the same day you book your consulate appointment. If you use a channeler and expedited apostille processing, you can compress this to 4–6 weeks, but plan conservatively.

Health Insurance: What Midwest Applicants Need to Know

Spain requires DNV applicants to hold health insurance with no copayments, valid in Spain for the full stay. Most US domestic plans — including Blue Cross Blue Shield, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, and similar plans common among Midwest employees — do not meet Spain's requirements because they include deductibles, co-pays, or do not provide coverage outside the US.

You will need a dedicated international or Spanish expat health insurance plan. See our full DNV health insurance guide for a comparison of options and what to look for in a compliant policy.

Policies With Copayments Are Not Accepted

Spain's requirement for “no copayments” (copago cero) means the insurer covers 100% — no excess, no deductible, no per-visit charge. This is stricter than most US-based international travel or health insurance products. Always request a certificate explicitly confirming “no copayments” or “sin copago” from your insurer before submitting your application.

The Employer Letter for Midwest Remote Workers

Your employer letter is a critical document. For employees at Midwest-based corporations or remote workers for companies headquartered in the US:

  • Print on official company letterhead with registered business address
  • Include your full name, job title, and gross salary (monthly and annual)
  • Explicitly state the work arrangement is fully remote and may be performed from Spain
  • State that the company is incorporated outside Spain and does not have operations in Spain
  • Include your employment start date and contract type
  • Signed by HR director or company officer with full name and title
  • Include company EIN and physical address

Freelancers should substitute client contracts and a professional accountant letter. See our DNV documents guide for templates.

Step-by-Step Process for Chicago Consulate Applicants

  • 1

    Book Your Appointment at cgespanachicago.org

    Book under National Visa › Digital Nomad. Chicago wait times of 3–8 weeks mean you may get an appointment sooner than expected. Book immediately — do not wait until documents are ready.

  • 2

    Start FBI Background Check (Same Day as Booking)

    Use an FBI-approved channeler for fastest turnaround. Immediately apply for the US Dept of State apostille once you receive results. This step takes the longest — 4–12 weeks depending on method chosen.

  • 3

    Purchase Spain-Compliant Health Insurance

    Research providers and purchase a policy with no copayments valid in Spain for at least 12 months. Obtain a certificate explicitly confirming “no copayments.”

  • 4

    Gather Income and Employment Documents

    Collect bank statements (6 months recommended), pay stubs, W-2/1099, employer letter, and any supporting business registration documents if self-employed.

  • 5

    Commission Sworn Spanish Translations

    All English documents require certified sworn translations. Allow 5–10 business days. My Spanish Visa can connect you with vetted translators experienced with Spain visa applications.

  • 6

    Complete EX-01 Application Form

    Download from the Spanish government portal, complete, print, and sign. Bring two copies plus all supporting documents with originals and two sets of photocopies to your appointment.

  • 7

    Attend Your Appointment at 180 N Michigan Ave

    Arrive 10 minutes early. The building security requires photo ID. Bring all documents plus fee (check current amount). Receive your tracking reference number upon submission.

  • 8

    Wait for Processing (4–10 Weeks)

    The Chicago consulate's processing timeline after submission is broadly similar to other US consulates at 4–10 weeks. Monitor via your reference number. Do not book non-refundable travel until approval is confirmed.

  • 9

    Collect Visa and Travel to Spain

    Return to the consulate to collect your passport with the visa affixed. Apply for your TIE card at the Oficina de Extranjería within 30 days of arriving in Spain.

After Approval: Your First Month in Spain

Once you arrive in Spain on your Digital Nomad Visa, several administrative tasks must be completed promptly:

  • TIE Card: Apply at the Oficina de Extranjería within 30 days. This is your physical residency card.
  • Empadronamiento: Register your address at the local Ayuntamiento. Required for most official processes.
  • Spanish Bank Account: Open a local account for rent payments and utilities. Bring your passport, NIE/TIE, and empadronamiento certificate.
  • Beckham Law Election: If you want Spain's special 24% flat income tax rate, apply within 6 months of Social Security registration. See our DNV tax guide for details.
  • Health Registration: If you plan to supplement private insurance with public health access after the initial period, understand the requirements for accessing Spain's public healthcare system.

Beckham Law for Midwest Digital Nomads

Spain's Beckham Law (Special Tax Regime for Relocated Workers) is available to all Digital Nomad Visa holders who become tax resident in Spain, including those who applied through the Chicago consulate. It offers a flat 24% income tax rate on Spanish-sourced income up to €600,000/year for up to 6 years of Spanish tax residency.

For US citizens applying from Midwest states, the same cross-border tax complexity applies as for all Americans: the IRS taxes US citizens on worldwide income regardless of residence. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion and Foreign Tax Credit mechanisms can offset double taxation, but the interaction with Spain's Beckham Law requires specialist advice. My Spanish Visa can refer you to qualified cross-border tax advisers.

Non-US Nationals Living in Midwest States

The Chicago consulate serves not only US citizens but all foreign nationals legally residing in its 13-state jurisdiction. Key notes:

  • Canadian nationals in the Midwest: If you are Canadian and reside in, say, Illinois or Michigan, apply at the Chicago consulate. You will need an RCMP criminal record check in addition to (or instead of) a US FBI check. See our Canada DNV guide for RCMP check details.
  • UK nationals in the Midwest: Post-Brexit, UK nationals are treated as non-EU for Spanish visa purposes. You'll need an ACRO Police Certificate from the UK plus an FBI check if you've been resident in the US long enough to have a US record to certify.
  • Green Card holders: Apply at the Chicago consulate if you reside in the 13-state jurisdiction, regardless of your nationality.
  • Recent movers: Your legal state of residence at the time of application determines your consulate. Ensure your state ID is updated to reflect a current Midwest address before booking.

Common Reasons for DNV Rejection: What Midwest Applicants Get Wrong

The most frequent reasons Spain Digital Nomad Visa applications are refused at the Chicago consulate (and US consulates generally) include the following. Review this list carefully before submitting your application:

Insufficient income documentation Provide 6 months of bank statements, not just 3; include employer letter, pay stubs, AND W-2/1099 — layered evidence is stronger than a single document
Health insurance with copayments or deductibles Explicitly confirm “no copayments” (sin copago) with the insurer before purchasing; get a written certificate stating this clearly
Incorrect apostille on FBI check FBI check must be apostilled by the US Department of State, not an Illinois or other state authority; double-check the issuing body on the apostille

DNV Renewal: What Happens After Year One

The Spain Digital Nomad Visa is initially valid for 1 year. Before expiry, you can renew for a residence permit of up to 2 years at a time from within Spain, up to a maximum total duration of 5 years. After 5 continuous years of legal residence, long-term EU residency becomes possible; after 10 years, Spanish naturalisation.

Renewal does not require returning to the Chicago consulate — it is handled at the Oficina de Extranjería in Spain. You will need updated income evidence, renewed health insurance, and updated criminal background check documents. See our DNV renewal guide for the full renewal process and checklist.

Renewal Timeline

Start your renewal process 2–3 months before your current permit expires. The renewal appointment system in Spain can have significant queues, and updated documents (especially criminal record checks with apostilles) take time to obtain.

Post-Submission: What to Expect from the Chicago Consulate

Once you have submitted your application, here is what typically happens:

  1. Processing period: 4–10 weeks. The consulate reviews your application against Spain’s DNV requirements and may contact you if additional documents are needed.
  2. Status checks: You can check the status of your application using the reference number provided at submission via the consulate’s online tracking system or by calling the consulate.
  3. Decision notification: The consulate will notify you when a decision has been made. If approved, you will be asked to return to collect your passport with the visa affixed. If a deficiency is identified, you may be asked to submit additional documents — respond promptly.
  4. Visa collection: Return in person to 180 N Michigan Ave to collect your passport. Bring your appointment confirmation and ID.
  5. Visa validity: Your visa is typically valid for 1 year from the date of issue. You must enter Spain and start the TIE process within that window.
  6. After arrival in Spain: Apply for your TIE card at the Oficina de Extranjería within 30 days. Register your address (empadronamiento) at the Ayuntamiento. Open a Spanish bank account. If electing Beckham Law, apply within 6 months of Social Security registration.

Frequently Overlooked Aspects of the DNV Application

In our experience working with Midwest applicants, these are the elements most often underestimated or overlooked:

  • The apostille on the FBI check takes longer than most applicants expect. Between the channeler, the US Department of State, and transit time, allow 4–8 weeks minimum even with expedited processing. Start this on day one.
  • Health insurance shopping takes more time than it should. Finding a policy that explicitly certifies “no copayments” in a format acceptable to the consulate is not always straightforward. Allow at least 1–2 weeks for research, purchase, and certificate issuance.
  • Sworn translation timelines vary significantly. Some translators specialising in Spain visa documents can turn around translations in 2–3 business days; others quote 2–3 weeks. Commission translations early and confirm your translator’s sworn (jurado) status before engaging them.
  • Bank statement presentation matters. Statements should be official documents from your bank — not screenshots from a banking app. If your bank provides downloadable PDFs with your name, account number, and bank letterhead, use those. Paper statements are also acceptable.
  • The medical certificate is easy to overlook. It is a simple document but needs a licensed physician’s signature and a sworn Spanish translation. Book your GP appointment early in the process.

Time Zone and Remote Work Schedule: Chicago to Spain

The practical reality of working remotely from Spain for US Midwest-based employers is one of the first questions Chicago applicants ask. Here is what to expect:

  • Standard time (winter): Spain is CET (UTC+1); Chicago is CST (UTC-6). Difference: 7 hours. A 9am Chicago call is 4pm Madrid.
  • Summer (daylight saving): Spain is CEST (UTC+2); Chicago is CDT (UTC-5). Difference: 7 hours year-round during spring/summer.
  • Practical schedule: A common approach is to start the Spanish workday around noon or 1pm Madrid time, which corresponds to 5–6am Chicago. By 8–9pm Madrid time, the full Chicago working day is underway. This is a shifted schedule but manageable for most remote roles.
  • Async advantage: Roles at companies with strong async cultures (project management, writing, development, design) often work seamlessly from Spain without any schedule change.
  • Client calls: End-of-day Chicago calls (4–6pm CST) are 11pm–1am Madrid — generally not sustainable long-term. Discuss expectations with clients and employers before committing.

Settling In Spain: First Two Weeks Priorities for Midwest Movers

Once you arrive in Spain on your Digital Nomad Visa, these are the highest-priority administrative tasks for your first two weeks:

  1. Secure your accommodation address. You need a Spanish address for empadronamiento and the TIE application. If you booked a short-term rental, confirm the exact address before arrival.
  2. Register empadronamiento at the Ayuntamiento. Book an appointment with your local town hall (Ayuntamiento) and bring your passport, visa, and proof of address (rental contract or host letter). You will receive your empadronamiento certificate — a key document for many subsequent processes.
  3. Apply for your TIE card. Book an appointment at the Oficina de Extranjería (immigration office) with your passport, visa, empadronamiento certificate, passport photos, and completed EX-23 application form. You will also pay a small fee (tasas). TIE cards are typically ready in 4–6 weeks.
  4. Open a Spanish bank account. Visit a local branch of Santander, BBVA, CaixaBank, or Sabadell. Bring your passport and NIE/TIE (or appointment receipt) and empadronamiento. Some banks can open accounts without a TIE using just your passport and NIE number.
  5. Get a Spanish SIM card. Spanish carriers include Movistar, Vodafone, Orange, and budget options like DIGI, Simyo, and Yoigo. A SIM with data is essential from day one for navigation and communication.

Frequently Missed Details That Make the Difference

Beyond the major document requirements, these smaller details often determine whether an application is smooth or needs supplementation:

  • Document currency: Bank statements older than 3 months at the time of your appointment may be questioned. Update them if your appointment date moved significantly from when you initially gathered documents.
  • Health insurance territorial coverage: Your policy certificate must explicitly state coverage “in Spain” or “in the European Union” or “worldwide including Spain.” Generic global coverage language without Spain specified has caused problems at some consulates.
  • EX-01 form completion: The form has sections about your intended address in Spain and expected duration of stay. “TBD” or blank entries raise questions; provide as much information as possible, even if approximate.
  • Photocopies in order: Organise your photocopies in the same order as your originals. A disorganised application bundle slows the consulate officer and can cause items to be missed.
  • Cover letter (optional but recommended): A brief cover letter introducing yourself, your employer/clients, your income, and your intended move plan shows preparedness and organisation. Not required but can help frame your application positively.

Working Remotely from Spain: Essential Practical Guide

Moving to Spain as a digital nomad involves more than obtaining the right visa. Here are the practical working realities that affect your daily life and productivity:

Internet and Connectivity in Spain

Spain has excellent broadband infrastructure in major cities and towns. Fibre optic (fibra óptica) is widely available from providers including Movistar (Telefónica), Vodafone, Orange, and DIGI. Typical fibre speeds in apartments are 300Mbps–1Gbps. Rural areas have more limited options but 4G/5G mobile coverage is generally good across the country.

  • Home broadband: Most rental agreements include or readily allow broadband installation. Average residential fibre: €30–€55/month including landline. DIGI offers budget options from around €20/month.
  • Coworking spaces: Every major Spanish city has a growing network of coworking spaces. Madrid alone has dozens, ranging from budget hotdesks (€150–€200/month) to premium private offices (€400–€800/month).
  • Cafes: Many Spanish cafes offer wifi, though the culture around long cafe working sessions is less established than in some other countries. Coworking spaces are more reliable for full-day work.

Banking for Remote Workers in Spain

Managing finances as a foreign resident in Spain involves several layers:

  • Spanish bank account: Essential for rent, utilities, and Spanish transactions. Open as soon as you have your NIE/TIE. CaixaBank, Santander, BBVA, and Sabadell are the most accessible. Some require a full TIE card; others accept an appointment receipt and passport.
  • International transfers: For receiving income in foreign currency (USD, GBP, CAD), services like Wise (formerly TransferWise), Revolut, and XE offer significantly better exchange rates and lower fees than traditional banks.
  • Currency conversion timing: If your income is in foreign currency, consider the exchange rate when converting to euros for Spanish expenses. Setting a regular transfer schedule helps reduce volatility impact.
  • Modelo 720: If you have foreign assets worth more than €50,000, you must file a Modelo 720 declaration with the Spanish Tax Authority (Agencia Tributaria) by March 31 each year. Failure to declare carries significant penalties. Professional tax advice is essential for asset-rich applicants.

Healthcare Access in Spain

Your visa health insurance covers private healthcare from day one. Longer-term options:

  • Private health insurance (your visa policy): Covers private consultations and hospital treatment without waiting lists. Spain’s private healthcare is high quality and widely available. Private specialists in Madrid and Barcelona are generally excellent.
  • Public healthcare (Sistema Nacional de Salud): Once you register with Social Security (required if working as autónomo or for a Spanish employer), you gain access to Spain’s public health system. This is free at point of use but has longer waiting times for non-urgent care.
  • Private health supplements: Many Spanish residents hold a supplemental private health insurance (seguro médico) from providers like established private health insurers in Spain, or a leading private insurer — covering private GP visits, specialists, and diagnostics — at very affordable rates (€40–€80/month for basic coverage).

Education for Children

If you are bringing children, Spain has excellent education options:

  • International schools: Major Spanish cities have British, American, and bilingual international schools. Fees range from €5,000–€20,000+ per year depending on the school.
  • Spanish public schools: Free and high quality. Taught in Spanish (and often regional language). Children adapt quickly, especially under age 10.
  • Concertados (semi-private): State-subsidised private schools, common in Spain, offering a middle ground between fully private and state education.
