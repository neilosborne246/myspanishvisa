What You're Actually Paying For

Basic Plans (60–100/month)

Covers: Doctor visits, emergency care, hospital stays, some surgery. Excludes: Dental, vision, preventative care, physiotherapy. Best for: Young, healthy expats; visa compliance minimums.

Mid-Tier Plans (100–180/month)

Covers: Basic + partial dental, vision screening, some physiotherapy. Better copay structure. Best for: Most expats wanting solid coverage without overpaying.

Comprehensive Plans (180–300+/month)

Covers: Everything + routine dental, eye exams, full physiotherapy, mental health, cosmetic procedures. Best for: Older expats, those with ongoing medical needs, peace of mind.

Hidden Costs & What to Ask

Enrollment Fee

Usually waived for online applications. Check before enrolling. Can be 50–100 euros.

Annual Deductible

You may pay the first 300–600 euros of medical costs per year. Ask before signing. Some plans offer zero-deductible options (costs more monthly).

Copays

Important for visa applicants: If you are applying for an NLV, Digital Nomad Visa, or Student Visa, your policy must have zero copayments — no out-of-pocket costs at point of treatment. This is a non-negotiable consulate requirement; any plan with copays will be rejected. Sanitas and Caser both offer fully no-copay policies that satisfy this requirement. For general expat living once you have residency, standard plans with copays are available at lower monthly premiums (doctor visit €15–30, ER €50–150, tests €0–50) — but these are not acceptable for the initial visa application.

Pre-existing Condition Exclusions

Many plans exclude pre-existing conditions for 6–12 months. Ask specifically about your conditions.

How to Apply

Step 1: Get a Quote

Contact 247 Expat Insurance, or go directly to Sanitas or Caser. Get quotes (free). Confirm the plan has no waiting periods, no copayments, no exclusions, and full coverage — all required for NLV/DNV/student visa compliance.

Step 2: Verify Visa Compliance

Ask the insurer: "Does this plan meet [your visa type] insurance requirements?" Confirm: zero copayments, no waiting periods, repatriation included, and that they will issue a consulate-valid certificate. Get all confirmation in writing before paying.

Step 3: Pay in Advance

Sanitas allows you to pay up to 6 months in advance — a critical practical advantage. Pay the full policy term upfront before your consulate appointment. You will receive the justificante de pago (payment receipt proving the policy has been paid) and the insurer's consulate-valid certificate confirming your coverage details. These two documents are what you present at the consulate.

Step 4: Activate and Prepare Your Application

Coverage starts from the policy start date. Your medical card arrives by email within 24 hours. Include the justificante de pago and the consulate-valid certificate in the health insurance section of your visa application. Download the provider app (Sanitas and Caser both have English-language apps) and explore your doctor network.

FAQ

Can I switch insurers mid-contract?

Yes, but you may face penalties (1–3 months charges). Ask about exit terms before signing. Some plans allow guilt-free cancellation in the first 30 days.

Do expats get charged more?

Not specifically. Age and health history determine cost. Older expats (60+) pay more. As a younger expat, your costs are comparable to Spanish nationals.

Are mental health services covered?

Basic plans: usually not. Mid-tier: partial (5–10 sessions/year). Comprehensive: full coverage. Ask explicitly if mental health is important to you.

What if I have a serious pre-existing condition?

Declare it honestly. Insurers may exclude the condition temporarily (6–12 months) or charge higher premiums. Dishonesty voids your policy.
