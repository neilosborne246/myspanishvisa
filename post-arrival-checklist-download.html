Frequently Asked Questions

Questions about post-arrival steps in Spain

How soon after arriving in Spain must I apply for my TIE card?

You must apply for your TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) within 30 days of arriving in Spain. Missing this deadline can cause complications with your residency status. The post-arrival checklist includes the exact steps, documents required, and how to book your cita previa (appointment) at the Oficina de Extranjería.

What is the padrón and why do I need to register?

The padrón (empadronamiento) is the official municipal register of residents. Registering at your local town hall is a legal requirement for all residents in Spain and is needed to access many public services, renew your visa, apply for your digital certificate, and register children for school. You should register as soon as possible after arriving — you will need a rental contract or property deed as proof of address.

Can I open a Spanish bank account as a new resident?

Yes. New residents can open a bank account in Spain. You will typically need your passport, NIE number, proof of address (padrón certificate), and proof of income or employment status. Some banks allow non-resident accounts before your TIE is issued. The checklist covers the most expat-friendly Spanish banks and what to bring to your appointment.

What is the digital certificate (certificado digital) and do I need it?

The certificado digital is an electronic identity certificate that allows you to deal with Spanish government agencies online — including tax authorities (Agencia Tributaria), social security (Seguridad Social), and the Extranjería. It is extremely useful and saves hours of in-person visits. You can obtain it through the FNMT website after your TIE is issued. The checklist covers the application process step by step.

What happens if I miss any of the post-arrival deadlines?

Some post-arrival tasks have legal deadlines — most critically the TIE application (within 30 days) and, for DNV holders, the Beckham Law application (within 6 months of social security registration). Missing these can result in fines or loss of important tax benefits. The checklist clearly marks time-sensitive tasks with their deadlines so nothing is accidentally overlooked.
