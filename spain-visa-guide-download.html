Visa Snapshot

The three main Spain visa routes for non-EU nationals

Each visa is covered in full in the guide. Here's a quick overview.

NLV

Non-Lucrative Visa

Ideal for retirees and those with passive income

  • Requires approx. €2,400/month passive income
  • No working of any kind permitted
  • Applied for at Spanish consulate in home country
  • 1-year visa, renewable up to 5 years
  • Leads to long-term residency after 5 years
DNV

Digital Nomad Visa

Ideal for remote workers and freelancers

  • Requires approx. €2,762/month remote income
  • Work for foreign clients/employers permitted
  • Beckham Law flat tax rate available
  • 3-year visa, renewable for 2 more years
  • Leads to long-term residency after 5 years
STUDENT

Student Visa

Ideal for language students and university enrolees

  • Requires proof of enrolment + financial means
  • Limited part-time work rights (20 hrs/week)
  • Requires acceptance letter from Spanish institution
  • Valid for duration of study programme
  • Can lead to Job Seeker Visa after graduation

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions about choosing a Spain visa

What is the difference between the NLV and the Digital Nomad Visa?

The Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) is for people with passive income — retirees, investors, or those with rental income — who do not intend to work in Spain. The Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) is for people who work remotely for foreign clients or employers and want to continue working while living in Spain. The income evidence requirements differ significantly, and they are processed through different channels.

Can I work in Spain on a Non-Lucrative Visa?

No. The Non-Lucrative Visa explicitly prohibits any form of work in Spain — including remote work for foreign companies. If you intend to continue working remotely while living in Spain, you need the Digital Nomad Visa. Working on an NLV is a serious immigration violation that can result in deportation and a ban on re-entry.

How much money do I need to qualify for each Spain visa?

Income requirements differ by visa type. For the NLV, the typical requirement is around €2,400 per month (400% of IPREM). For the DNV, the minimum is 200% of IPREM, approximately €2,762 per month. For the Student Visa, you need to demonstrate sufficient funds to cover living costs for the duration of study. The Decision Guide includes a comparison table with current 2026 figures for all three visa types.

What if I am not sure which Spain visa is right for me?

That's exactly what this guide is for. It includes a step-by-step decision flowchart designed to guide you to the right visa based on your personal situation — your income type, whether you plan to work, and your long-term goals. If your situation is complex or unusual, we recommend booking a consultation with one of our visa specialists.

Does the guide cover visa renewal and the path to permanent residency?

Yes. Each visa section covers the initial application, renewal options, and the typical path to long-term residency and eventually Spanish citizenship. Both the NLV and DNV lead to a 5-year long-term residency permit after five consecutive years of legal residence in Spain, which in turn opens the path to Spanish citizenship after 10 years (or less for some nationalities).
