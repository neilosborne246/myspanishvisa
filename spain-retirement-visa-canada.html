The Spain Retirement Visa for Canadians: The Non-Lucrative Visa

The NLV is Spain's designated long-stay visa for non-EU nationals who want to live in Spain without working.

Canada and Spain don't have a working holiday agreement for retirees — the Non-Lucrative Visa is the correct route. It allows Canadian citizens to live in Spain full-time, receiving passive income from Canadian and international sources, without being permitted to work in Spain. It's renewable and leads to long-term residency and eventually Spanish citizenship.

Spain is increasingly popular with Canadians — particularly those from Ontario and British Columbia — who want to escape harsh winters without the high cost of US retirement destinations. Barcelona and the Costa del Sol both have growing Canadian communities, and Spanish healthcare consistently ranks among the world's best.

💡

Canada vs. Spain cost comparison: Retirees from Toronto and Vancouver often find Spain 30–40% cheaper for everyday expenses — groceries, dining, healthcare costs, and property. Combined with Spain's climate and quality of life, the financial case for retiring in Spain from Canada is compelling.

Eligibility for Canadian Citizens

The core requirements apply to all nationalities, with specific Canadian document requirements below.

  • Valid Canadian passport: At least 6 months' validity beyond intended stay. Two recent passport photographs.
  • Passive income: Approximately €2,400/month for a single applicant. CPP, OAS, private pension, RRIF withdrawals, and rental income all qualify.
  • RCMP criminal record check: Royal Canadian Mounted Police background check — not a provincial check. Must be apostilled through Global Affairs Canada and translated into Spanish.
  • Medical certificate: From a licensed Canadian physician, apostilled through Global Affairs Canada, translated into Spanish.
  • Private health insurance: A Spanish-compliant policy with no copayments. Provincial health insurance (OHIP, MSP, etc.) does NOT qualify — you will need private international health insurance. Annual cost: CAD $1,200–$3,500 depending on age.
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain: Signed rental contract, property deed, or formal host invitation.

Income Requirements for Canadian Applicants (2026)

What counts as income for Canadian NLV applicants, and how to document it correctly for the Spanish consulate.

2026 Income Thresholds

Single applicant: €2,400/month (approximately CAD $3,500/month at April 2026 rates)

Each dependent: ~€600/month extra

CAD/EUR rate for reference only. Exchange rates fluctuate — use current rate at time of application.

Acceptable income sources for Canadians:

  • Canada Pension Plan (CPP): Service Canada statement showing monthly benefit. CPP maxes out around CAD $1,300/month (2026) — typically needs supplementing.
  • Old Age Security (OAS): Service Canada statement. Maximum OAS approximately CAD $750/month. Combined CPP + OAS often approaches but doesn't reach the threshold alone.
  • Private/employer pension: Pension provider statement on official letterhead showing monthly payment.
  • RRIF withdrawals: Regular scheduled RRIF distributions count. Account statements showing distribution schedule required.
  • Rental income from Canadian property: Lease agreement plus 6 months of bank statements showing deposits.
  • Investment income (dividends, interest from TFSA, non-registered accounts): Brokerage/account statements showing consistent income.
  • Savings approach: Liquid lump sum — consulates typically require €28,800+ (12x monthly threshold) for a single applicant.
⚠️

All financial documents must be apostilled and translated: CPP/OAS letters from Service Canada, bank statements, pension provider letters — all require apostille through Global Affairs Canada and certified Spanish translation. Allow 6–10 weeks for this process.

Documents Required: RCMP Check, Global Affairs Apostille

Canada's apostille process runs through Global Affairs Canada. Here is the full document list for Canadian NLV applicants.

  • Completed visa application form (EX-01)
  • Valid Canadian passport + certified copies + 2 passport photos
  • RCMP criminal record check (Certified Criminal Record Check): Apostilled through Global Affairs Canada, translated into Spanish. Allow 3–8 weeks for RCMP processing, plus apostille time.
  • Medical certificate: From a licensed Canadian physician, apostilled, translated.
  • Income proof: CPP/OAS letters (Service Canada), private pension statements, RRIF statements, bank statements (3–6 months) — all apostilled and translated.
  • Private health insurance certificate: Covering all of Spain, comprehensive coverage, no copayments.
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain
📌

Global Affairs Canada apostille: Since Canada acceded to the Hague Apostille Convention, documents can now be apostilled through Global Affairs Canada (federal documents) or provincial authorities (provincial documents like birth certificates). The RCMP criminal record check is a federal document — apostille through Global Affairs Canada. Processing varies by province; allow 4–8 weeks.

Applying Through Spanish Consulates in Canada

Apply at the Spanish consulate covering your Canadian province of residence. Appointments are required and must be booked online in advance.

Toronto Consulate

Covers: Ontario, and parts of Atlantic Canada. Busiest Canadian consulate for NLV applications. Book well in advance.

Montreal Consulate

Covers: Quebec, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, PEI, Newfoundland. French and English service available.

Vancouver Consulate

Covers: British Columbia. Strong demand from BC retirees. Growing number of Canadian applications from this office.

Calgary Consulate

Covers: Alberta and Prairie provinces. Verify exact provincial jurisdiction before booking.

Step-by-step application process:

  • Step 1: Apply for RCMP criminal record check — start immediately (3–8 weeks processing).
  • Step 2: Gather income documents (CPP/OAS letters, pension statements, bank statements).
  • Step 3: Get documents apostilled through Global Affairs Canada (federal) or provincial authorities (provincial documents).
  • Step 4: Translate all apostilled documents into Spanish via a sworn translator.
  • Step 5: Book consulate appointment online. Attend with complete dossier.
  • Step 6: Pay visa fee (~€80–€150). Await decision (8–16 weeks). Collect approved visa sticker.
  • Step 7: Enter Spain within the visa's validity window. Register at Extranjería within 30 days for NIE/TIE.

Canadian Tax Implications of Retiring in Spain

Moving from Canada to Spain has significant tax consequences on both sides. Plan this carefully before you go.

  • Departure from Canada: When you cease to be a Canadian resident, you must file a departure tax return (T1 with Form T1161 — List of Properties). The CRA may deem a disposition of certain assets (deemed disposition) on departure day.
  • Non-resident withholding tax: CPP and OAS paid to non-residents are subject to a 25% withholding tax (reduced to 15% under the Canada-Spain tax treaty on pensions). This affects your net income calculation.
  • Spain taxes worldwide income: As a Spanish tax resident (183+ days in Spain per year), you file a Spanish declaración de la renta reporting all worldwide income, including CPP, OAS, RRIF withdrawals, and Canadian investment income.
  • Canada-Spain double taxation treaty: Prevents double taxation on the same income. The treaty determines which country has primary taxing rights on each income type. Pension income is generally taxable in Spain as your country of residence, subject to treaty withholding rates.
  • TFSA caution: Canadian Tax-Free Savings Accounts (TFSA) are not recognized as tax-free by Spain. Investment income within a TFSA is taxable in Spain once you are a Spanish resident.
⚠️

Get cross-border tax advice before moving: The interaction between Canadian departure rules, non-resident withholding, and Spanish residency taxation is complex. Consult a tax advisor with Canadian-Spanish expertise well before your intended move date.

Frequently Asked Questions: Spain Retirement Visa for Canadians

What is the Spain retirement visa for Canadians?

The Spain retirement visa for Canadian citizens is the Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV). It allows Canadians to live in Spain without working, provided they demonstrate sufficient passive income — approximately €2,400/month for a single applicant in 2026. Canadian Pension Plan (CPP), Old Age Security (OAS), private pensions, RRSP/RRIF withdrawals, and rental income all count toward the threshold.

Which Spanish consulate in Canada handles my NLV application?

There are four Spanish consulates in Canada: Toronto (Ontario and Atlantic Canada), Montreal (Quebec and Maritime provinces), Vancouver (British Columbia), and Calgary (Alberta). Apply at the consulate covering your Canadian province of legal residence.

What is the RCMP criminal record check and how do I get one?

Canadian applicants must provide an RCMP Certified Criminal Record Check — not a provincial police check. Apply through the RCMP website or an accredited fingerprinting service. Processing times vary: 3–8 weeks via RCMP, faster via accredited services. The certificate must be apostilled through Global Affairs Canada and translated into Spanish by a sworn translator.

Do CPP and OAS count as income for the Spain retirement visa?

Yes. Both Canada Pension Plan (CPP) and Old Age Security (OAS) count as qualifying passive income. You'll need official Service Canada statements showing monthly amounts. The combined maximum CPP + OAS may be close to but often below €2,400/month depending on the CAD/EUR exchange rate — most applicants supplement with savings, private pension, or investment income.

How does the Canada-Spain tax treaty affect Canadian retirees?

The Canada-Spain double taxation treaty prevents Canadians from being taxed on the same income in both countries. Once resident in Spain, Spain taxes your worldwide income. A 25% Canadian non-resident withholding tax applies to pensions (reduced to 15% under the treaty). You must file a Canadian departure tax return (T1161) when ceasing Canadian residency. Consult a tax advisor specializing in Canadian-Spanish taxation before moving.
