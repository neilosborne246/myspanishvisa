Student Visa Valencia Spain: Study in the City That Invented Paella

Valencia has world-class universities, an extraordinary food and cultural scene, a Mediterranean beach 10 minutes from campus, and a cost of living that's significantly lower than Barcelona or Madrid. Here's everything you need to know about studying in Valencia on a Spanish student visa.

Pick up a paella made in Valencia and you understand immediately why the city takes food seriously. The real version — saffron-tinged rice, green beans, chicken and rabbit, cooked over a wood fire in a wide, shallow pan — is nothing like the tourist imitation served everywhere else. Valencia is a city that does things properly, and that philosophy extends to its universities, its infrastructure, and its quality of student life.

For international students looking at Spain, the conversation tends to start with Barcelona. Barcelona is excellent — and also expensive, crowded with tourists, and increasingly priced out of reach for students on a realistic budget. Valencia gives you a city with comparable culture, better weather, a genuine beach within city limits, and living costs that are meaningfully lower. It is consistently one of the most popular Erasmus destinations in all of Spain, which tells you something about how students rate the experience once they're there.

This guide covers the universities, the visa process, the neighbourhoods, and the practical realities of student life in Valencia.

Universities in Valencia for International Students

Universitat de València (UV)

The Universitat de València is one of Spain's oldest universities, founded in 1499. It is a comprehensive research university with strong faculties across sciences, humanities, law, social sciences, medicine, and pharmacy. UV has extensive international exchange agreements and a large Erasmus programme — each year it welcomes thousands of international students and sends a similar number abroad. The main campus is in Burjassot (science faculties) and the historic centre, with the main humanities and law buildings situated beautifully near the city centre.

UV is the right choice for students in humanities, social sciences, law, and life sciences who want an established, research-intensive institution with a genuine university town atmosphere.

Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV)

The UPV is consistently ranked among Spain's top technical universities and has a strong international reputation in engineering, architecture, computer science, telecommunications, and agrifood science. The UPV campus in Camino de Vera is a substantial, well-equipped modern campus with its own student accommodation, sports facilities, and a campus life that feels genuinely self-contained.

For students in engineering, architecture, or computer science, UPV is one of the best choices not just in Valencia but in Spain. Its architecture school in particular has an outstanding reputation — it produced Santiago Calatrava, one of the most celebrated architects of the modern era. The City of Arts and Sciences complex in Valencia, visible from across the city, is Calatrava's work and a daily reminder of what the school produces.

ESIC Business School Valencia

ESIC is a specialist business and marketing school with a campus in Valencia, part of a nationwide Spanish network. It's a good option for students focused specifically on business, marketing, and commercial studies who want a more vocational, industry-connected programme than a general university offers.

The Spanish Student Visa: What You Need

Non-EU students enrolled in a full-time course of more than 90 days in Spain need a student visa before they travel. The visa is applied for at your nearest Spanish consulate — you cannot get it on arrival. Key requirements include:

  • Official letter of acceptance from your Valencia university or institution
  • Proof of sufficient financial means — typically around €700–€900 per month for the duration of your course, shown via bank statements, scholarship documentation, or a sponsor's letter
  • Private health insurance valid in Spain, with no exclusions and no co-payments (many universities provide this or have affiliated partners)
  • Clean criminal record certificate from your country of residence
  • Medical certificate confirming no infectious diseases
  • Valid passport with at least one year of validity remaining
  • Completed visa application form and recent passport photos
  • Proof of accommodation in Valencia (university residence contract, rental agreement, or host family documentation)

Once you arrive in Spain, students staying longer than six months must obtain a TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) — the foreigner's identity card — from the local Oficina de Extranjeros. This is mandatory and must be done within 30 days of arrival.

Application timing: Apply for your student visa at least 8–12 weeks before your course start date. Some consulates have longer waiting times. Don't leave this to the last minute — document preparation takes time, and consulate appointments can be limited.

Cost of Student Life in Valencia

Here is a realistic monthly budget for a single international student in Valencia in 2026:

Shared flat (the most common option)

  • Room in shared flat (Benimaclet or Ruzafa): €350–€600
  • Groceries and cooking at home: €200–€280
  • Eating out and coffee: €150–€250
  • Transport (metro or bike): €30–€50
  • Mobile phone: €15–€25
  • Utilities (usually included in student flat): €0–€80
  • Social life, culture, weekends: €100–€200
  • Total: approximately €850–€1,500 per month

This compares favourably to Barcelona, where a shared room alone typically costs €500–€800, before you factor in the generally higher cost of food, transport, and leisure. Valencia's market culture — the Mercado Central and Mercado de Colón are extraordinary — keeps the cost of fresh food genuinely low.

Best Neighbourhoods for Students in Valencia

Benimaclet

Benimaclet is Valencia's classic student neighbourhood, particularly for UV students whose faculties are nearby. It has a slightly bohemian, university-town character — independent cafés, cheap restaurants, bookshops, and the kind of neighbourhood bodegas where you can eat a three-course lunch for €10. It is quieter than Ruzafa but full of student energy, with a strong local feel that doesn't feel overwhelmed by tourism.

Ruzafa

Ruzafa is Valencia's most popular neighbourhood for postgraduate students and longer-term international residents. It's more vibrant and gentrified than Benimaclet, with better restaurant and nightlife options, but slightly more expensive. Postgrad students and Erasmus students who want to be in the thick of the city tend to gravitate here.

Cabanyal

The former fishing village at the beach is Valencia's cheapest option for students who value proximity to the sea. It's a longer commute to most campus locations, but if you are doing a year abroad or a longer programme and want to feel genuinely embedded in a distinct Valencia neighbourhood, Cabanyal has real character and competitive prices. The neighbourhood has been attracting younger residents in recent years as gentrification arrives slowly from the centre.

Getting Around Valencia: The Bike City

Valencia is one of Spain's most cycling-friendly cities — a claim that is backed by infrastructure rather than aspiration. The city has an extensive network of dedicated cycling lanes, and the flat topography makes cycling practical year-round. The Valenbisi bike-share scheme gives students access to a network of docking stations across the city at a very low annual subscription cost.

For longer distances, the metro connects the main university areas (Benimaclet, Tarongers) with the city centre and the beach. A student metro pass is available and reasonably priced. The city is compact enough that most students find they rarely need to take public transport for anything except longer trips.

The Erasmus Experience in Valencia

Valencia is one of Spain's top Erasmus destinations, and this is not an accident. Both UV and UPV have large, well-organised international offices with good English-language support for incoming students. The Erasmus student network in Valencia is active — there are regular social events, language exchange meetups, and the general social infrastructure that makes arriving without an existing social circle much less daunting than it might be elsewhere.

The size of the international student community also means there is an established network of student-friendly services: estate agents accustomed to short-term leases, Spanish language courses calibrated for Erasmus arrivals, and student orientation events at the start of each semester.

The Valencia Advantage: Beach, Food, and Fallas

Three things that distinguish studying in Valencia from studying almost anywhere else in Europe.

The beach: Playa de la Malvarrosa and La Patacona are proper sandy Mediterranean beaches a 10–15 minute cycle from the city centre. For students from northern Europe, the ability to go for an evening swim in October — and again from May through September — is transformative. It makes the city feel alive in a way that landlocked university cities simply cannot match.

The food: Valencia's market culture, its paella tradition, and its broader commitment to eating well at every price point means that student life here involves genuinely good food even on a tight budget. The Mercado Central is one of the most beautiful food markets in Europe — and it is a practical place to shop, not a tourist spectacle.

Fallas: If you arrive in Valencia as an Erasmus or international student in January or February, the city will slowly escalate around you towards the Fallas festival in March. Giant satirical sculptures appear on street corners. Fireworks go off every lunchtime. The Cremà on the night of 19 March — when all the sculptures are burned simultaneously — is one of the most extraordinary collective experiences you will have anywhere. Students consistently describe it as one of the highlights of their year in Valencia.

Frequently Asked Questions

What universities are in Valencia for international students?
Valencia has two major public universities: the Universitat de València (UV), one of Spain's oldest and most prestigious institutions, strong in sciences, humanities, and law; and the Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV), consistently ranked among Spain's top technical universities, with strength in engineering, architecture, computer science, and agrifood science. ESIC Business School also has a Valencia campus for business and marketing students.
How much does it cost to live in Valencia as a student?
Valencia is one of the more affordable student cities in Spain. A shared room in a flat costs €350–€600 per month. A single student can budget approximately €850–€1,500 per month all-in, covering rent, food, transport, and a social life. This is significantly less than equivalent living costs in Barcelona or Madrid.
What are the requirements for a Spanish student visa?
You will need: a letter of acceptance from your Valencia institution, proof of sufficient funds (typically €700–€900 per month), private health insurance with no co-payments, a clean criminal record, a valid passport, and a medical certificate. The visa is applied for at your nearest Spanish consulate. Allow at least 8–12 weeks before your course start date.
Can I work in Spain on a student visa?
Yes, with restrictions. Spanish student visa holders can work up to 30 hours per week in jobs that don't interfere with your studies. You'll need a work permit, which is usually straightforward once you have your student visa and TIE card. Many international students in Valencia take part-time work in hospitality, English teaching, or within their university.
Is Valencia part of the Erasmus programme?
Yes. Both UV and UPV are major Erasmus participants with extensive exchange agreements across Europe and beyond. Valencia is one of the most popular Erasmus destinations in Spain — the combination of affordable living, good universities, and exceptional quality of life makes it a perennial top choice for exchange students.
Is Valencia a good city for international students?
Yes — consistently. Valencia offers lower living costs than Barcelona or Madrid, excellent public transport and cycling infrastructure, a strong international student community, and a Mediterranean lifestyle that most students find far preferable to northern European university cities. The beach is a 10-minute bike ride from the city centre. Ruzafa and Benimaclet have the café and bar culture that makes student social life very easy.
