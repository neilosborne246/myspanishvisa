Living in Gran Canaria as an Expat: Why Las Palmas Is Having a Moment

Las Palmas de Gran Canaria is not a beach resort with extras. It is a proper Spanish city of 380,000 people — the eighth largest in Spain — with a working port, a university, an active cultural scene, multiple distinct neighbourhoods, and a street-level vitality that catches most visitors completely off guard.

The Gran Canaria that most tourists see — Maspalomas, Playa del Inglés, the dunes — is the southern end of a very different island. The two experiences are genuinely distinct, and which one you choose defines your life here entirely. This guide is primarily about Las Palmas, because that's where the most interesting story is being written right now.

Las Palmas vs The South — Two Different Islands

Before anything else, understand that Gran Canaria is not one place for expats. It is two, and they attract very different people for very different reasons.

Las Palmas de Gran Canaria

City life. Real neighbourhoods with their own characters, local bars where regulars actually know each other, university energy, working professionals, serious café culture, and the Playa de Las Canteras — an excellent urban beach right inside the city. This is where you move if you want to live in something rather than simply be somewhere warm. The international and nomad-friendly community here is growing fast and has genuine substance.

The South (Maspalomas, Playa del Inglés, Meloneras, Puerto Rico)

Resort territory. Established British and German retirement communities with decades of roots. All-inclusive hotels, beach clubs, English-language menus, warm dune landscape, and a reliable infrastructure built specifically for expat comfort. This is excellent for retirees who want the sun package without complexity or cultural friction. It is a genuinely different proposition from Las Palmas.

Both are valid. The only mistake is not knowing which one you actually want before you arrive.

Why Las Palmas Is Having a Moment

Something has shifted in Las Palmas over the past few years, and it's worth being specific about what.

The Canary Islands government has officially backed Gran Canaria as a digital nomad destination through the GranCanaria Remote programme — a real, funded government initiative with resources, networking events, and a genuine institutional welcome to remote workers. This is not marketing. It is policy, and it has changed the energy of the city.

The nomad community has grown rapidly, bringing international connections and a certain optimism that mixes productively with the city's existing long-term expat base. Property costs, while rising, remain significantly below mainland Spain's major cities. And Playa de Las Canteras — a 3km urban beach protected by a natural reef — gives Las Palmas something genuinely unusual: a proper city lifestyle with a world-class beach at the end of the road.

Spanish cultural life is intact here. The city feels lived-in rather than staged. Its markets, local restaurants, and street life have not been packaged for visitors. That authenticity is something many expats, having exhausted the resort south, discover they have been looking for all along.

Cost of Living in Gran Canaria (Real Numbers, 2026)

The Canary Islands' own tax regime delivers a meaningful everyday cost advantage. IGIC — the local equivalent of VAT — is charged at 7%, versus mainland Spain's 21%. That difference appears in restaurant bills, services, and utilities without you having to do anything to capture it.

Las Palmas

  • 1-bedroom apartment, Triana or Vegueta area: €700–€1,000/month
  • 1-bedroom apartment, Las Canteras beachfront: €750–€1,100/month
  • Cheaper options in inland barrios: from €600/month
  • Groceries for one: €220–€300/month
  • Utilities (electricity, water, internet): €80–€120/month
  • Eating out: menú del día in local restaurants €10–€12
  • Total comfortable monthly budget for one: €1,500–€2,100

The South (Maspalomas area)

  • 1-bedroom apartment: €600–€950/month
  • Dining out in tourist areas tends to be priced accordingly — allow more for food

For comparison: a comfortable single-person life in Barcelona typically costs €2,500 or more per month. In Dublin, €3,000 or more. Gran Canaria offers strong value without the compromises usually associated with budget destinations.

Best Neighbourhoods in Las Palmas

Las Canteras / Alcaravaneras

Beachfront living in the city's most international neighbourhood. A mix of digital nomads, long-term expats, and local professionals. Excellent restaurants, café culture, and street life. The beach is at the end of the road — literally. This is premium Las Palmas living and prices reflect that, but the lifestyle value is strong.

Triana

The city's historic commercial heart. Beautiful early twentieth-century architecture, pedestrian shopping streets, elegant old buildings, and a distinctly urban Spanish character. Popular with those who want to feel embedded in the city rather than beside it. Slightly more affordable than Las Canteras.

Vegueta

The old town. Colonial-era architecture, quiet streets, an atmosphere that feels more like a small Andalusian city than an Atlantic island capital. Less touristy than you would expect. More residential, genuinely peaceful, and popular with longer-term expats who want character over convenience.

Ciudad Jardín

A leafy, residential neighbourhood slightly inland. Calmer than the beachfront areas. Popular with families and expats who have been here long enough to prioritise space and tranquillity over being close to the action.

Mesa y López

The commercial centre. Convenient for everything, with strong transport links and good amenities. Less character than the other options, but a practical base for those who care more about logistics than ambience.

Healthcare in Gran Canaria

Healthcare in Las Palmas is of a high standard. The two main public hospitals — Hospital Universitario de Gran Canaria Dr. Negrín and Complejo Hospitalario Universitario Insular-Materno Infantil — are full-service public facilities with comprehensive specialisms.

For expats who hold a Non-Lucrative Visa, private health insurance is a visa requirement. The good news is that private healthcare in Gran Canaria is of high quality and considerably cheaper than comparable coverage in the UK, US, Australia, or Canada. Expect to pay €100–€200 per month for a comprehensive policy as a healthy person aged 60–65, though premiums vary significantly by age and provider.

Private options in Las Palmas include Hospiten Gran Canaria, Clínica Parque, and several other established providers. English-speaking GPs and specialists are available in the city, particularly in the Las Canteras area. Most long-term expats are satisfied with the standard of care they receive, both public and private.

The Government's Remote Work Initiative

GranCanaria Remote is not a PR campaign. It is an official Canary Islands government programme with real funding behind it, designed to position Las Palmas as a serious digital nomad destination.

The programme offers information resources for incoming remote workers, runs networking events that bring the community together, and has actively invested in improving the coworking infrastructure of the city. El Cubo, the flagship coworking space in Las Palmas, has become a genuine hub — well-connected, popular, and a focal point for the nomad community in a way that few government-backed coworking initiatives manage to achieve.

This institutional backing matters more than it might initially seem. It signals long-term commitment. It provides resources that a grassroots nomad community cannot generate alone. And it has attracted a wave of remote workers who have tested the city and, in many cases, decided to stay.

Weather Year-Round — The "Eternal Spring" City

Las Palmas has earned its reputation as the City of Eternal Spring. The numbers bear it out:

  • Year-round average temperatures: 20–24°C — remarkably consistent regardless of season
  • Trade winds keep summers from becoming oppressively hot — a significant advantage over Seville, inland Spain, or even the Canarian south
  • The south of Gran Canaria (Maspalomas) is drier, warmer, and more consistently sunny; Las Palmas is slightly cooler and occasionally windier
  • Rainfall is low but not zero — sporadic light rain is possible in winter months, usually brief
  • No cold winters. No punishing summers. Just a very agreeable consistency that allows you to work outside and live outdoors year-round

This is the climate that attracts nomads who cannot work effectively in intense heat but cannot face northern winters. It is also the climate that has made Gran Canaria a favoured destination for professional athletes in winter training. The island simply delivers reliable outdoor conditions in a way that few places in the world match.

Community Life in Gran Canaria

Las Palmas has a diverse, cosmopolitan expat community that has evolved well beyond the traditional British-and-German core. Increasing numbers of Dutch, Scandinavian, French, American, and other nationalities have arrived — particularly since the nomad community began to grow. This diversity gives the city an international energy without it feeling rootless or transient.

The nomad community runs organised meetups, coworking events, language exchange evenings, and social gatherings. El Cubo serves as a focal point for many of these events. The traditional British and German retirement communities are strongest in the south — Maspalomas and Playa del Inglés — though there is also a long-established northern European presence in Las Palmas itself.

Spanish-language integration is possible at every level. The university of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria (ULPGC) offers language programmes, private academies are abundant, and the city's population is engaged enough with its international community to make integration feel rewarding rather than frustrating. Those who put the effort into Spanish — or even some degree of social participation — find the community opens up considerably.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Las Palmas a good place to live as an expat?
Yes, genuinely. Las Palmas de Gran Canaria is a real city of 380,000 people with excellent infrastructure, a cosmopolitan expat community, Spain's best year-round climate, and a growing reputation as one of Europe's most liveable expat destinations. The combination of city life, beach access, affordable living, and the government's active nomad-friendly programme has accelerated its rise significantly.
Is Gran Canaria expensive to live in?
No — it is one of Spain's more affordable places to live. The Canary Islands operate under their own tax regime with IGIC at 7% instead of mainland VAT at 21%, which reduces everyday costs. A 1-bedroom apartment in Las Palmas costs €700–€1,000 per month; a comfortable single-person budget is €1,500–€2,100 per month. Compared to mainland cities like Barcelona or Madrid, Gran Canaria is significantly cheaper.
Which is better — Las Palmas or the south of Gran Canaria?
They offer completely different experiences. Las Palmas is a real city with urban life, cultural energy, a mixed international community, and an excellent beach on its doorstep. The south — Maspalomas, Playa del Inglés — is established resort territory with large British and German retirement communities, more tourist infrastructure, and a warmer, drier climate. Your answer depends on whether you want to live in a city or a resort.
What visa do I need to live in Gran Canaria as a non-EU citizen?
The same as the rest of Spain. Non-EU citizens who won't be working in Spain typically apply for the Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV). Remote workers and freelancers apply for the Digital Nomad Visa (DNV). Both require applying through your Spanish consulate before arrival. Gran Canaria's official government support for remote workers makes it a particularly welcoming destination for DNV holders.
Is the internet good in Las Palmas?
Yes, excellent. Fibre coverage is widespread in Las Palmas with symmetric speeds readily available from major providers. Coworking spaces including El Cubo offer reliable, fast connections. Most Las Palmas apartments have easy access to fibre — verify at the specific property, but connectivity is generally not a concern in the city.
What is Playa de Las Canteras like?
Las Canteras is genuinely one of Spain's best urban beaches — a 3km stretch of sheltered sand right inside Las Palmas city. It is protected by a natural reef that keeps the water calm. You can walk from your city apartment, swim, eat at beachside restaurants, and walk back — all within the urban fabric. This is what makes Las Palmas unusual: proper city life with a world-class beach at the end of the road.
