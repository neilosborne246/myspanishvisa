Frequently Asked Questions

Questions about the DNV checklist and process

What documents do I need for the Spain Digital Nomad Visa?

The core DNV documents include: valid passport, recent biometric photos, criminal record certificate with apostille and sworn translation, medical certificate with apostille and sworn translation, compliant private health insurance, and proof of remote employment or self-employment income. The full checklist breaks each category down item by item, with separate sections for employees and the self-employed.

What is the income requirement for the Spain Digital Nomad Visa in 2026?

For 2026 the minimum income requirement is 200% of Spain's IPREM, currently approximately €2,762 per month for a single applicant. Each additional adult dependant adds 75% of IPREM and each child adds 25%. The checklist includes the current IPREM figures and a calculation guide to help you determine your exact threshold.

Can self-employed freelancers apply for the Spain Digital Nomad Visa?

Yes. Self-employed freelancers can apply, but the documentation differs from employed applicants. You will need client contracts showing a minimum 3-month working relationship, recent invoices, bank statements showing regular income, and company registration documents if applicable. The checklist has dedicated sections for both employment routes.

What is the Beckham Law and should I apply for it with my DNV?

The Beckham Law (Régimen Especial de Impatriados) is a Spanish tax regime that allows qualifying DNV holders to pay a flat 24% income tax on Spanish-source income for up to 6 years, rather than the standard progressive rates. You must apply within 6 months of registering with Spanish social security. Missing this window means losing the benefit entirely. The post-approval section of the checklist covers this timing.

How is the DNV checklist different from the NLV checklist?

The Digital Nomad Visa and Non-Lucrative Visa have different income evidence requirements. The DNV requires proof of active remote work income (employment letters, client contracts, invoices), whereas the NLV requires passive income (pension, investments, rental income). The application process is also different — DNV applications are submitted via the UGE (Unidad de Grandes Empresas), while NLV applications are made at your home country's Spanish consulate. This checklist is tailored specifically to DNV applicants.
