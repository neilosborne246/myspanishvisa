Private Health Insurance in Spain: A Complete Guide for Expats (2026)

Whether you are applying for a Non-Lucrative Visa or simply planning your move to Spain, understanding your healthcare options is essential. This guide covers everything from visa requirements to the best private providers for English speakers.

Home / Blog / Private Health Insurance in Spain: A Complete Guide for Expats

Spain has an excellent public healthcare system — consistently ranked among the best in Europe. But as a foreign national moving to Spain, you cannot simply assume you will have access to it from day one. Your healthcare situation depends entirely on your visa category, your employment status, and how long you have been in the country. For many expats, private health insurance is not just sensible — it is legally required.

Health Insurance and Your Spain Visa: What Is Required?

Non-Lucrative Visa requirement: Private health insurance with no co-payments is a mandatory condition of the Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV). Without a qualifying policy, your visa application will be refused. This is non-negotiable and frequently the point where applications fall down if applicants buy the wrong type of policy.

For the Non-Lucrative Visa, your policy must meet all of the following criteria:

  • Issued by an insurer authorised to operate in Spain
  • Comprehensive cover (hospitalisation, emergency care, specialist consultations, surgery)
  • No co-payments — meaning you pay nothing at the point of use for covered services
  • Coverage for the full duration of your visa (typically one year, renewable)
  • Cover in Spain, not just travel/emergency cover

For the Digital Nomad Visa, health insurance requirements are similar but there is more flexibility regarding international coverage, since DNV holders may work for clients in other countries. Speak with a specialist to confirm what qualifies for your specific application.

For the Student Visa, health insurance is also required and must cover the duration of your studies in Spain.

Spain's Public Healthcare System: The Seguridad Social

Spain's public healthcare system (Sistema Nacional de Salud) is funded through social security contributions and covers virtually all residents who pay into the system. If you work in Spain as an employee or as an autónomo (self-employed), you will pay social security contributions and gain access to public healthcare — covering GP visits, specialist referrals, emergency care, hospitalisations, and prescription medicines at subsidised rates.

However, the public system does not cover everything. Dental care beyond basic extractions, most optical services, and many complementary therapies are excluded. Waiting times for specialist appointments and non-urgent procedures can be long, particularly outside major cities. This is why many residents supplement their public entitlement with private insurance even after they are enrolled in the Seguridad Social.

Types of Private Health Insurance in Spain

The Spanish private health market offers several distinct product types. Understanding the differences helps you choose the right policy for your situation.

Full Medical Insurance (Seguro Médico Completo)

This is the most comprehensive option and the one required for visa purposes. It gives you access to a network of private doctors, clinics, and hospitals at no additional cost (on a no co-pay policy). Cover typically includes GP consultations, specialist appointments, diagnostic tests, surgery, hospitalisation, and emergency care. Some policies also include maternity care, mental health support, and physiotherapy.

Mixed or Reimbursement Insurance

This type of policy allows you to see any doctor you choose — not just those in the insurer's network — and then claim a percentage reimbursement from your insurer. It is more flexible but generally more expensive, and you must be comfortable managing claims and upfront costs.

Hospitalisation-Only Cover

A lower-cost option covering only hospital admissions and surgical procedures. Day-to-day GP and specialist visits are not covered. This is not suitable for NLV applications, which require comprehensive outpatient cover.

Dental Insurance

Stand-alone dental insurance is widely available in Spain and relatively affordable. Given that neither the public system nor most standard health plans cover routine dental work, this is a sensible addition for most expats planning a long-term stay.

Top Private Health Insurance Providers for Expats in Spain

The Spanish private health market has numerous providers. Below are those most commonly chosen by English-speaking expats:

Sanitas

Sanitas is a subsidiary of BUPA and is one of the most expat-friendly insurers in Spain. Their network of hospitals and clinics is extensive, and they have strong English-language customer support. Sanitas policies are widely accepted by Spanish consulates for NLV applications. Costs are competitive, and their digital app makes managing appointments straightforward.

Cigna Global

Cigna offers internationally-focused health plans that are popular with higher-income expats and those who travel frequently. Their plans are more expensive than local Spanish providers but offer broader global coverage and excellent English-language support. Particularly popular with American expats.

Adeslas

One of the largest Spanish health insurers by network size. Adeslas plans are competitively priced and widely accepted for visa applications. Their network includes hospitals across Spain, including in rural areas. Less international focus than Sanitas or Cigna but excellent value for those settling in one region.

AXA IMED

AXA's Spanish health division, which trades as IMED in some regions, is popular for its high-quality hospital network and premium service. Their plans are mid-to-upper range in price but include access to some of the best private hospitals in Spain.

Allianz Care

A strong choice for those who want international portability alongside their Spanish cover. Allianz Care plans can follow you if you travel or work elsewhere, making them popular with Digital Nomad Visa holders who work internationally.

What to Look For When Choosing a Policy

Before committing to any health insurance policy for your Spain visa or residency, check the following:

  • No co-pay confirmation: For NLV applications, this must be explicit in the policy documentation. Ask the insurer to confirm in writing.
  • Network coverage in your region: Check that the insurer has clinics and hospitals close to where you plan to live — not just in Madrid and Barcelona.
  • English-language support: Will you be able to speak to customer service and doctors in English when needed?
  • Pre-existing conditions: Most Spanish health insurers exclude pre-existing conditions for a waiting period (typically 6–12 months) or permanently. Read the exclusions carefully before you buy.
  • Mental health cover: Not all standard plans include psychiatric and psychological services. If this matters to you, confirm coverage before purchasing.
  • Maternity cover: Most plans have lengthy waiting periods for maternity-related claims (often 8–12 months). Plan ahead.

How Much Does Private Health Insurance Cost in Spain?

Pricing varies widely based on your age, health status, chosen provider, and level of cover. As a rough guide:

  • Ages 18–35: approximately €50–€90 per month for a standard no co-pay plan
  • Ages 36–50: approximately €80–€140 per month
  • Ages 51–65: approximately €120–€220 per month
  • Ages 66 and above: €180–€350+ per month, and some providers may decline cover or impose significant exclusions

Older applicants, particularly those over 70, sometimes face difficulty finding qualifying policies at reasonable cost. This is one of the most common challenges our team helps NLV applicants navigate — getting the right policy for your age and health situation before you apply.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is private health insurance compulsory for the Non-Lucrative Visa?
Yes. Private health insurance with no co-payments is a mandatory requirement for the Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV). The policy must provide comprehensive cover in Spain with no co-pay clauses, and must be issued by a company authorised to operate in Spain. Travel insurance or short-term policies will not be accepted.
What does 'no co-payments' mean for NLV health insurance?
Co-payments are fixed charges you pay each time you use a medical service — for example, €5 per GP visit or €15 per specialist consultation. For NLV purposes, the Spanish consulate requires a policy with zero co-payments, meaning all covered services are included with no additional charge at point of use.
Can I use my existing travel insurance or EHIC card for the NLV?
No. Travel insurance is not accepted for the Non-Lucrative Visa. The EHIC (or GHIC for UK nationals) covers temporary stays but does not satisfy residency visa requirements. You need a full private health insurance policy from a company authorised to operate in Spain, with cover for the entire period of your visa.
How much does private health insurance cost in Spain?
Costs vary significantly depending on your age, the level of cover, and the provider. As a general guide, expect to pay between €50 and €200 per month for a standard policy. Older applicants and those with pre-existing conditions will typically pay more. Internationally-focused providers like Cigna Global and AXA IMED tend to be priced higher but offer English-language support and broader coverage.
Once I have residency, can I access the Spanish public health system?
If you are working in Spain and paying social security contributions, you will automatically have access to the public system (Seguridad Social). However, NLV holders are not permitted to work, so they must maintain private health insurance throughout their visa period. Some NLV holders choose to keep private insurance even after gaining access to the public system for the faster appointment times and access to English-speaking doctors.
Are dental and optical costs covered by Spanish private health insurance?
Basic dental and optical care is not covered by Spain's public health system, and most standard private health insurance policies in Spain do not include comprehensive dental or optical cover either. You can purchase standalone dental insurance policies or choose a premium health plan that bundles in these extras.
