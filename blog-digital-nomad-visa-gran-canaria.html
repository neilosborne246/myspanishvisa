Digital Nomad Visa Gran Canaria: Spain's Best Kept Secret for Remote Workers

The Canary Islands government isn't quietly hoping remote workers will discover Gran Canaria — they've built an entire programme to attract them. Here's why Las Palmas belongs at the top of every DNV shortlist, and exactly how the visa works.

Home / Blog / Digital Nomad Visa Gran Canaria: Spain's Best Kept Secret for Remote Workers

The Canary Islands government isn't quietly hoping remote workers will discover Gran Canaria — they've actively gone after them. The GranCanaria Remote programme is a funded government initiative, backed by the Cabildo de Gran Canaria, that positions Las Palmas de Gran Canaria as a top European destination for digital nomads and remote workers. El Cubo coworking hub has become a genuine community anchor. The city's "Eternal Spring" climate — 20–24°C, every month, year after year — means the promise of perpetual working weather is not marketing copy. It is accurate.

Gran Canaria is one of the best Digital Nomad Visa destinations in Spain. This article explains why — in specifics rather than adjectives — and walks through everything you need to know about the DNV application for Gran Canaria: requirements, costs, the coworking scene, internet quality, the Beckham Law tax angle, and how the city compares to its main European competitors.

Why Gran Canaria Is a Top DNV Destination

The case for Las Palmas de Gran Canaria rests on several genuine advantages, not just favourable geography.

Official government backing

GranCanaria Remote is not a private initiative or a tourism marketing slogan — it is supported by the Cabildo de Gran Canaria, the island's governing council. This translates into actual resources: events, information programmes, welcome initiatives, and a genuine institutional commitment to the nomad community. Gran Canaria actively wants remote workers; most destinations merely tolerate them.

Year-round working climate

Las Palmas averages 20–24°C regardless of season. Consistent trade winds keep summers manageable — there is no punishing heat, no grey winters, and no disrupted work schedule caused by weather. You can work outdoors, at beach cafés, or in climate-controlled coworking with complete predictability year-round. This is not an exaggeration: the climate genuinely holds.

Coworking infrastructure

Las Palmas has El Cubo as its flagship hub, Lanzadera Las Palmas as a startup-accelerator coworking space, and a growing ecosystem of smaller spaces. The infrastructure is real and operational — not aspiring to exist. The city has the depth of coworking provision that you would expect to find in a destination twice its size.

Cost and value

Las Palmas is affordable relative to the quality of life it delivers. Lower than Lisbon, significantly lower than Barcelona, and with the Canary Islands IGIC tax rate at 7% (versus mainland VAT at 21%) reducing everyday costs across groceries, restaurants, services, and utilities. The tax structure difference is not cosmetic — it compounds across every purchase you make.

Community and critical mass

Organised meetups, nomad social events, and an international mix of British, German, Dutch, American, French, and Latin American workers have created the self-reinforcing dynamic that distinguishes a genuine nomad hub from a nice city that happens to have Wi-Fi. Nomads attract more nomads. Las Palmas has reached that tipping point.

City and beach combined

Las Palmas is a real city of 380,000 people — Spain's eighth largest — with Playa de Las Canteras, one of the best urban beaches in Spain, three kilometres from the city centre. You are not choosing between city life and beach access. Both are available simultaneously, within the same daily routine.

Las Palmas Coworking Scene

The coworking ecosystem in Las Palmas is deeper than most guides acknowledge.

El Cubo

The flagship. El Cubo is the most popular nomad coworking hub in Las Palmas — well-equipped, reliable internet, good community atmosphere, and regular events that make it the social gravitational centre of the Las Palmas nomad scene. If you spend more than a few weeks in Las Palmas, you will almost certainly end up here at some point. It has become the de facto meeting place for the city's international remote worker community.

Lanzadera Las Palmas

A startup accelerator with coworking facilities. More entrepreneurial in character than El Cubo — well-suited to those building companies rather than simply working remotely for existing employers. Good for founders, freelancers scaling their practice, and anyone who finds the typical nomad coworking atmosphere insufficiently focused.

Smaller spaces and café culture

Multiple smaller coworking spaces have opened as demand has grown. Café culture in Las Palmas is also strong, with a number of venues well-suited to laptop work. Hot desk memberships at the main spaces typically run €150–€300 per month — day passes available at most locations if you want to try before committing.

Internet Speeds and Connectivity

Internet quality is one of Gran Canaria's genuine strengths as a nomad destination, and one that is often understated in comparison to competitors.

Fibre coverage in Las Palmas is excellent. Symmetric gigabit connections are available from major providers including Movistar, Orange, Vodafone, and DIGI. The infrastructure is modern and well-maintained. All major coworking spaces — El Cubo included — offer reliable, fast connections designed specifically for remote workers.

Most apartments in Las Palmas have straightforward access to fibre broadband. Always verify connectivity for a specific property before signing a lease, but in practice this is rarely a concern in the city. 5G coverage in Las Palmas provides a solid backup option for mobile working.

The south resort areas have good connectivity in developed zones; verify for specific properties if you are considering basing yourself outside Las Palmas. For most nomads, Las Palmas city is the obvious base — and internet quality is simply not a compromise you need to make here.

Digital Nomad Visa Requirements — Overview

Spain's Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) was introduced in 2023 and is designed for non-EU citizens who work remotely for employers or clients based outside Spain. Gran Canaria — specifically Las Palmas — has become one of the most popular landing points for DNV applicants in Spain.

Key requirements for 2026:

  • Active remote working arrangement: employment contract with a non-Spanish company or demonstrable self-employment with predominantly non-Spanish clients
  • Minimum income of approximately €2,646 per month (3x the Spanish Minimum Wage — verify the current SMI-linked figure, as it updates annually)
  • At least 80% of income from non-Spanish sources (for employed applicants); self-employed applicants must demonstrate a remote working arrangement and sufficient income
  • Private health insurance valid in Spain with no exclusions, for the full duration of the permit
  • Clean criminal record from all countries lived in over the past five years
  • No Spanish tax residency in the previous five years

The DNV can be applied for at your Spanish consulate before arrival (this route grants a 1-year permit). It can also be applied for in-country if you enter on a tourist visa — this in-country route grants a 3-year permit from the outset, which many applicants prefer. Both routes have their logistics; the right choice depends on your circumstances.

Cost of Living for DNV Holders in Gran Canaria

These are realistic 2026 figures for Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. The IGIC effect (7% vs mainland VAT at 21%) is baked into all of these numbers.

  • 1-bedroom apartment in Las Palmas: €700–€1,000 per month (Las Canteras beachfront area runs slightly higher)
  • Coworking hot desk membership: €150–€300 per month
  • Groceries: €220–€300 per month
  • Utilities (electricity, water, internet): €80–€120 per month
  • Eating out: menú del día in local spots €10–€12; broader range €15–€35 per meal
  • Monthly total for a nomad in Las Palmas: approximately €1,500–€2,100

For context: Lisbon — the destination Las Palmas most directly competes with — has seen significant rent inflation over the past three years. Las Palmas is now comparable or slightly cheaper on most line items. Compared to Barcelona or Madrid, Las Palmas is significantly cheaper across the board. The DNV income requirement of ~€2,646 per month leaves a comfortable margin above typical living costs — meaning you can save, not just break even.

The Beckham Law (Ley Beckham) — The Tax Angle

The Special Expat Tax Regime — nicknamed the Beckham Law after the footballer who famously used it — is a provision that DNV holders should investigate before making any final decisions.

If eligible, DNV holders who become Spanish tax residents can opt into the regime and pay a flat 24% income tax rate on Spanish-source income up to €600,000 per year, rather than progressive rates that reach up to 47%. The regime is available for the first six years of Spanish tax residence and must be applied for within six months of starting work in Spain.

This is a significant financial consideration for many nomads, particularly those on higher incomes where the difference between a flat 24% rate and progressive rates reaching 47% is substantial over a six-year period.

However, two important caveats apply specifically to Gran Canaria. First, the Canary Islands have their own regional tax framework that interacts with the national regime — specialist advice is particularly important here, not just advisable in general. Second, the benefit is not uniform: it depends heavily on income level, income structure, and individual circumstances. Not everyone benefits equally from the Beckham Law, and the calculation is not always intuitive.

The strong recommendation: take advice from a tax specialist before applying for your DNV if the Beckham Law is relevant to your situation. Do this early — before you commit to a timeline, not after you have already arrived.

The Nomad Community in Las Palmas

Las Palmas has one of Spain's most organised remote-worker communities outside Madrid and Barcelona — and in some respects, the community dynamic is stronger than in larger cities, where the nomad population is more dispersed.

The GranCanaria Remote programme drives regular events: meetups, networking sessions, social occasions, language exchanges. The international mix is broad — British, German, Dutch, American, French, and Latin American workers are all present in meaningful numbers. The community is not mono-cultural, which keeps the social experience more interesting than some nomad hubs that end up being predominantly one nationality.

Networking in Las Palmas often happens organically rather than through formal channels: at El Cubo, at Las Canteras beach, at the city's café scene. The critical mass matters. There are enough nomads here that you do not need to seek connections deliberately — they find you through shared spaces and shared interests.

Online communities are active: Facebook groups, Nomad List, and dedicated Slack channels all serve the Las Palmas nomad community specifically. Gran Canaria consistently appears in Nomad List's top-rated destinations, with high marks across internet quality, safety, cost, and weather. These ratings reflect the actual experience of the nomads who live there — not marketing claims.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Gran Canaria good for digital nomads?
Yes, genuinely one of Spain's best destinations. The combination of an official government programme (GranCanaria Remote), established coworking infrastructure centred on El Cubo, year-round working climate at 20–24°C, affordable living costs, and a strong international nomad community makes Las Palmas one of the most compelling DNV destinations in Europe. It consistently ranks highly on nomad community platforms like Nomad List.
What coworking spaces are in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria?
El Cubo is the flagship nomad coworking hub — well-established, community-focused, popular with the international remote worker community. Lanzadera Las Palmas adds a startup-accelerator dimension. There are also several smaller coworking spaces and a strong café-working culture. Hot desk memberships typically run €150–€300 per month.
What is the internet like in Las Palmas?
Excellent. Fibre coverage is widespread with symmetric gigabit connections available from major providers. All major coworking spaces offer reliable, fast internet. Most apartments in Las Palmas can access fibre with no difficulty. Internet quality is one of Gran Canaria's genuine strengths as a nomad destination — it is not a compromise you have to make here.
What is the Beckham Law and does it apply in Gran Canaria?
The Special Expat Tax Regime — nicknamed the Beckham Law — allows eligible DNV holders who become Spanish tax residents to pay a flat 24% income tax rate on Spanish-source income for up to 6 years, instead of progressive rates reaching 47%. It applies across Spain including Gran Canaria. The Canary Islands' own tax framework means specialist advice is particularly important here. Apply within 6 months of starting work in Spain if you want to use it.
What is GranCanaria Remote?
GranCanaria Remote is an official initiative backed by the Cabildo de Gran Canaria — the island's governing council — to promote Gran Canaria as a digital nomad and remote-work destination. It provides information resources, networking events, and represents a genuine institutional commitment to the nomad community rather than just a marketing slogan. It is one of the factors that distinguishes Gran Canaria from other Spanish destinations that merely accept nomads rather than actively welcoming them.
How does Las Palmas compare to Lisbon or Madeira for digital nomads?
Las Palmas competes directly with both. Its advantages include a larger city feel than Madeira (which can feel isolated after a few months), lower costs than Lisbon (which has seen significant rent inflation), and stronger official government support than either. The climate is similar across all three. Gran Canaria's tax regime (IGIC at 7%) and Beckham Law access give it a specific financial edge. Nomad List ratings put Las Palmas consistently in Europe's top tier.
