Student Visa San Sebastián: Study in Spain's Gastronomic Capital

Not every city earns its reputation. San Sebastián has. For a certain kind of student, it is simply the best city in the world to study — and this guide explains exactly why, what it costs, and how to get there on a Spain student visa.

Published 24 July 2026  ·  By My Spanish Visa

Most student city guides talk about scale: tens of thousands of students, dozens of faculties, sprawling campuses, vast international communities. San Sebastián offers something entirely different. It is a small, sophisticated, Atlantic city of around 190,000 people, built around a bay so beautiful it has been called the most perfectly shaped in Europe. It has more Michelin-starred restaurants per capita than any city on earth. And it is home to an institution that has changed the way the world thinks about food education: the Basque Culinary Center.

If you are a culinary arts student, a food scientist, a gastronomy researcher, or a law student attracted to the prestige of IE University's San Sebastián campus, this is not a city you end up in by accident. You come here with intention, because what San Sebastián offers cannot be replicated anywhere else. The question is not whether it is worth it. The question is whether you are the right student for it — and whether you understand what you are signing up for, financially and logistically, before you arrive.

The Basque Culinary Center — A World-Class Reason to Come

The Basque Culinary Center was founded in 2011 by a consortium of Basque chefs including Juan Mari Arzak, Andoni Luis Aduriz, Pedro Subijana, Karlos Arguiñano, Martin Berasategui, and others — names that defined the avant-garde of European cuisine. This is not a marketing detail. It is the structural reason why the BCC is different from every other culinary school in the world. The institution was conceived by the people who built the Basque gastronomic movement from within, and it sits inside the city that produced it.

The BCC offers a four-year Bachelor's Degree in Gastronomy and Culinary Arts, covering everything from kitchen technique and food science to business management, food anthropology, and innovation. Beyond the bachelor's, the BCC runs a range of Masters programs — in food innovation and management, food design, food product development, and gastronomic sciences — many with significant English-language instruction. Executive education programs attract working chefs, food industry professionals, and entrepreneurs from across Europe and Latin America.

What separates BCC students from culinary graduates anywhere else is context. You are not studying food in a vacuum. You are in a city whose cider houses open in January, whose fishing boats supply the market at La Bretxa every morning, whose pintxos bars are laboratories of technique disguised as neighbourhood bars. The restaurants you visit as part of your education — Arzak, Mugaritz, Akelarre — are the same restaurants your professors helped create. The curriculum and the city are inseparable by design.

University of the Basque Country — UPV/EHU

For students who want a broader academic programme, the University of the Basque Country (UPV/EHU) is the public university serving the Basque region. Its Gipuzkoa campus operates in and around San Sebastián, offering undergraduate and postgraduate programmes across sciences, engineering, social sciences, humanities, and business. Fees are substantially lower than private institutions, and the university participates in Erasmus exchanges, giving students a well-established international student community to connect with.

UPV/EHU is a large, research-active university with genuine academic depth. Students in economics, environmental sciences, materials engineering, and Basque studies in particular will find strong programmes here. The trade-off is that most teaching is conducted in Spanish and Basque rather than English, which makes language preparation genuinely important for incoming international students. Those who arrive with solid Spanish and a willingness to engage with Basque culture will find this a richly rewarding environment.

IE Law School and Mondragon Unibertsitatea

IE University is one of Spain's most internationally recognised higher education institutions, known primarily for its business and law programmes. Its San Sebastián campus hosts IE Law School, offering LLM and specialist legal programmes with instruction primarily in English. For law students who want a prestigious Spanish institution, strong European legal networks, and the quality of life that San Sebastián provides, this is a compelling combination. The campus attracts an internationally mobile student body and a faculty with significant practice experience.

Mondragon Unibertsitatea operates on a cooperative model that is unusual in European higher education — and intentionally so. Founded in the Basque cooperative tradition, Mondragon emphasises entrepreneurship, cooperative business structures, and applied learning. Its San Sebastián presence offers programmes in business, engineering, and humanities. Some content is available in English. For students interested in alternative economic models, social enterprise, or Basque cooperative culture, Mondragon is worth serious consideration. It is a smaller, more intensive environment than UPV/EHU, with a strong sense of institutional identity.

Student Life in San Sebastián — What It Is Actually Like

San Sebastián is a compact, walkable city, and that compactness shapes student life in ways that matter. You are never far from the sea, from the old town, from the market, or from a good bar. The city does not have the sprawling campus geography of a large university city. Instead, it has neighbourhoods, and the student neighbourhood is Gros.

Gros sits east of the Urumea river, facing Zurriola beach — a genuine surf beach with Atlantic swells and a culture to match. It has the city's best concentration of cafés, independent bars, and affordable restaurants outside the Parte Vieja. It is lively without being chaotic, and considerably more affordable than the old town for accommodation. Most students looking for a shared flat in San Sebastián start their search in Gros. The neighbourhood of Antiguo, closer to UPV/EHU's campus, is the other main student residential area.

The Parte Vieja — the old quarter, the pintxos heartland — becomes a central part of student social life in a way that has no real equivalent in most university cities. Eating in San Sebastián is not expensive if you do it as locals do. A txikiteo, the local practice of moving bar to bar eating one or two pintxos at each, gives you access to some of the world's most technically accomplished bar food for €2–3 per piece. An evening in the Parte Vieja with five or six stops might cost €15–20 and be more satisfying than a restaurant dinner costing four times as much elsewhere. For culinary students in particular, this is not just cheap eating: it is fieldwork.

The city's Atlantic climate means rain, particularly in autumn and winter. This is not the Mediterranean Spain of constant sunshine. Summers are mild and genuinely pleasant at 18–22°C. The surf is best in autumn. Hiking in the surrounding mountains is year-round. Students who want outdoor pursuits alongside their studies will find a city that supports exactly that. Students who came for the beach and sunshine of southern Spain may need to recalibrate their expectations.

What Does Student Life Cost in San Sebastián?

San Sebastián is the most expensive city in the Basque Country and among the most expensive in Spain for accommodation. This is the honest starting point. It is not Murcia, it is not Salamanca, and it is not Valencia. The flip side is that what you get for your money — in terms of city quality, safety, food, and general standard of living — is exceptional. But you need to plan for it.

Monthly budget guide for students in San Sebastián (2026):

  • Room in shared flat, Gros or Antiguo: €450–€700/month
  • Private studio (solo): €800–€1,100/month
  • Food (pintxos culture, markets, home cooking): €300–€450/month
  • Local transport (bus pass, walking-friendly city): €40–€60/month
  • Private health insurance: €50–€100/month
  • Utilities (typically included in shared flats): €0–€80/month
  • Realistic total: €1,200–€1,800/month

The food offset is genuine and significant. If you eat as a San Sebastián resident eats — markets, home cooking, and strategic pintxos evenings rather than sit-down restaurants — your food costs can be surprisingly reasonable for the quality involved. The city's food culture is not a luxury layer on top of daily life; it is woven into daily life at every price point.

For comparison: a student in Murcia might manage €800–€1,000/month all-in. A student in Salamanca might manage €900–€1,200. San Sebastián is a step above both. Budget carefully, look for shared accommodation early (the rental market is competitive), and treat the food culture as part of your education rather than a luxury.

Spain Student Visa Requirements

To study in San Sebastián for longer than 90 days, non-EU students need Spain's Type D Student Visa, formally the visado de estudios de larga duran de duración. This is applied for at the Spanish consulate or embassy in your home country before you travel. You cannot apply from within Spain.

Documents you will need

  • Valid passport with at least six months remaining validity beyond your intended stay
  • Official acceptance letter or enrollment confirmation from your Spanish institution
  • Proof of financial means — typically bank statements showing at least ~€600/month for the duration of your course
  • Comprehensive private health insurance valid in Spain for the entire duration of your stay
  • Clean criminal record certificate from your home country (or any country you have lived in for five or more years)
  • Completed visa application form and recent passport photographs
  • Proof of accommodation arrangements in Spain

Processing times vary by consulate, but you should plan for a minimum of 8–12 weeks from application submission to visa in hand. Starting the process 2–3 months before your intended travel date is the minimum sensible lead time. Consulates in some countries take longer; check yours specifically and build in a buffer.

Part-time work on a student visa

Spain's student visa permits up to 30 hours per week of part-time work, which is a meaningful allowance for self-funding students. The hospitality sector in San Sebastián — given the density of restaurants, bars, and food businesses — offers practical work opportunities that for culinary students have the added advantage of being directly relevant to their studies. Note that finding English-language work is harder here than in larger or more tourist-heavy cities; Spanish language ability is an asset.

The Language Picture

The language situation in San Sebastián is more layered than in most Spanish cities, and it is worth understanding before you arrive. There are effectively three languages in play: Spanish, Basque (Euskara), and to a growing extent, English.

Basque is the indigenous language of the region and is present everywhere — on street signs, in official communications, in conversation between locals. It is a language isolate, unrelated to Spanish or any other European language, and genuinely challenging to learn. You are not expected to speak it as a foreign student, and locals are accustomed to being addressed in Spanish. But Basque is a living cultural reality, not a curiosity, and an awareness of its significance is part of understanding where you are.

At the institutional level, the language varies considerably by programme. IE Law School runs its programmes primarily in English. The Basque Culinary Center offers English-taught Masters programmes, and some BCC bachelor content is available in English, though a significant proportion is in Spanish. UPV/EHU programmes are primarily in Spanish and Basque. Mondragon offers some English-language options. For daily life — shopping, banking, navigating the city, socialising outside your student cohort — Spanish is the working language, and solid Spanish proficiency will substantially improve your experience.

Students arriving specifically for English-taught programmes at IE or BCC will manage without Spanish initially. But those who invest in language before arrival will unlock a city that communicates in ways that simply are not accessible to those who rely on English alone.

Is San Sebastián the Right Student City for You?

San Sebastián is a specific city for a specific kind of student, and the fit matters more here than in a large, generalist university city where almost anyone can find their niche.

San Sebastián works particularly well if you are

  • A culinary arts or gastronomy student, particularly one coming specifically for the Basque Culinary Center
  • A law student drawn to IE Law School's programmes and European legal networks
  • A business or entrepreneurship student interested in Mondragon's cooperative model
  • Someone who values a sophisticated, small, culturally rich city over a large campus environment
  • A student who sees food culture, outdoor life, and Atlantic landscape as part of the educational experience
  • Someone with a reasonable financial cushion for a higher-cost Spanish city

San Sebastián is probably the wrong choice if you are

  • On a very tight student budget and need one of Spain's more affordable cities
  • Looking for the large, sprawling international student community found at major Spanish universities
  • Expecting Mediterranean climate — consistent sunshine and warmth year-round
  • Seeking a generalist undergraduate programme across a wide range of subjects
  • Not yet decided on your field and looking to use university as a discovery process

For the student who fits the first list, San Sebastián is not a compromise or a secondary option. It is the only sensible choice. Nowhere else in the world can you study gastronomy inside the culinary culture that shaped modern European cooking, eat pintxos from bars that function as working laboratories of food technique, and do it all in a city small enough that it never stops feeling human. That is the offer. Whether it is yours to take depends on who you are and what you are studying.

Frequently Asked Questions

What universities are in San Sebastián?
The main institutions are the University of the Basque Country (UPV/EHU) with its Gipuzkoa campus, the globally famous Basque Culinary Center (BCC), IE Law School (part of IE University), and Mondragon Unibertsitatea. Each serves different academic needs: UPV/EHU for broad undergraduate and postgraduate study; BCC for culinary arts and gastronomy; IE Law for legal programmes; Mondragon for cooperative business and engineering.
What is the Basque Culinary Center and why is it famous?
The Basque Culinary Center is a culinary arts university based in San Sebastián, co-created by some of the world's leading Basque chefs including Juan Mari Arzak, Andoni Luis Aduriz, and Pedro Subijana. It offers a bachelor's degree in Gastronomy and Culinary Arts, Masters programmes in food innovation, and executive programmes. Its location in the city with the world's highest density of Michelin stars is no coincidence — students study, research, and eat within one of the world's great food cultures.
How much does student life cost in San Sebastián?
More than most Spanish cities — and you should know this going in. A realistic monthly budget for a student in San Sebastián is €1,200–€1,800, including accommodation in Gros (€450–€700 for a room in a shared flat), food, transport, and personal spending. The genuine food offset is real: eating well in the Parte Vieja's pintxos bars costs far less than restaurant dining of equivalent quality elsewhere. It is still more than Murcia, Salamanca, or Seville — but the quality of daily life is in a different register entirely.
What student visa do I need to study in San Sebastián?
Spain's Type D Student Visa (visado de estudios). Apply at the Spanish consulate in your home country with: your university acceptance letter, proof of financial means (~€600/month minimum), comprehensive private health insurance, and a clean criminal record certificate. Apply at least 2–3 months before your course start date. On a student visa, you are permitted to work up to 30 hours per week alongside your studies.
Is English spoken at San Sebastián universities?
It varies significantly by institution. IE Law School programmes are primarily in English. The Basque Culinary Center has English-taught Masters and some bachelor-level content, though parts of the programme are in Spanish. UPV/EHU programmes are mostly in Spanish and Basque. Mondragon has some English-taught programmes. For all practical daily life in San Sebastián, Spanish is the working language, though younger Basques increasingly speak English.
Which neighbourhood do students live in San Sebastián?
Gros is the main student and young professional neighbourhood. It is lively, has a great café and bar scene, is adjacent to Zurriola surf beach, and is more affordable than the Parte Vieja or Centro. It is the neighbourhood to look for shared accommodation if you are studying at any of San Sebastián's institutions. Antiguo is also popular for students at UPV/EHU's campus.
