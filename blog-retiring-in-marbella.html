Retiring in Marbella: The Non-Lucrative Visa and the Costa del Sol Life

Marbella is more expensive than Seville or Alicante — let's get that out of the way immediately. But for retirees who want a combination of exceptional climate, world-class golf, reliable private healthcare, and a well-established English-speaking community, the cost-to-lifestyle ratio is genuinely compelling.

Every year, thousands of British, Irish, American, Canadian, Australian, and South African retirees look seriously at Marbella. Many look and go elsewhere — to Alicante, to Seville, to the Algarve — because of the cost. Some of those are making the right decision for their budget. But many discover, when they sit down with the actual numbers, that Marbella is not as far out of reach as its reputation suggests.

This guide is written for people who are genuinely considering retiring here: it gives you the real costs, the honest lifestyle assessment, the practical steps for the Non-Lucrative Visa, and the healthcare picture. No gilding, no selling. Just what you need to make a clear decision.

Is Marbella Right for You? An Honest Assessment

Marbella retirement works particularly well for a specific kind of person. You probably fit the profile if:

  • Climate is a primary motivator — you genuinely want warm, reliably sunny weather year-round
  • You want to play golf seriously, or enjoy outdoor activities that require good weather (hiking, cycling, tennis, beach walking)
  • You value having an established, English-speaking expat community around you, particularly in the first years
  • You have a pension, investment income, or savings that comfortably exceed the NLV income threshold (approximately €2,400 per month for a single person)
  • You are comfortable with the fact that you will need a car for most of daily life

Marbella is probably not the right choice if budget is the primary constraint. If you are working with an income of €1,500–€1,800 per month, the same budget will go further and be less stressful in Malaga city, Seville, or Alicante — all excellent places to retire in Spain. Marbella rewards a reasonable buffer above the minimum, because the gap between a comfortable life and a stressed one is wider here than in cheaper Spanish cities.

Monthly Budget Breakdown for Marbella Retirees

Here are three realistic budget scenarios for 2026. All figures cover a single person; for couples, add roughly 50% to rent and perhaps 30% overall.

Entry-level: making it work on a modest pension

  • Rent, one-bedroom apartment in San Pedro or outer Nueva Andalucía: €850–€1,100
  • Groceries (Mercadona, Lidl): €220–€280
  • Utilities (electricity, water, internet): €110–€150
  • Private health insurance (age 60–70): €100–€180
  • Car costs (insurance, fuel, maintenance): €180–€250
  • Dining out (local tapas bars, menu del día lunches): €150–€250
  • Leisure, miscellaneous: €150–€200
  • Total: approximately €1,760–€2,410 per month

At this level, life is comfortable but careful. You live well by most global standards — the climate, beaches, and outdoor life are free — but you won't be dining at beach clubs or joining a golf club with a high membership fee.

Comfortable: the typical NLV retiree lifestyle

  • Rent, two-bedroom apartment in a complex with pool (Nueva Andalucía or east Marbella): €1,300–€1,800
  • Groceries plus regular dining out: €500–€750
  • Utilities: €130–€180
  • Private health insurance: €150–€280
  • Car: €250–€350
  • Golf (pay-and-play or modest membership), leisure, travel: €400–€700
  • Total: approximately €2,730–€4,060 per month

At this level, you are living a genuinely good life. Regular golf, meals out, the odd weekend trip to Seville or Granada, good wine from the local Mercadona at €5 a bottle. This is what most comfortable NLV retirees actually experience day-to-day.

Premium: Marbella as you imagine it

  • Rent, villa or large apartment in a prime location: €2,500–€5,000+
  • Food and dining at beach clubs and good restaurants: €1,000–€2,000
  • Utilities: €200–€400 (villas with pools can run higher)
  • Private health insurance: €200–€400
  • Car (possibly two vehicles): €400–€800
  • Golf club membership, travel, entertainment: €800–€2,000
  • Total: €5,100–€10,600 per month and upward

Best Areas for Retirees in Marbella

Old Town (Casco Antiguo) and central Marbella

The Old Town is the most appealing area for retirees who do not want to depend on a car for every errand. Walking distance from the seafront promenade, the covered market, good restaurants, pharmacies, and daily shops — it functions as a proper small town in miniature. The area around the Parque de la Alameda and the old castle walls is particularly pleasant. Apartments here tend to be older and smaller than in the residential complexes, but the trade-off in character and convenience is real. A one-bedroom apartment runs €900–€1,400 per month depending on condition and exact location.

Nueva Andalucía — best value for the golf lifestyle

If golf is part of your retirement plan, Nueva Andalucía is the obvious base. It sits in the golf valley, within easy reach of Aloha Golf, Las Brisas, and Los Naranjos — all excellent courses. Apartment complexes here are generally modern, well-maintained, and come with pools and gardens. You pay considerably less per square metre than you would near the coast, and you get more space for your money. Many long-term expats who initially lived in more expensive coastal areas have relocated to Nueva Andalucía and found it preferable in practical terms.

East Marbella and the Santa Clara area

The eastern part of the municipality, around the Santa Clara Golf Club and Las Chapas beach, is quieter and less internationally saturated than the areas around Puerto Banús. Properties here include a mix of apartments and detached villas, and the atmosphere is noticeably more relaxed. It is further from central services, which makes a car even more essential, but for retirees prioritising calm and greenery it is worth considering.

San Pedro de Alcántara — the most Spanish option

San Pedro genuinely feels like a Spanish town rather than an international enclave. If you want to shop at local markets, have coffee at a bar where you are the only foreigner, and integrate into Spanish daily life rather than an expat bubble, San Pedro is your best option in the Marbella municipality. It is also meaningfully cheaper — rents typically run 20–30% lower than equivalent properties in central Marbella. The beach (Playa de San Pedro) is long, uncrowded outside summer, and perfectly pleasant.

The Non-Lucrative Visa: What Marbella Retirees Need to Know

For British, American, Canadian, Australian, South African, and other non-EU nationals, the Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) is the correct route to long-term residence in Marbella. It is designed for people who wish to live in Spain without taking employment — funded by pensions, investments, rental income from abroad, or savings.

The key requirements for 2026:

  • Income of approximately €28,800 per year (around €2,400 per month) for a single applicant — demonstrated through bank statements, pension letters, or a combination
  • An additional approximately €7,200 per year for each additional dependent (spouse, child)
  • Private health insurance with comprehensive cover, no exclusions, no co-payments, and valid throughout Spain
  • Clean criminal record certificate from all countries of residence in the past five years, with apostille
  • Medical certificate confirming no notifiable infectious disease
  • Proof of accommodation in Spain (rental contract or property deeds)
  • Valid passport with at least one year of remaining validity

The application is made at your Spanish consulate before you move. Processing typically takes 4–8 weeks, though times vary by consulate. The visa is initially granted for one year, then renewable for two-year periods. After five years of continuous legal residence, you become eligible for long-term EU residency. After ten years, Spanish nationality is possible for some nationalities (British and American nationals face a longer path than others).

Important: The NLV requires you to spend the majority of your time in Spain — specifically, you cannot be absent for more than six consecutive months per year, or more than ten months in total over the two-year renewal period. This is a genuine residency requirement, not a formality. Many retirees who split time between Spain and the UK are caught out by this on renewal.

Healthcare for Retirees: Private Cover in Marbella

Private healthcare in Marbella is excellent and well-suited to the needs of international retirees. The flagship facility is Hospital Vithas Xanit Internacional in Benalmádena — a high-quality private hospital with English-speaking consultants, emergency services, diagnostic imaging, cardiology, orthopaedics, and oncology. Most major international health insurers are accepted here, and the hospital is experienced with the specific needs of non-Spanish patients navigating the system for the first time.

Within Marbella itself, Hospital Costa del Sol (public) provides emergency and general services for residents. There are also numerous private clinics and GP practices in the town that cater specifically to the international community, offering English-language consultations for everything from routine check-ups to specialist referrals.

British retirees who receive a UK State Pension should check whether they are eligible to register an S1 form with the Spanish health system. If eligible, this can provide access to Spain's public healthcare system from arrival — significantly reducing insurance costs. Speak to a specialist before assuming eligibility.

For everyone else, private health insurance is both a visa requirement and a practical necessity. Comprehensive policies covering hospital treatment, specialists, and GP access typically run:

  • Age 60–65: €100–€200 per month
  • Age 65–70: €150–€300 per month
  • Age 70–75: €220–€400 per month

These figures are approximate and vary significantly by provider, health history, and cover level. Spanish insurers (Sanitas, Adeslas, Asisa, DKV) and international providers (AXA, Cigna, Allianz Care) all operate on the Costa del Sol.

Golf and the Outdoor Lifestyle

It would be dishonest to discuss Marbella retirement without giving the golf its proper weight. The Costa del Sol is one of the world's most concentrated golf destinations — over 70 courses within a comfortable drive of Marbella, ranging from village municipal courses to championship venues like Valderrama (in Sotogrande, about an hour west) and Real Club de Golf de Las Brisas in Nueva Andalucía.

For retirees who play golf, this is transformative. You can play year-round — even in January, you will rarely encounter weather that stops play. Annual memberships at respected clubs in the area start from around €2,000–€4,000 per year, significantly less than equivalent courses in the UK or Ireland. Pay-and-play green fees on weekdays at many courses run €40–€80.

Beyond golf, the outdoor lifestyle is rich. The Serranía de Ronda provides excellent hiking. The seafront promenades from Marbella to Puerto Banús and beyond are ideal for walking and cycling. Tennis clubs are plentiful. The sea stays warm enough to swim comfortably from May through October. For active retirees, Marbella provides a consistently outdoor existence that is difficult to replicate in northern Europe.

The Expat Community for Retirees

Marbella's retired expat community is large, well-organised, and welcoming to newcomers. The British Society of Marbella, the International Women's Club, numerous golf societies and charity committees, and various social clubs provide structured entry points for people who have just arrived and are building a new social life from scratch.

This matters more than it might seem. One of the biggest challenges in early retirement abroad is social isolation — particularly in the first year when you do not yet know anyone locally and the daily structure of working life has gone. Marbella's infrastructure around this problem is better than most destinations, with a critical mass of English speakers who have been through the same transition and generally understand what newcomers are navigating.

There are also active online communities (Facebook groups, WhatsApp networks) covering practical topics: finding a doctor, Spanish lessons, car registration, recommended tradespeople. The collective knowledge of a large expat community is a genuinely useful resource that smaller destinations cannot provide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money do I need to retire in Marbella?
A comfortable retirement in Marbella typically requires €2,000–€2,800 per month for a single person, covering rent, food, utilities, private health insurance, and leisure. Couples sharing costs can live comfortably on €3,000–€4,000 per month. The Non-Lucrative Visa requires you to demonstrate an annual income of approximately €28,800 for a single applicant (2026 figures), with an additional €7,200 per dependent.
Can I retire to Marbella from the UK on a Non-Lucrative Visa?
Yes. British nationals are among the most common applicants for Spain's Non-Lucrative Visa for Marbella. You apply through the Spanish consulate in the UK before moving. You will need to demonstrate sufficient income (around €2,400 per month for a single applicant), hold comprehensive private health insurance, and provide a clean criminal record certificate with apostille.
What is the best area in Marbella for retirees?
The Old Town is ideal for retirees wanting walkability and Spanish character. Nueva Andalucía offers excellent value and proximity to golf. The east Marbella/Santa Clara area is quieter and more residential. San Pedro de Alcántara appeals to those wanting a genuinely Spanish daily life at a lower price point.
Is private healthcare in Marbella good enough for retirees?
Yes. Private healthcare on the Costa del Sol — particularly at Hospital Vithas Xanit Internacional in Benalmádena — is of a high standard with English-speaking staff experienced with international patients. Most non-EU retirees hold private health insurance as a visa requirement. Comprehensive cover typically costs €150–€350 per month depending on age and provider.
Is Marbella more expensive to retire to than other parts of Spain?
Yes — Marbella is more expensive than Seville, Alicante, Valencia, or Malaga city. Rents are higher and the general cost of living is elevated compared to most of Andalusia. However, the lifestyle payoff — 320 days of sunshine, world-class golf, excellent healthcare, a well-organised international community — is real and significant.
Can I play golf in Marbella year-round?
Yes — the Costa del Sol has over 70 golf courses within a 30-minute drive of Marbella. The climate means you can play comfortably in every month of the year, with mild winters keeping the courses in good condition. Annual golf memberships are widely available and significantly cheaper than equivalent courses in the UK or Ireland.
