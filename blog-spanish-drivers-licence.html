Exchanging Your Foreign Driving Licence for a Spanish One

Whether you hold a UK, EU, US, Canadian, or Australian licence, Spain has different rules for each. Here is what you need to know — including when you must exchange, what documents you need, and who has to sit a test.

Once you become a resident in Spain, the clock starts ticking on your right to drive on your home-country licence. The rules vary significantly depending on where your licence was issued — and the consequences of missing the deadline can be frustrating and costly. This guide cuts through the confusion and tells you clearly what applies to your situation.

UK Licence Holders: The 2023 Agreement

After a difficult few years post-Brexit, during which UK residents in Spain faced considerable uncertainty about their driving entitlement, the UK and Spanish governments signed a bilateral recognition agreement in March 2023. This agreement, published in Spain's official state bulletin (BOE), restored the right of UK licence holders to exchange their British licence for a Spanish one without sitting a driving test.

The practical implications for UK expats are as follows:

  • You can drive in Spain on your UK licence for six months from the date you obtain your Spanish residency (your NIE and TIE card).
  • Within that six-month window, you must present yourself at your provincial traffic headquarters (Jefatura Provincial de Tráfico, or DGT office) to make the exchange.
  • The exchange is administrative — no theory test or practical driving assessment is required.
  • Your UK licence is surrendered and sent to the DVLA in the UK. You receive a Spanish licence in return, which is then valid for ten years before renewal.
  • The agreement applies both to people who were already resident in Spain before the Brexit transition period ended and to those who move to Spain now.

Important: International Driving Permits are not an alternative for UK residents. Once you are legally resident in Spain, an IDP does not extend your driving entitlement beyond the six-month window.

EU and EEA Licence Holders

If you hold a licence issued by an EU or EEA member state, you can drive in Spain on that licence without restriction for the first two years of residency. You are, however, required to register with the DGT's Central Register of Drivers within six months of establishing residency — this is a registration requirement, not an exchange, and does not change your licence.

After two years, your options are:

  • Renew your licence in your country of issue and continue driving in Spain on the renewed EU licence.
  • Exchange for a Spanish licence — again, no test required for EU/EEA holders. Once exchanged, the Spanish DGT licence is renewed in Spain every ten years.

EU licences are mutually recognised across member states, so the exchange is straightforward and bureaucratic rather than technical. The key point is that failing to exchange or renew after two years means you technically cannot drive legally in Spain until you regularise your situation.

Non-EU Licence Holders: Countries with Exchange Agreements

Spain has bilateral driving licence exchange agreements with a number of countries outside the EU and EEA. Nationals of these countries can exchange their foreign licence for a Spanish one without taking a driving test, subject to the usual administrative process. The list includes Algeria, Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Japan, South Korea, Morocco, Peru, the Philippines, Brazil, El Salvador, Serbia, Tunisia, and Turkey, among others.

If your country is on this list, the process mirrors the UK and EU exchange: present yourself at the DGT with the required documents, surrender your foreign licence, and receive a Spanish one in exchange.

US, Canadian, and Australian Licence Holders: The Test Route

As of 2026, the United States, Canada, and Australia do not have driving licence exchange agreements with Spain. Residents from these countries who want a Spanish driving licence must pass a full Spanish driving test — both the theory component and the practical road test.

In the meantime, they can drive legally in Spain on their home-country licence for six months from the date they take up legal residency. After that period, they must have passed (or be in the process of sitting) the Spanish test.

A few practical points for this group:

  • The Spanish theory test is available in English at most test centres, though availability varies by location. Some centres also allow you to take the practical test with an English-speaking examiner.
  • You must study and sit the theory test at a recognised autoescuela (driving school), which provides the test booking system and preparation materials.
  • The practical test is conducted in a Spanish-registered vehicle provided by the autoescuela.
  • An International Driving Permit (IDP) may be used as a translation document alongside your foreign licence during the initial six-month period, but it is not a substitute for a Spanish licence once that period expires.

The Exchange Process: Documents You Will Need

Regardless of your nationality, if you are eligible to exchange (rather than sit a test), the standard document set required at the DGT is:

  • Completed application form (available at the DGT office)
  • Your valid passport or national identity card
  • Your current, valid foreign driving licence
  • Proof of legal residency in Spain — your TIE card or padrón certificate
  • Two recent passport-sized photographs (colour, white background)
  • A medical certificate (informe de aptitud psicofísica) from an authorised Spanish driving medical centre (centro médico de conductores). This is a brief assessment covering vision, reaction time, and basic physical fitness — not a full health check-up. These centres are found in most towns and appointments are usually available within a few days.
  • A signed declaration that you do not hold a driving licence issued by any other country
  • A signed declaration that your licence has not been suspended or cancelled

You will also pay a small administrative fee at the DGT. Your original foreign licence is retained and typically forwarded to the relevant authority in your home country — the DVLA in the UK, the DMV in the US, or the equivalent body elsewhere.

Where to Go: DGT Offices

Driving licence exchanges and applications are handled by the provincial DGT (Dirección General de Tráfico) offices. You can find your nearest office on the DGT website. In larger cities such as Madrid, Barcelona, and Seville, demand is high and appointments may need to be booked several weeks in advance. In smaller provincial capitals, waiting times are often shorter.

Some procedures can be initiated online through the DGT's electronic portal if you hold a Spanish digital certificate (certificado digital) or have registered with the Cl@ve system, though the medical certificate and photograph submission typically still require an in-person visit at some stage.

After the Exchange

Once you hold a Spanish driving licence, you are subject to Spain's penalty point system. New residents start with a clean slate of 12 points (long-term residents and young drivers start with different allocations). Serious traffic offences reduce your points; careful driving over a period of time can restore them. The minimum age to drive in Spain is 18 for category B (standard car) licences.

Spain drives on the right, and the vast majority of vehicles have manual gearboxes. If your home-country licence carries an automatic-only restriction, your Spanish licence will reflect that restriction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive in Spain with a UK licence after Brexit?
Yes, but only for a limited period once you become a resident. Following the bilateral agreement signed in 2023 between the UK and Spanish governments, UK licence holders who become resident in Spain can exchange their British licence for a Spanish one without sitting a driving test. You have six months from obtaining your residency to make the exchange. After that window closes, you cannot legally drive until the exchange is complete.
Do EU licence holders need to exchange for a Spanish licence?
EU and EEA licence holders can drive in Spain on their existing licence for the first two years of residency without exchanging. After two years, they must either renew their EU licence in their country of issue or exchange it for a Spanish licence. Once exchanged, the Spanish licence is renewed every ten years. They must also register with the provincial traffic headquarters (DGT) within six months of taking up residency in Spain.
Do US, Canadian, or Australian residents need to take a Spanish driving test?
It depends. Spain has exchange agreements with a number of countries that allow direct licence swaps without a test. The US, Canada, and Australia are not on this list as of 2026, which means residents from those countries must pass a full Spanish driving test (both theory and practical) to obtain a Spanish licence. They can drive on their home licence for the first six months after taking up residency, after which they need either a Spanish licence or must rely on their test booking.
What documents do I need to exchange my licence for a Spanish one?
The standard documents required at the DGT are: a completed application form; your valid passport or national identity card; your current foreign driving licence; proof of residency in Spain (padrón certificate or TIE card); two recent passport-sized photographs; a medical certificate of fitness to drive from an authorised Spanish centro médico; a declaration that you do not hold a driving licence in any other country; and a declaration that your licence has not been suspended or revoked. Your original foreign licence will be taken from you and surrendered to your home country's authority.
What happens if I drive without exchanging my licence after the deadline?
Driving with an expired entitlement — whether your home-country licence is no longer valid in Spain or you have missed the exchange deadline — is a traffic offence. The standard fine is €200. Beyond the financial penalty, if you are involved in an accident while technically not authorised to drive, your insurance cover may be compromised.
Can I use an International Driving Permit in Spain?
Tourists and short-term visitors from non-EU countries can use an International Driving Permit (IDP) alongside their home-country licence. However, an IDP is only valid for one year and must be obtained in your home country before you arrive. Once you become a resident of Spain, an IDP is no longer a valid substitute — residents must hold a Spanish licence or go through the exchange or test process.
