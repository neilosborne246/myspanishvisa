Healthcare in Spain for Expats: Public vs Private, Visa Rules and Costs 2026

Spain has one of the world's best-ranked healthcare systems. But how you access it — and what you need before you arrive — depends entirely on your visa type and residency status. Here's what you need to know.

Healthcare is one of the most important practical considerations when moving to any new country, and Spain is one of the better destinations in the world on this front. The country has a universal public health system (Sistema Nacional de Salud, or SNS) that is consistently ranked in the top 10 globally. Private healthcare is also well-developed, widely available, and significantly cheaper than equivalent private cover in the US, UK, or Australia.

The key thing to understand as an incoming expat is this: your access to each system depends on your visa category, your employment or tax status, and how long you have been resident in Spain. This guide cuts through the complexity to give you a clear picture.

Important: Private health insurance is a mandatory requirement for the Non-Lucrative Visa, Digital Nomad Visa, and Student Visa. Without a compliant policy, your visa application will be refused. The policy must have no exclusions and no co-payments (copagos) — a requirement that rules out many budget policies.

Spain's Public Healthcare System

The Spanish public health system is decentralised — it is administered region by region (comunidad autónoma), which means there are some variations in service quality and waiting times between regions. The national framework, however, provides a consistently high standard of care that is funded through social security contributions and general taxation.

What the public system covers when you are eligible:

  • GP (médico de cabecera) consultations — free at point of use
  • Hospital treatment, surgery, and emergency care — free
  • Specialist referrals — free, though waiting times vary
  • Maternity and paediatric care — free
  • Prescriptions — subsidised at 10–60% of cost depending on your income and status
  • Mental health services — available but variable by region

The public system does not cover dental care beyond emergency extractions, routine eye tests, or glasses. These are private expenses in Spain for the vast majority of residents.

Waiting times in the public system

For GP appointments and emergency care, waiting times are generally short. For specialist appointments and elective surgery, waiting times can be significant — measured in months rather than weeks in some regions and for some conditions. This is the primary reason many longer-term expats choose to hold private insurance even after becoming eligible for the public system.

Who Can Access the Spanish Public System?

Access to the Spanish public health system depends on your status:

EU citizens

EU citizens living in Spain as residents have full access to the public system on the same basis as Spanish nationals. If you are a resident and contributing to Spanish social security through employment or self-employment, you receive an individual health card (Tarjeta Sanitaria Individual, TSI) and full coverage from registration.

British nationals post-Brexit

British nationals are treated as non-EU third-country nationals for immigration and healthcare purposes. However, there is an important exception: British retirees who are receiving a UK State Pension and move to Spain on an NLV may be able to register their S1 form with the Spanish public health system. The S1 (formerly E121) is a document issued by the UK Department for Work and Pensions that transfers healthcare entitlement to Spain, allowing the holder to access public healthcare as if they were a Spanish resident. If you are in this situation, it is worth pursuing this route — it significantly reduces your ongoing insurance costs and gives access to the full public system.

Non-EU residents on NLV, DNV, or Student Visa

During your initial visa period, you are required to hold private health insurance. You cannot access the public system as of right. After spending sufficient time in Spain and becoming a formal tax resident with social security contributions, pathways to the public system open up — either through employment (which creates automatic entitlement), through the Convenio Especial, or through individual regional health authority arrangements.

Private Health Insurance: The Visa Requirement in Detail

For the Non-Lucrative Visa, Digital Nomad Visa, and Student Visa, the health insurance you purchase to satisfy the visa requirement must meet specific criteria. Getting this wrong is a common and costly mistake.

What the visa policy must have

  • Full medical coverage in Spain with no territorial exclusions
  • No co-payments (copagos) — the insurer covers costs fully at point of use
  • No pre-existing condition exclusions that create gaps in basic care
  • Coverage for the full duration of the visa period (minimum one year)
  • Issued by an insurer authorised to operate in Spain
  • A certificate of coverage (certificado de cobertura) that can be presented with the visa application

What will not be accepted

  • Travel insurance — even comprehensive annual policies
  • Policies with co-payments, even small ones
  • Policies from insurers not authorised in Spain
  • EHIC cards (now the GHIC for British nationals) — these cover temporary visits only

Recommended Spanish private insurers

Several Spanish health insurance companies are well-established among the expat community and offer policies that clearly meet visa requirements. The most commonly used are:

  • Sanitas — large network, strong English-language support, widely accepted for visa applications
  • Adeslas — good nationwide network, competitive pricing, clear visa-compliant policy tiers
  • Asisa — strong in certain regions, well-regarded quality of care
  • AXA Health Spain — popular with British expats, strong English-language communication
  • DKV Seguros — increasingly popular, good digital tools

What Does Private Health Insurance Cost in Spain?

Private health insurance in Spain is considerably cheaper than equivalent coverage in the US, UK, or Australia. Here are realistic 2026 estimates by age for a policy with no co-payments (visa-compliant):

  • Age 30–39: approximately €55–€90 per month
  • Age 40–49: approximately €75–€120 per month
  • Age 50–59: approximately €100–€170 per month
  • Age 60–65: approximately €150–€250 per month
  • Age 65–70: approximately €180–€320 per month

Family policies and couples policies attract discounts compared to individual rates combined. Adding dental coverage adds €15–€35 per month typically.

For context, a 60-year-old paying $1,000+ per month for private health insurance in the US would pay approximately €180–€220 per month in Spain for equivalent or better coverage.

The Convenio Especial: An Intermediate Route

Once you have been a legal resident in Spain for at least 12 months and are not otherwise entitled to the public health system, you can access it through the Convenio Especial — a voluntary contribution scheme that grants full public system access in return for a monthly payment.

The 2026 Convenio Especial monthly rates are approximately:

  • Under 65 years: €60 per month
  • 65 and over: €157 per month

The Convenio Especial does not satisfy the private insurance requirement for your visa initial application — you still need private cover for that. But once your initial residency permit is established and you wish to transition to public healthcare access, the Convenio Especial can be an affordable route, particularly for those under 65.

Healthcare on Different Visa Types: Summary

Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV)

Private health insurance is mandatory for the visa application and must be maintained throughout your permit period. It must have no co-payments and no territorial exclusions. British NLV holders receiving a UK State Pension should investigate the S1 form route to public healthcare access alongside their private policy. After five years of continuous legal residence, alternative pathways to public system access may become available.

Digital Nomad Visa (DNV)

Private health insurance is mandatory for the initial visa application on the same terms as the NLV. However, if your employment income is subject to Spanish social security contributions — which can apply in some DNV situations depending on your employment structure — you may become entitled to public healthcare relatively quickly. This depends on the specific contractual and tax structure of your work. A tax adviser familiar with the DNV is worth consulting.

Student Visa

Private health insurance is mandatory. Student policies specifically designed for the visa are available from most Spanish insurers and are typically the cheapest category of compliant policy — often €40–€70 per month for students under 30. The insurer must still be Spain-authorised and the policy must meet the no co-payment standard.

Practical Tips for Expats

  • Buy your health insurance before applying for your visa — you need the certificate for the application
  • Choose an insurer with a good English-language support line, particularly when first settling in
  • Register your address (empadronamiento) with your local town hall promptly after arrival — this is often required for accessing public health services and registering with a local health centre
  • Get a Spanish health card (TSI) as soon as you are entitled to the public system — this card is what you present at clinics and hospitals
  • Private dental and optical care are routine costs in Spain for almost all expats — budget accordingly
  • Pharmacies (farmacias) in Spain are well-stocked and pharmacists are skilled and helpful for minor conditions — they are often a useful first stop before a GP visit

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need private health insurance for a Spanish visa?
Yes — private health insurance is a mandatory requirement for the Non-Lucrative Visa, Digital Nomad Visa, and Student Visa. The policy must provide full coverage in Spain, with no exclusions and no co-payments (copagos). It must be from an insurer authorised to operate in Spain, and must be valid for the full duration of your initial visa period.
Can I access Spain's public healthcare system as an expat?
It depends on your situation. EU citizens with an EHIC card can access public healthcare on the same basis as Spanish citizens during temporary visits. British retirees receiving a UK State Pension can often register their S1 form to access public healthcare from arrival. Non-EU residents on an NLV or DNV are required to use private insurance initially; access to the public system typically becomes available after becoming a tax resident and accumulating social security contributions, or through the Convenio Especial arrangement.
How much does private health insurance cost in Spain?
Costs vary by age, coverage level, and insurer. A healthy 40-year-old can expect €75–€120 per month; a 55-year-old, €100–€170 per month; a 65-year-old, €150–€280 per month. Pre-existing conditions may result in exclusions or higher premiums. Popular Spanish insurers used by expats include Sanitas, Adeslas, Asisa, and AXA Health Spain.
What does 'no co-payments' mean for visa insurance requirements?
A co-payment (copago) is an amount you pay each time you visit a doctor or use a medical service, even with insurance. Spanish visa regulations require that the health insurance policy used for your visa application has no co-payments — meaning the insurer covers costs fully at point of use without you contributing a share each time. Policies with co-payments are cheaper but will not satisfy the visa requirement.
What is the Convenio Especial and can I use it to access public healthcare?
The Convenio Especial is a voluntary contribution scheme that allows legal residents who are not otherwise entitled to public healthcare to join Spain's public health system by paying a monthly fee. In 2026, this is approximately €60 per month for those under 65, and €157 per month for those 65 or over. It provides access to the full public system but is not a substitute for the private insurance required for your initial visa.
Is the quality of healthcare in Spain good?
Spain's healthcare system is consistently ranked among the best in the world. Public hospitals in major cities are modern and well-equipped. Private hospitals and clinics in expat-heavy areas frequently have English-speaking medical staff and shorter waiting times than the public sector for non-urgent care.
