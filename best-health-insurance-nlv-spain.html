What You Need to Know About NLV Health Insurance

Spain's Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) is a residence visa for people who will live in Spain without working, such as retirees and people living on savings or passive income. But there's a critical requirement many applicants overlook: you must have private health insurance to qualify.

ℹ

Key Point: Spain's immigration authorities require proof of private health insurance at the time of your visa application. This is not optional. You cannot use travel insurance or rely solely on Spain's public healthcare system for visa approval.

Why Health Insurance Matters for Your NLV Visa

The Spanish government introduced the health insurance requirement to ensure visa holders have adequate medical coverage and don't become a burden on the public healthcare system. Your insurance policy serves as proof of financial responsibility and shows authorities you're prepared for healthcare costs during your stay in Spain.

  • Visa approval requirement: Immigration will request proof of valid insurance covering the entire territory of Spain
  • No copayment mandate: Your policy must include "sin copago" (no copayment) — this means no per-visit or per-prescription charges
  • No waiting periods: Cover must apply from the policy start date, without waiting periods (sin carencias)
  • Ongoing obligation: You must maintain continuous coverage throughout your residency in Spain

NLV Insurance vs. Travel Insurance

Many people mistakenly think travel insurance covers the NLV visa requirement. It doesn't. Travel insurance is short-term, limited coverage designed for vacations. Immigration authorities specifically reject travel policies for NLV applications.

What you need is private health insurance — a comprehensive annual policy issued by an insurer authorised to operate in Spain that covers you year-round.

Private Cover Stays in Place

Non-Lucrative Visa holders are not automatically entitled to Spain’s public health system, and registering on the padrón (empadronamiento) does not change that. Plan to keep your private policy running without a break: you show continuous cover again at every renewal.

What to Look For in an NLV Insurance Plan

Non-Negotiable Features

  • Sin Copago (No Copayment): Zero per-visit charges and zero prescription costs. This is mandatory for visa approval.
  • National Coverage: Must cover all of Spain, not just your region. Immigration specifically checks this.
  • No Waiting Periods: Cover from the start date, with no waiting periods (sin carencias).
  • Annual Coverage: Starting when you arrive and covering at least the first year, not travel insurance or short-term plans.
  • Authorised Insurer: Authorised to operate in Spain (listed with the DGSFP, Spain’s insurance supervisor).

Quality Factors to Compare

  • Network Size: Larger networks = easier specialist access and shorter wait times
  • Hospital Coverage: Does it cover private hospitals in your region? Which ones?
  • Emergency Coverage: Must include 24/7 emergency care and ambulance services
  • Outpatient Care: GP visits, specialist consultations, diagnostics (X-rays, blood tests)
  • Prescription Drugs: Full coverage for medications or percentage reimbursement?
  • Pre-existing Conditions: Is your condition accepted, excluded or priced in?

What Affects the Price

Premiums depend mainly on each applicant’s age, where in Spain you will live and your medical history, and they usually rise with age. Published price bands go out of date quickly, so a personalised quote is the only reliable figure for your budget.

Navigating Pre-Existing Conditions

⚠

Important: Pre-existing conditions affect your insurability and premiums. Be honest on applications — insurers review medical history, and dishonesty voids your coverage.

Typical Outcomes by Condition Severity

  • Minor conditions: Accepted with standard or slightly higher premium (allergies, treated hypertension, controlled asthma)
  • Moderate conditions: Often accepted, sometimes with an exclusion for that condition or a higher premium (diabetes, arthritis, back problems)
  • Serious conditions: May be denied, or accepted with substantial exclusions (cancer under active treatment, uncontrolled heart disease, recent stroke)

Strategies for Pre-Existing Conditions

  • Apply early: Get quotes before your official visa application. Don't wait until the last minute.
  • Disclose fully: Answer the health questionnaire completely, so the offer you receive is the one that applies.
  • Ask for a personalised assessment: Spanish Health Insurance, an exclusive Sanitas insurance agent, can explain how Sanitas would assess your case before you apply.
  • Gather documentation: Medical reports, recent test results, and treatment history help your case.
  • Check the certificate: A visa policy should have no waiting periods, so ask how any exclusion will appear before you buy.

When an Insurer Denies Coverage

If an insurer rejects your application due to pre-existing conditions, don't panic. This doesn't mean you're uninsurable. Your options:

  1. Ask about exclusions. A condition may be excluded rather than declined, so check how any exclusion will appear on your certificate.
  2. Get specialist help early. Spanish Health Insurance, an exclusive Sanitas insurance agent, can talk you through the Sanitas assessment before you apply (see below). Acceptance is never guaranteed.
  3. Ask whether an exclusion is possible. Rather than declining, an insurer may accept you but exclude a specific condition from cover.
  4. Wait for clinical improvement. If you're in active treatment, applying once your condition is stable can change the outcome.
✓

Real-world outcome: Many applicants with serious pre-existing conditions (diabetes, hypertension, arthritis) can still be insured, sometimes with an exclusion or a higher premium. Don't assume rejection means the visa is off the table.

The No-Copay Requirement Explained

One of the most misunderstood aspects of NLV insurance is the "sin copago" (no copayment) requirement. Let's clarify what this means and why it matters.

What "Sin Copago" Actually Means

In Spain's healthcare system, a "copago" is a per-visit charge or co-payment. For example, you might pay €3 for a doctor visit, €5 for a prescription, or €10 for an emergency room visit — amounts the patient must cover out-of-pocket.

Sin copago means: Your insurance covers 100% of these co-payments. You pay only your premium — no additional charges when you visit a doctor, fill a prescription, or use a hospital.

Why Sin Copago Matters for the Visa

Consulates ask for it, and it has two practical effects:

  • Financial proof: It proves you can afford healthcare without draining your bank account on every visit. The visa requires a minimum income, and sin copago ensures you won't deplete savings on medical costs.
  • Healthcare access: Without sin copago, some older applicants might avoid seeking treatment due to cost, straining public emergency services. Sin copago ensures you'll seek preventive care early.

Standard Spanish Insurance vs. NLV Requirements

Standard Spanish private insurance (for Spanish citizens and residents) often includes copagos of €3–10 per visit. This is acceptable for Spanish insurance purposes but NOT acceptable for NLV visa approval.

Make sure your quote explicitly states "sin copago" or "zero copayment." If you're unsure, ask the insurer directly: "Is this policy sin copago for all covered services?"

ℹ

Key distinction: "Sin copago" applies to normal medical services (doctor visits, prescriptions, hospital stays). It does NOT typically cover cosmetic procedures, dental work, or experimental treatments — but these aren't required for the visa anyway.

Can You Switch Insurance Providers After Visa Approval?

Yes, you can switch health insurance providers after receiving your NLV visa. However, there are important considerations:

When Switching Makes Sense

  • Better rate elsewhere: You found a cheaper provider offering similar coverage
  • Network issues: Current provider's doctor network is weak in your region
  • Service problems: Customer service or claims processing has been poor
  • Renewal time: When your annual policy renews (best time to switch without penalties)

Considerations Before Switching

Your health is assessed again: A new insurer reviews your medical history and may exclude conditions the old policy covered. This matters most if you're mid-treatment.

Price increases at renewal: Some insurers offer lower introductory rates, then increase significantly at renewal. Comparing at renewal can keep costs down, but weigh any new exclusions.

Health changes: If your health deteriorated since your first policy, the new insurer may charge more or impose exclusions. Timing your switch during good health is strategic.

Best Practices for Switching

  1. Switch during contract renewal periods (typically anniversary dates) to avoid termination penalties.
  2. Get quotes 2–3 months before renewal to compare rates and time your decision.
  3. If you have pre-existing conditions, check how the new insurer treats them before signing, and keep no waiting periods on the new certificate.
  4. Keep proof of continuous coverage (letter from old insurer) to avoid gaps that could affect visa renewal.
  5. Once switched, notify your visa renewal contact (lawyer or immigration agency) of the new policy. Immigration wants proof of continuous coverage.
ℹ

Visa renewal tip: When you renew your NLV residence, you'll need to show proof of continuous health insurance. Switching to a significantly cheaper provider is acceptable as long as coverage remains uninterrupted.
