## 2. Target audience

### Segment 1 — Retirees (NLV primary)
Typically 55–70, UK/Ireland/South Africa/Australia. Have savings or pension income. Want to retire to Spain but find the process intimidating. They research extensively before acting, read forums, and often ask questions in Facebook groups before committing. Pain points: "Am I eligible?" "What income do I need?" "What if I get rejected?"

### Segment 2 — Remote workers and digital nomads (DNV primary)
Typically 28–45, UK/US/Canada. Have a job or clients outside Spain. Want to live in Spain legally. Tech-savvy, will search Google and scroll Instagram. Pain points: "Can I really live in Spain and work for my UK company?" "What counts as proof of income?" "Is the DNV worth it vs NLV?"

### Segment 3 — Families relocating
Couples or families, often 35–55. Moving for lifestyle, children's education, or retirement. Higher complexity needs (multiple applicants, school enrolment, healthcare). Pain points: "Can my partner apply with me?" "What happens with my kids?" "How long does it all take?"

### Segment 4 — Property buyers converting to residency
People who bought or are buying in Spain and now need a visa or residency. Often confused about the link between property ownership and visa eligibility. Pain points: "I own a property — does that help?" "Do I need to be a resident to live there?"

### Where they are online
- Facebook groups: British Expats in Spain, Americans in Spain, Move to Spain, specific regional groups (Costa del Sol, Valencia, Barcelona)
- Instagram: lifestyle content, expat accounts, Spain-focused creators
- Google: searching "how to get Spain visa", "non lucrative visa requirements 2026", "digital nomad visa Spain income"
- LinkedIn: DNV segment specifically (professionals, remote workers)

## 4. Channel strategy

### Channel 1 — SEO blog content (primary channel)

The single highest-leverage organic activity. A well-ranked blog post generates leads indefinitely.

**Why this fits:** All four audience segments search Google when they start researching. Ranking for intent-rich queries (e.g. "non lucrative visa Spain income requirements 2026") puts My Spanish Visa in front of people at the exact moment they need help.

**Content format:** Long-form guides (1,200–2,500 words), comparison posts, FAQ-format articles, checklist-style posts. Every post should have a clear CTA — either a lead form, a checklist download, or a link to book a consultation.

**Effort level:** High (requires consistent production)

**Priority post types:**
- Pillar guides: one comprehensive guide per visa type
- Supporting cluster posts: specific questions around each visa (income, documents, timeline, renewal, mistakes)
- Comparison posts: "NLV vs DNV — which is right for you?"
- Problem-solving posts: "Spain visa rejected — what to do next"
- Location posts where relevant: "Moving to Valencia / Barcelona / Andalusia"

---

### Channel 2 — Facebook groups (community lead generation)

**Why this fits:** Expat Facebook groups have thousands of active members asking visa questions daily. Being genuinely helpful in these communities builds brand recognition and drives traffic organically — without spending a penny.

**How to use this correctly:**
- Join the 10–15 most active English-speaking Spain expat groups
- Monitor daily for visa and residency questions
- Answer helpfully and thoroughly — don't drop a link immediately
- After providing value, mention the site naturally: "We cover this in detail at myspanishvisa.com if you want the full breakdown"
- Post original content occasionally (not every day) — helpful tips, myth-busting, "common questions we get asked" style posts
- Never spam. Groups that feel spammed will remove you.

**Best groups to target:**
- British Expats in Spain (and regional variants)
- Americans / Canadians / Australians moving to Spain
- Digital Nomads in Spain
- Retire to Spain
- Specific city/region groups (Costa del Sol, Valencia, Alicante, Madrid, Barcelona)
- Spain Visa Help groups

**Effort level:** Medium (daily 20–30 minutes of monitoring and responding)

### Channel 3 — Facebook page posts (SEO signal)

**Why this fits:** Google indexes public Facebook page posts. Posting keyword-rich, structured content on the My Spanish Visa Facebook page creates additional indexed content that can appear in search results — especially for long-tail queries where there's limited competition. Even with no followers, this content is being read and ranked by Google.

**How to use this:**
- Post 3–4 times per week on the Facebook page
- Write posts as mini-articles, not captions — 150–300 words, keyword-rich, structured
- Include a link back to the relevant blog post or lead form
- Topics should mirror the blog content calendar — each blog post should have a corresponding Facebook post
- Use headings and clear structure within the post text (Google reads this)
- Include the key keyword phrase naturally in the first two lines

**Effort level:** Low (repurpose and condense blog content)

**Why this fits:** Instagram is strong for lifestyle-adjacent topics and builds visual trust. Retirees and families often research on Instagram. DNV audience is active on Instagram and LinkedIn. DMs are a real lead generation channel — people ask questions in DMs before they fill in a form.

**Effort level:** Medium (3–5 posts per week, mix of formats)

### Channel 5 — LinkedIn (DNV audience)

**Why this fits:** The Digital Nomad Visa audience — remote workers, freelancers, contractors — is disproportionately active on LinkedIn. Posts about remote work + Spain residency perform well here.

## 9. The Facebook-as-SEO strategy (expanded)

This deserves its own note because it's a smart and underused tactic.

**How it works:** Google crawls and indexes public Facebook page posts. A well-written, keyword-rich post on the My Spanish Visa Facebook page will appear in Google search results — even without any followers or engagement on the post. This is additional indexed content at zero cost.

**How to maximise it:**
- Write Facebook posts as mini-articles, not social captions
- Lead with the target keyword phrase in the first sentence: "The Non-Lucrative Visa income requirement in 2026 is one of the most common questions we receive..."
- Include a clear answer and then link to the full blog post
- Post consistently (3–4x per week) — volume of indexed content matters
- Use the same keywords your blog is targeting, but in a more conversational format
- Treat each Facebook post as a supporting document for the corresponding blog post

**What to expect:** Don't expect the Facebook posts themselves to rank for competitive keywords. They supplement the blog by capturing long-tail queries, appearing in branded searches, and reinforcing the site's topical authority signal. It's a free SEO multiplier.
