Cheapest Cities to Live in Spain in 2026

Spain is already dramatically more affordable than the UK, US, or Canada. But within Spain, the price differences between cities are enormous. Here is where your money goes furthest.

Home / Blog / Cheapest Cities to Live in Spain in 2026

Spain's reputation for affordable living is well earned — but the gap between its cheapest and most expensive cities is wider than most people realise. Living in Barcelona or San Sebastian comfortably might cost you €2,500–€3,000 per month. In Lugo, Jaen, or Zamora, the same lifestyle costs €900–€1,200. That is a difference of €15,000–€25,000 per year.

For retirees on fixed incomes, remote workers trying to maximise savings, or anyone who simply wants to live well without overspending, choosing the right Spanish city can transform your financial picture. This guide covers the cities that consistently rank as Spain's most affordable, with honest figures on rents, monthly budgets, and what you actually get for your money.

One important note before we start: Spain's visa income requirements are set nationally, not locally. The Non-Lucrative Visa requires approximately €2,400 per month in passive income regardless of whether you live in Lugo or Marbella. But choosing a cheap city means that income goes far further — and your monthly surplus grows considerably.

Why Spain Remains Western Europe's Best Value

Even in its most expensive cities, Spain undercuts comparable destinations in France, Germany, the Netherlands, or the UK. The Spanish public sector keeps many everyday costs low: public transport fares, utility rates, and basic services are priced accessibly by European standards. The Mediterranean diet of fresh vegetables, legumes, fish, and olive oil is both healthy and inexpensive — you can eat superbly on €300–€400 per month in most Spanish cities.

Private health insurance — required for most non-EU visa holders — is also notably cheaper in Spain than in the UK or US, typically running €80–€180 per month for a healthy adult. And while Spain has no state pension provision for non-residents, access to excellent private healthcare at these prices is a significant financial advantage.

Lugo, Galicia: Spain's Most Affordable City Overall

Lugo consistently tops independent cost-of-living rankings as Spain's cheapest city for all-round daily expenses. The city is a genuine gem that most outsiders overlook: it is encircled by the best-preserved Roman walls in the world (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), has a lively university population that keeps the bar and restaurant scene active, and offers all the urban amenities you need — hospitals, supermarkets, transport links — at prices well below the national average.

Rent for a modern, centrally located one-bedroom apartment runs €400–€550 per month. A three-course lunch menu with wine (the menú del día) is typically €10–€12. Utilities for a two-person household average €80–€110 per month. A single person living reasonably comfortably in Lugo can manage on €800–€1,000 per month all-in.

The trade-off with Lugo is climate and location. Galicia is green because it rains — considerably more than the Mediterranean south. If year-round sunshine is a priority, Lugo is not your city. But for remote workers who want low costs, fast internet, safety, and an authentic Spanish lifestyle without tourist crowds, it is hard to beat.

Jaen, Andalusia: Cheapest Southern Sun

Jaen holds a distinctive position: it is the most affordable provincial capital in southern Spain, and it delivers the Andalusian sunshine that the north cannot match. The city sits at the heart of the world's largest olive oil producing region, surrounded by vast seas of olive trees, and its food culture reflects this — local produce is extraordinarily fresh and cheap.

Grocery bills for a single person eating well in Jaen run €200–€250 per month. Central one-bedroom rents sit at €450–€600. The city has all essential services — hospitals, shopping, public transport — and is just 90 minutes from Granada and two hours from Seville. The expat community is small compared to coastal towns, but growing, particularly among those who discover the city as an affordable base for exploring Andalusia.

Jaen has not been overhyped or heavily marketed to foreigners, which is both its challenge (fewer English-speaking services) and its appeal (genuinely authentic, uncrowded, and welcoming).

Zamora, Castile and Leon: Best for Property Buyers

Zamora is worth special attention for anyone considering buying rather than renting. Property prices per square metre in Zamora are among the very lowest in Spain — frequently cited around €640–€800/m² in 2026, compared to over €4,000/m² in central Barcelona. For a retiree or remote worker considering buying as a long-term investment, Zamora makes the arithmetic look very different.

The city has a well-preserved medieval old town, good road and rail connections to Madrid (around 2.5 hours by train), and a quiet, dignified atmosphere. It is not a major expat hub, so settling here requires Spanish language ability and a genuine interest in integrating with the local community. But for those who want that, Zamora offers exceptional value.

Caceres, Extremadura: UNESCO Heritage on a Tight Budget

Caceres is another UNESCO World Heritage city — its old town is extraordinarily well preserved, with medieval towers, palaces, and city walls that have been used as filming locations for Game of Thrones. The city has a strong university, a laid-back pace of life, and a rental market that remains very accessible: €450–€600 per month for a central one-bedroom.

Monthly living costs for a single person in Caceres sit around €900–€1,100. Extremadura as a region has lower prices than most of Spain — food, services, and housing all cheaper than the national average. The closest large international airports are Seville (3 hours) or Madrid (3.5 hours), which is a consideration for frequent travellers.

Almeria, Andalusia: Cheapest Mediterranean Coastline

If you want Mediterranean sea and sand at the lowest possible price, Almeria is the answer. The city sits at the eastern end of Andalusia, with a dramatic castle overlooking the sea and some of Spain's most unspoiled beaches within easy reach. It is notably drier and sunnier than the rest of Spain — more sunshine hours per year than any other city in Europe.

One-bedroom rents in Almeria city centre run €550–€700 per month. Monthly living costs for a single person average €1,000–€1,250. The surrounding province of Almeria also has some of the most affordable coastal towns for property purchase — places like Vera or Mojacar where retired expats from Northern Europe have settled in significant numbers.

Granada, Andalusia: Culture and Value for Expats

Granada sits at an interesting intersection: it is considerably more affordable than other Andalusian tourist cities like Seville or Malaga, yet it has one of Spain's most distinctive cultural identities, anchored by the Alhambra palace complex and a thriving flamenco scene. The city's large student population (the University of Granada is one of Spain's oldest) keeps prices low and social life active.

The famous Granada tapas tradition — where every drink comes with a free plate of food — genuinely reduces dining costs for residents. Central one-bedroom apartments run €550–€800 per month. The city has a growing number of foreign residents, particularly among remote workers and language teachers, and English-speaking services are more widely available than in smaller cities like Jaen or Zamora.

Murcia: Family-Friendly and Affordable

Murcia is often overlooked in favour of its Costa Blanca and Costa Calida neighbours, but it makes a compelling case as a base for families or retirees who want city infrastructure at low cost. As the capital of its own autonomous region, Murcia has a full range of hospitals, schools, shopping, and public services. The regional food reputation is excellent — Murcia is known as the orchard of Europe for its market gardening — and fresh produce is superb value.

Rent in Murcia city runs €600–€750 per month for a one-bedroom. Monthly living costs for a couple sit at around €1,800–€2,200 including rent, which compares very favourably with Madrid or Barcelona. The nearby Mar Menor lagoon offers calm, warm, shallow water ideal for families and those with mobility considerations.

The Visa Reality: Cheap City, National Requirements

Choosing an affordable city does not change the visa income requirements — those are set by the Spanish state and apply nationally. The Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) requires approximately €2,400 per month in passive income for a single applicant; the Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) requires approximately €2,850 per month in verifiable remote earnings.

What the cheap city choice changes is how much of that income you actually need to spend each month. If you live in Lugo on €900 per month, and your NLV requires you to demonstrate €2,400 in passive income, you are keeping €1,500 per month as surplus. In Barcelona on €2,800 per month in living costs, the same income leaves you with just €400 buffer — a very different financial position.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest city to live in Spain?
Lugo in Galicia consistently ranks as Spain's most affordable city overall, with one-bedroom rents starting around €400–€550 per month and a full monthly budget for a single person of €800–€1,000. Jaen, Zamora, and Caceres also rank among the cheapest cities in Spain for daily living costs.
Can I live in Spain on €1,000 a month?
Yes, in several inland and smaller cities in Spain. Cities like Lugo, Jaen, Zamora, and Caceres allow a single person to live comfortably on €900–€1,100 per month, covering rent, food, utilities, transport, and entertainment. Coastal cities and major metros cost more.
What is the cheapest place to live in Spain by the sea?
Almeria is widely considered Spain's most affordable Mediterranean coastal city, with monthly budgets for a single person around €1,000–€1,250 including rent. Torrevieja on the Costa Blanca is also very affordable, with one-bedroom apartments available from around €550 per month.
Do I still need to meet income requirements even if I live in a cheap city?
Yes. The income requirements for the Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) and Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) are set nationally, not based on where you live in Spain. The NLV requires approximately €2,400 per month for a single applicant regardless of whether you live in Lugo or Barcelona. The advantage of a cheap city is that your actual living costs will be far lower than the income threshold, leaving you with significant surplus each month.
Is Granada a good place to live cheaply in Spain?
Granada is one of the most popular affordable cities for expats in Spain, combining genuine cultural richness (the Alhambra, flamenco, Moorish heritage) with low living costs. The tradition of free tapas with drinks significantly reduces entertainment and dining budgets. Central one-bedroom apartments cost €550–€800 per month to rent.
Is it cheaper to live in Spain than Portugal?
Spain's cheapest cities are generally more affordable than comparable Portuguese cities. Lisbon and Porto have seen significant price increases in recent years and are now comparable to or more expensive than Spanish cities like Valencia or Seville. Spain's cheaper inland cities — Lugo, Jaen, Zamora — have no real equivalent in Portugal at equivalent price levels.
