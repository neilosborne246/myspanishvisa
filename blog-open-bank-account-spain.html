How to Open a Bank Account in Spain as a Foreigner (2026 Guide)

Whether you are arriving in Spain on a visa or buying property from abroad, having a Spanish bank account is essential. Here is everything you need to know — from the documents required to the best banks for English-speaking expats.

A Spanish bank account is not optional for expats — it is the financial foundation of your life in Spain. You need one to set up utility direct debits, receive salary payments, pay rent, buy property, and in many cases, satisfy the requirements of your visa application. The good news is that the process is straightforward once you understand the two distinct paths: resident and non-resident accounts.

Resident vs Non-Resident Bank Accounts: What Is the Difference?

Spanish banks offer two types of accounts for foreigners, and the distinction matters. A resident account (cuenta de residente) is a standard personal current account available to anyone who holds legal residency in Spain — meaning you have your TIE card or are registered on the Padrón. These accounts come with full features: credit cards, overdraft facilities, mortgages, and better interest rates.

A non-resident account (cuenta de no residente) is designed for people who own property in Spain or need Spanish banking facilities without living there full-time. These accounts are somewhat more restricted but still perfectly functional for most day-to-day needs. The key practical point: you can open a non-resident account before you arrive in Spain with your visa, then convert it to a resident account once you have your TIE card.

Documents Required to Open a Non-Resident Account

If you are not yet a resident in Spain, you will typically need the following to open a bank account:

  • Your valid passport (national ID card if you are an EU citizen)
  • Proof of your home address — a recent utility bill or bank statement, usually no older than three months
  • Proof of your employment or income status — a payslip, tax return, or government letter
  • A Non-Resident Certificate (Certificado de No Residencia) — required by some banks, obtained from a Spanish National Police station

Documents from abroad may need to be translated by a certified translator, though many international banks and branches in tourist areas will accept English-language documents directly. Confirm with your chosen bank before visiting.

Documents Required to Open a Resident Account

Once you have your Spanish residency, opening a full current account is much simpler. You will need:

  • Your valid passport or national identity card
  • Your NIE number (Número de Identidad de Extranjero) — this is mandatory
  • Your TIE card (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) if you have already collected it
  • Proof of your Spanish address — your rental contract, empadronamiento certificate, or a recent utility bill
  • Proof of income or employment status

Your NIE number is the single most important prerequisite. Without it, you cannot open a resident account, sign a rental contract, buy property, or register for utilities in your own name. If your visa application is underway, obtaining your NIE should be one of your first priorities on arrival.

Which Spanish Banks Are Best for Expats?

Spain has a well-developed banking sector, and most major banks have English-language online banking and mobile apps. Here are the most popular choices among the English-speaking expat community:

Sabadell

Sabadell is widely considered the most expat-friendly of the major Spanish banks, partly because it acquired the former TSB operations in the UK and has long experience dealing with British customers. Their English-language online portal and dedicated expat team make them a strong first choice for UK nationals in particular.

BBVA

BBVA offers an excellent digital banking app with full English support. Their online account opening process is one of the smoothest in Spain, and they operate a wide branch network across the country. Particularly popular with American expats due to BBVA's North American footprint.

CaixaBank

Spain's largest retail bank by customer numbers. CaixaBank's CaixaBankNow app is well-regarded, and their Hello! account is specifically designed for new arrivals. Branch availability is excellent, including in smaller towns and villages.

Santander

For UK and Australian expats, Santander's international presence can simplify transfers between your home country and Spain. Their branch network and digital banking are solid, though some expats report more varied experiences with English-language customer service in smaller branches.

Online Banks and Fintech Alternatives: Important Limitations

Revolut, Wise, and N26 are popular with digital nomads and short-term visitors — and for good reason. They are easy to set up, cheap for international transfers, and come with slick apps. However, for anyone planning to actually live in Spain, they come with critical limitations you need to understand.

Key limitation: Most Spanish utility providers (electricity, gas, internet), landlords requiring direct debit, and government agencies will only accept a Spanish IBAN — one that starts with ES. Revolut and N26 issue Lithuanian or German IBANs, which are frequently rejected. If you are buying property, the notary will require a Spanish bank transfer from a Spanish account.

Use fintech accounts as a supplement — great for spending abroad and cheap transfers home — but not as a replacement for a proper Spanish bank account.

Can You Open a Bank Account Remotely Before Moving to Spain?

Several banks now allow you to begin the account-opening process before you arrive in Spain. BBVA and Sabadell both offer online applications that can be started with a video identification call. However, for most banks you will still need to visit a branch at some point to complete the process, particularly to present physical documents and collect your card and PIN.

If you are buying property in Spain and need a Spanish account set up quickly, your solicitor or visa specialist can sometimes assist by making an appointment on your behalf or accompanying you to the bank to ensure smooth communication.

Step-by-Step: Opening Your Spanish Bank Account

  • Step 1: Obtain your NIE number if you are a resident, or gather your passport and proof of address if you are opening a non-resident account
  • Step 2: Research which bank suits your needs — consider branch locations, English support, and digital banking quality
  • Step 3: Book an appointment at your chosen branch (walk-ins are often possible but an appointment avoids waiting)
  • Step 4: Bring all required documents, including translated copies if needed
  • Step 5: Complete the application forms and identity verification — the bank may request a video call for online applications
  • Step 6: Your card will be issued — either immediately or by post within a few days
  • Step 7: Activate your online banking and set up your account preferences in English

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Arriving at the bank without your NIE number is the most common — and most avoidable — error. Banks cannot open a resident account without it, and you will simply be turned away. Make sure your NIE application is complete before booking your bank appointment.

Do not rely solely on a fintech account for your Spanish life. The inability to accept Spanish direct debits will cause real-world problems when your landlord, electricity provider, or local council requires a proper Spanish IBAN.

Do not assume all documents from home will be accepted without translation. While major branches in expat areas are often accommodating, smaller branches may insist on certified Spanish translations of foreign documents.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I open a bank account in Spain without an NIE number?
As a non-resident, you can open a non-resident bank account without an NIE number by presenting your passport and proof of address from your home country. However, once you become a resident, you will need your NIE to upgrade to a resident account and for most financial transactions in Spain.
Which Spanish banks are best for English-speaking expats?
Sabadell, BBVA, and CaixaBank are among the most popular choices for English-speaking expats. They have English-language online banking platforms and branches in tourist and expat areas where staff often speak English. Online alternatives like Wise or N26 can also complement your Spanish account for international transfers.
Do I need to be in Spain to open a bank account?
Some banks allow you to begin the account-opening process online, but you will typically need to visit a branch or video-verify your identity to complete the process. If you are buying property in Spain, you will need a Spanish IBAN, which requires a proper bank account — not just an e-money account like Revolut.
What is a non-resident certificate and do I need one?
A non-resident certificate (Certificado de No Residencia) is a document issued by a Spanish police station confirming that you do not live in Spain. Some traditional banks require this to open a non-resident account. You can obtain it by visiting a National Police station with your passport. Not all banks require it — check with your chosen bank beforehand.
Can I use Revolut or N26 instead of a Spanish bank account?
You can use fintech accounts for day-to-day spending, but they have real limitations in Spain. Many utility providers, landlords, and government agencies require a Spanish IBAN (starting with ES) to set up direct debits. If you are buying property, the notary will require a Spanish bank transfer. For expats planning to live in Spain, a full Spanish bank account is essential.
How long does it take to open a bank account in Spain?
With all documents ready, you can open a resident bank account in a single branch visit — usually 30 to 60 minutes. Non-resident accounts may take a few extra days if the bank needs to verify your documents. Online account openings typically take 1 to 3 business days for identity verification.
