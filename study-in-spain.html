Why Study in Spain?

Spain offers more than just education—it's a life-changing experience.

Quality of Education

Spanish universities consistently rank among Europe's best. Degrees are internationally recognised and employers worldwide value a Spanish education.

Affordability

Tuition and living costs are significantly lower than the UK, US, or Northern Europe—making Spain an excellent value proposition for international students.

Quality of Life

Mediterranean climate, vibrant culture, excellent food, and a relaxed pace of life. Spain consistently ranks high in quality-of-life indices.

Gateway to Europe

Schengen membership means you can travel freely across Europe during breaks. Direct flights connect Spain to most major cities.

Cultural Richness

Art, history, architecture, festivals—studying in Spain immerses you in one of Europe's most culturally dynamic countries.

Spanish Language

Spanish is the world's 4th most spoken language. Studying in Spain means learning or improving your Spanish in an immersive environment.

International Community

Spanish universities attract students from around the world. You'll build a truly global network while studying.

Post-Study Options

Work authorisation, residency pathways, and job seeker visas make it possible to transition from study to work and stay long-term.

Types of Study in Spain

Different pathways depending on your goals and timeline.

University Degrees

Bachelor's (Grado), master's (Máster), and doctoral (Doctorado) programmes. Many universities offer English-taught options. Duration: 3–4 years for bachelor's, 1–2 for master's.

Language Schools

Intensive Spanish courses for all levels. From short-term (2–4 weeks) to semester-long programmes. Taught by native instructors in small groups.

View guide →

Vocational Training

Professional courses and certifications (FP). Practical skills training in fields like hospitality, IT, healthcare, and skilled trades. 1–2 year programmes.

Semester Abroad

Exchange programmes (Erasmus+) allowing you to spend 1–2 semesters at a Spanish university while enrolled at your home institution.

Postgraduate Research

Research degrees and doctoral programmes. Ideal for academic careers. Often funded through scholarships and research grants.

Summer Schools

Short, intensive programmes (2–6 weeks) during summer. Ideal for students wanting to experience Spanish education without long-term commitment.

Do You Need a Visa to Study in Spain?

Your visa requirements depend on your nationality and course length.

Are you an EU/EEA citizen?
No visa needed. You can live and study in Spain freely using your national ID card or passport. You may need to register with local authorities, but no visa application is required.
Are you a non-EU/EEA citizen with a course 90+ days long?
Student visa required. You must apply for a Spanish student visa before travelling. Applications are submitted to the Spanish consulate in your home country. View full visa guide.
Are you a non-EU/EEA citizen with a course under 90 days?
Tourist entry may be possible. You may be able to enter Spain on a standard tourist visa or visa waiver (depending on your nationality) and study a short course. However, a formal student visa is more secure—consult with your consulate.

Pro tip: If you're unsure about visa requirements, use our eligibility check or book a consultation to confirm your specific circumstances.

How Much Does It Cost to Study in Spain?

Breakdown of tuition fees, living costs, and visa expenses.

Tuition Fees

  • Public universities (EU students): €1,000–€3,000/year for undergraduate; €1,500–€4,000/year for master's
  • Public universities (non-EU students): €3,500–€15,000/year for undergraduate; €5,000–€20,000/year for master's (varies by region and programme)
  • Private universities: €8,000–€30,000+/year depending on institution and programme
  • Language schools: €400–€2,000/month depending on course intensity and location

Living Costs

  • Budget cities (Granada, Salamanca, Malaga): €700–€900/month
  • Mid-range cities (Seville, Valencia): €900–€1,200/month
  • Expensive cities (Madrid, Barcelona): €1,200–€1,500+/month
  • Typical breakdown: Accommodation €400–€700, food €150–€250, transport €20–€50, leisure €100–€200

Visa & Administrative Costs

  • Student visa application: €80–€160 (consulate fee)
  • Residence permit (TIE): €10–€20 (issued in Spain after arrival)
  • Health insurance: €500–€1,500/year (may be included in tuition or offered by universities)
💡

Financial support: Spanish universities and language schools offer scholarships to international students. EU students may also qualify for Erasmus+ grants. Check with individual institutions.

Where to Study in Spain?

Spain's major university cities, each with distinct character and advantages.

Madrid

Spain's capital. Home to Universidad Autónoma, Complutense, and IE University. Most expensive but largest student population and job market. Vibrant nightlife, museums, endless entertainment.

Barcelona

Mediterranean coastal city. ESADE, UB, UAB are world-ranked. High cost of living but excellent quality of life. Beaches, architecture, tech scene. Catalan culture adds unique dimension.

Valencia

East coast, Mediterranean climate. Universidad de Valencia and Universitat Politècnica. More affordable than Madrid/Barcelona. Modern City of Arts and Sciences, great beaches.

Seville

Andalusian charm, rich history. Universidad de Sevilla. More relaxed pace, lower cost of living. Flamenco culture, historic architecture. Hot summers, mild winters.

Granada

University city in Andalusia. Universidad de Granada. Very affordable, vibrant student community. Stunning Alhambra, close to Sierra Nevada mountains. Popular with Spanish language students.

Salamanca

Historic university town. Universidad de Salamanca (founded 1218). Excellent for Spanish language study. Small, intimate atmosphere. Very affordable. Golden sandstone architecture.

Malaga

Coastal Andalusian city. Universidad de Málaga. Modern facilities, good weather year-round. Growing tech and creative sectors. Lower cost of living, beach lifestyle.

Bilbao

Basque Country, northern coast. Universidad del País Vasco. Modern, progressive city. Excellent museums and cultural scene. Higher cost than southern cities.

Can You Work While Studying in Spain?

Yes—with restrictions and authorisation.

Student visa holders are typically permitted to work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official university breaks (summer, Christmas, Easter). Some contracts offer flexibility during exam periods.

How to work:

  • Obtain work authorisation from your university's International Office
  • Register with the Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria) and obtain a tax identification number (NIF)
  • Most students work in hospitality, tutoring, freelance work, or administrative roles
  • Wages: €6–€12/hour depending on sector and experience. Enough to cover basic living costs
⚠️

Important: Work must be authorised and declared to tax authorities. Undeclared work can jeopardise your student visa status and residency. Always register properly.

What Happens After You Finish Studying?

Several pathways for graduates to stay and work in Spain.

Job Seeker Visa

Stay in Spain for 12 months to search for employment and apply for work permits. Available to non-EU graduates. Learn more

Work Visa

Transition directly to employment if you secure a job offer. Employers sponsor your work visa. Most common pathway for graduates.

Residency Pathways

Long-term residency (residence card) available after 5 years of continuous legal residence. Gateway to permanent residency. Explore options

Further Study

Progress to a master's or doctoral programme. Many students choose to deepen their expertise with postgraduate degrees.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I study in Spain in English?

Yes. Many universities offer bachelor's and master's degrees taught in English, especially at larger institutions like IE University, ESADE, and Universidad Autónoma de Madrid. Additionally, hundreds of accredited language schools across Spain offer intensive Spanish courses for foreigners. The choice depends on your goals—English-taught programmes for degree studies, or Spanish-intensive courses to learn the language.

How much does it cost to study in Spain?

University costs vary: public university degrees cost €1,000–€3,000/year for EU students, and €3,500–€15,000/year for non-EU students. Private universities are more expensive (€8,000–€30,000+/year). Language schools typically charge €400–€2,000/month depending on intensity and location. Living costs average €800–€1,500/month depending on the city. Overall, Spain is significantly cheaper than the UK or US for higher education.

Do I need a visa to study in Spain?

EU/EEA citizens do not need a visa. Non-EU/EEA nationals need a student visa if the course is 90 days or longer. For courses under 90 days, you may be able to enter Spain on a tourist visa. The student visa is renewable as long as you're enrolled and meet the requirements. Our full student visa guide covers eligibility, documentation, and application steps.

Can I work while studying in Spain?

Yes, with restrictions. Student visa holders are typically permitted to work up to 20 hours per week during term time, and full-time during official breaks. You need authorisation from your institution and must register with Spanish tax authorities (Agencia Tributaria). Most students find part-time work in hospitality, tutoring, or administration. Earnings are modest but sufficient to cover living costs.

What happens after I finish studying in Spain?

After graduation, non-EU/EEA graduates can apply for a job seeker visa (valid 12 months) to remain in Spain and find employment. If offered a work contract, you can transition to a work visa. Alternatively, if you've lived in Spain long enough and meet residency requirements, you may be eligible for long-term residency. Many students transition directly into employment or postgraduate studies.

Is Spain expensive to live in as a student?

No. Spain is one of Europe's most affordable countries to live in. A student budget of €800–€1,200/month covers accommodation, food, transport, and leisure in most cities. Larger cities like Madrid and Barcelona are costlier (€1,200–€1,500/month), while smaller cities like Granada or Salamanca are significantly cheaper (€700–€900/month). Student discounts are widely available on transport, museums, and entertainment.

Is Spain a good country to study in for international students?

Spain consistently ranks as one of the most popular study destinations in Europe. Key attractions: affordable tuition compared to the UK or US, excellent quality of life, warm climate, rich culture, widespread Spanish language (the world's second most spoken), strong university rankings in certain fields, and access to Europe.

How much does it cost to live in Spain as an international student?

Monthly living costs for a student in Spain range from approximately €700-1,200 depending on the city. Madrid and Barcelona are the most expensive. Cities like Valencia, Seville, Granada, and Salamanca are significantly cheaper. This includes accommodation, food, transport, and basic entertainment.

What are the top Spanish universities for international students?

Spanish universities with strong international reputations and good English-language provision include: Universidad Autónoma de Madrid, Universitat Pompeu Fabra (Barcelona), Universidad de Barcelona, IE Business School, ESADE, and several others. Rankings vary by field. Business schools in Spain (IE, ESADE) are internationally well-regarded.

Can I stay in Spain to work after graduating?

After completing your studies, you can apply to change your student visa to a work permit (if you have a job offer from a Spanish employer) or the Digital Nomad Visa (if working remotely). Planning this transition before your student visa expires is essential.
