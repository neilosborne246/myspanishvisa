Best Places to Live in Spain for Expats in 2026

Spain offers an extraordinary variety of places to call home — from Mediterranean beach cities to Basque pintxos bars to sun-drenched Andalusian streets. Here is how to choose the right one for you.

Spain is one of the world's most popular destinations for people relocating from English-speaking countries — and the range of places to settle is genuinely wide. Whether you are a retiree seeking sun and simplicity, a remote worker chasing cafes and culture, or a family looking for good schools and outdoor space, Spain has somewhere that fits. The challenge is navigating the options.

This guide focuses on the destinations that consistently attract and retain expats from the UK, US, Canada, Australia, and South Africa — cities and regions where English-speaking support networks, good private healthcare, and accessible visa pathways all combine with a high quality of life.

How to Choose Where to Live in Spain

Before diving into individual cities, it helps to set your decision framework. The key variables for most expats are:

  • Climate: The south and southeast get 300+ days of sunshine; the north is green, lush, and considerably wetter
  • Cost of living: Barcelona and Madrid command premium prices; Valencia, Seville, Malaga, and Alicante offer far better value
  • Expat community size: Coastal areas, Barcelona, and Madrid have large established expat communities; smaller cities offer more immersive Spanish living
  • Transport links: International connectivity matters if you plan regular trips home
  • Visa eligibility: Non-EU nationals need the right visa category — more on this at the end

Valencia: The Expat Sweet Spot

Valencia has steadily climbed to the top of expat satisfaction surveys, and for good reason. Spain's third-largest city manages to combine the infrastructure and culture of a major metropolis with a cost of living and pace of life that feels manageable rather than exhausting. The beach is four kilometres from the city centre. The old town — El Carmen — has medieval walls and tapas bars that fill every evening. The City of Arts and Sciences is one of the most striking pieces of modern architecture in Europe.

Practically: a one-bedroom apartment in a central neighbourhood like Ruzafa or Benimaclet costs €800–€1,100 per month to rent. A meal out for two, with wine, rarely exceeds €35. The metro, tram, and bus network is excellent and cheap. Valencia also has a large and growing international community — several English-speaking expat groups, international schools, English-speaking doctors and dentists — while retaining a genuine Spanish character that many Barcelona or Madrid expats find harder to find in their cities.

Valencia suits: retirees, remote workers, families, and anyone who wants genuine Spanish city life at a price point that makes sense.

Barcelona: Cosmopolitan Energy at a Price

Barcelona is, for many, the dream — and it largely lives up to it. The architecture is extraordinary (Gaudi's Sagrada Familia is still being finished, over a century after it began), the food scene is world-class, the beaches are right in the city, and its position on the Mediterranean coast makes it one of Europe's most liveable cities by almost any measure.

The trade-off is cost. Barcelona is the most expensive city in Spain for housing: a one-bedroom apartment in a desirable neighbourhood such as Eixample, Gracia, or Poblenou typically runs €1,400–€2,200 per month in 2026. The rental market has been tight for years. Day-to-day living costs are also higher than elsewhere in Spain.

Barcelona suits: those with higher budgets, creative professionals, people who want a truly international city with a major airport and excellent air connections, and anyone who relishes the unique cultural identity of Catalonia.

Madrid: Capital Living With Depth

Madrid divides opinion among expats. Some find it too landlocked, too hot in summer, and too intense. Others consider it the finest city in Spain — and make a strong case. As the capital, Madrid has Spain's best concentration of world-class museums (the Prado, the Reina Sofia, the Thyssen), an astonishing restaurant scene, and a public transport network (metro, bus, commuter rail) that makes car ownership largely unnecessary.

Housing is expensive compared to most Spanish cities but somewhat more accessible than Barcelona. A decent one-bedroom in neighbourhoods like Salamanca or Chamberí runs €1,300–€1,900 per month. The city's altitude (650m) gives it a more continental climate than the coasts — hot and dry in July and August, genuinely cold in winter, but very sunny year-round. Proximity to Barajas Airport, with direct flights to virtually everywhere, is a major plus.

Seville: Andalusian Character and Emerging Expat Scene

Seville is one of Spain's most beautiful cities and has long been underrated as an expat destination — partly because its summers are genuinely fierce (July and August regularly exceed 40°C). But the winters are glorious, the spring is spectacular (Semana Santa and the April Feria are unmissable), and the cost of living is among the most reasonable of any Spanish city of its size.

A one-bedroom apartment in Triana, La Macarena, or El Centro costs €750–€1,100 per month to rent. Tapas culture is alive and well — many bars still serve free food with drinks. The expat community has grown significantly in recent years, with a noticeable influx of American and UK remote workers drawn by the affordability and the quality of life. Direct flights from Seville Airport to the UK are improving, though Malaga or Madrid remains the main international hub for most.

Malaga: Costa del Sol's Urban Heart

Malaga has transformed dramatically over the past decade from a transit point (people flew through on their way to beach resorts) into a destination in its own right. The city now has a seriously good cultural offering — the Pompidou Centre, the Picasso Museum, the Carmen Thyssen — a beautifully renovated port area, and a restaurant scene that has moved well beyond beach chiringuitos.

It also has the warmest winters of any mainland Spanish city, one of Spain's major international airports with direct flights to the UK, US, and beyond, and quick access to the Costa del Sol's beaches, mountains, and smaller towns. Rent in central Malaga sits at €900–€1,200 for a one-bedroom; in surrounding towns it can be substantially less. The expat community is enormous and well-organised, with English-speaking services of every description readily available.

San Sebastian: Quality of Life in the Basque Country

San Sebastian (Donostia in Basque) is consistently rated one of the highest quality-of-life cities in Europe — and anyone who has visited understands why. The bay is stunning, the old town is charming, and the city has more Michelin stars per capita than almost anywhere on earth. The Basque Country also has a distinct culture, language, and identity that many expats find fascinating and welcoming.

The trade-offs: it rains more than the south (though the summers are beautiful), the cost of living is higher than most Spanish cities, and the expat community is much smaller and less English-centric than in coastal Andalusia. This suits some perfectly — it makes integration into Spanish (and Basque) life easier. But it requires more Spanish language ability and a greater willingness to adapt.

Alicante: Budget-Friendly Mediterranean Living

Alicante is one of the most underrated cities in Spain for expats seeking the Mediterranean lifestyle at a sensible price. The city has a 300,000-strong population, a beautiful old town dominated by a hilltop castle, a sandy city beach (Playa del Postiguet), good public transport, and an international airport with direct connections to most UK and European cities. Yet rental prices remain around 30–40% below Valencia or Barcelona — a one-bedroom in a central neighbourhood costs €650–€900 per month.

The Costa Blanca's sunny, dry climate — similar to the Costa del Sol but with a slightly cooler summer — makes it comfortable year-round. The large British expat community means English-speaking services are readily available. For remote workers, Alicante increasingly ticks boxes: digital nomad cafes, co-working spaces, and fast fibre internet are well established.

Granada: Culture, History and Great Value

Granada is one of Spain's most captivating cities — the Alhambra palace complex is among the most visited monuments in Europe — and it remains genuinely affordable by any comparison. The city has a large student population that keeps the social scene vibrant and prices low, and it holds one of Spain's best-preserved traditions of free tapas with drinks.

Rent for a central one-bedroom runs €550–€800 per month. The weather is excellent — hot in summer, cold and sometimes snowy in winter (the Sierra Nevada ski resort is just 30 minutes away), and beautifully mild in spring and autumn. The expat community is smaller than in coastal cities but growing, with a notable concentration of language teachers, remote workers, and younger creatives.

Which Visa Suits Your Lifestyle?

Non-EU nationals cannot simply move to Spain and start living. You need the right visa before you arrive. The two most relevant categories for English-speaking expats are:

Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV): For retirees and people with passive income (pensions, investments, rental income) who will not work in Spain. Requires approximately €2,400 per month in passive income for a single applicant, private health insurance, and a clean criminal record. Renewable annually, then in two-year blocks.

Digital Nomad Visa (DNV): For remote workers who earn their income from clients or employers outside Spain. Requires approximately €2,850 per month in verifiable remote income. Allows you to work remotely for non-Spanish companies while living in Spain legally.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best city in Spain to live in as an expat?
It depends on your priorities. Valencia consistently tops expat satisfaction surveys for its balance of quality of life, affordability, climate, and community. Barcelona suits those who want a cosmopolitan urban environment. Malaga is ideal for those prioritising sunshine and a relaxed coastal lifestyle.
Is it expensive to live in Spain as an expat?
Spain is significantly more affordable than the UK, US, Canada, or Australia. A single person can live comfortably in cities like Valencia or Seville on €1,500–€2,000 per month including rent. Barcelona and Madrid are more expensive, with comfortable living typically requiring €2,200–€3,000 per month.
Which visa do remote workers need to live in Spain?
Remote workers and digital nomads who earn their income from clients or employers outside Spain should apply for Spain's Digital Nomad Visa (DNV). It requires a minimum income of approximately €2,850 per month and allows you to work remotely while living in Spain legally. It is renewable and can eventually lead to permanent residency.
Where is the best place to live in Spain for families?
Valencia is widely recommended for families due to its combination of good international schools, affordable housing, safe neighbourhoods, beaches, and parks. Malaga and Alicante also have excellent family infrastructure. Barcelona offers the largest range of international schools but at a higher cost of living.
Is Barcelona or Madrid better for expats?
Both are outstanding cities. Barcelona appeals more to those who want a Mediterranean coastal environment, a strong creative and tech scene, and access to Catalonia's unique culture. Madrid suits those who prefer a classic European capital with excellent public transport, world-class museums, and arguably Spain's finest restaurant scene. Both are more expensive than most other Spanish cities.
How long can I stay in Spain without a visa?
Non-EU citizens (including UK nationals post-Brexit) can stay in Spain and the wider Schengen Area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. To stay longer, you must apply for a long-stay visa before you arrive — either the Non-Lucrative Visa, the Digital Nomad Visa, or another appropriate category.
