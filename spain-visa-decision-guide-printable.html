1 Quick Decision Tree — Find Your Spain Visa Route

Answer each question in order. Follow the YES or NO path until you reach a result.

Q1: Are you a citizen of an EU / EEA country or Switzerland?
YES
As an EU/EEA/Swiss national you have the right to live and work in Spain without a visa. You only need to register on the Registro Central de Extranjeros within 3 months of arrival.
→ No visa required. Register as EU resident.
NO — continue to Q2
You are a non-EU national and will need a visa or residence permit to stay in Spain long-term.
Q2: Do you plan to work while living in Spain?
YES — continue to Q3
Working in Spain (including remotely for foreign clients or employers) requires a work-authorising visa. The NLV does not permit any work.
NO
If you have sufficient passive income (pension, investments, rental income) and do not intend to work, you are likely eligible for the Non-Lucrative Visa.
→ Likely route: Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV)
Q3: Will you work remotely for foreign clients or employers (not Spanish companies)?
YES
The Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) is designed for remote workers and freelancers working for non-Spanish companies. You must earn at least 200% of IPREM (approx. €2,762/month in 2026).
→ Likely route: Digital Nomad Visa (DNV)
NO — continue to Q4
If you want to work for a Spanish employer or seek employment in Spain, different permits apply (work permit via job offer). This guide covers long-stay visas only.
Q4: Are you enrolled in a study programme at a Spanish institution?
YES
The Student Visa is the appropriate route for language students, university students, and vocational programme students at accredited Spanish institutions. You can work up to 20 hours per week.
→ Likely route: Student Visa
NO
Your situation may be more complex. Contact a visa specialist to discuss your options, which could include the Job Seeker Visa, Entrepreneur Visa, or other residence permits.

2 Non-Lucrative Visa (NLV) — At a Glance

NLV

Who it's for: Retirees, people with investment income, those with rental income or pension income who do not plan to work in Spain. Popular with UK, US, Australian, Canadian, and South African nationals.

Income Requirements (2026)

  • Single applicant: approx. €2,400/month (400% IPREM)
  • Per additional adult: + approx. €600/month (100% IPREM)
  • Per child: + approx. €150/month (25% IPREM)
  • Income must be passive (pension, investment, rental)
  • Bank savings may be accepted — confirm with your consulate

Key Facts

  • No working of any kind permitted (including remote work)
  • Applied for at Spanish consulate in home country
  • 1-year initial visa, renewable for 2+2 years
  • Must spend 183+ days per year in Spain
  • Leads to long-term residency after 5 years
  • Path to Spanish citizenship after 10 years (less for some)

Pros

  • Straightforward if you have passive income
  • Well-established, widely understood by consulates
  • Brings the whole family under one application
  • Path to permanent residency and citizenship
  • Access to Spanish public healthcare after padrón + TIE

Cons

  • No working permitted — any work risks deportation
  • Must maintain high passive income year on year
  • Tax residency in Spain means worldwide income taxed in Spain
  • Must remain in Spain 183+ days/year to maintain status
  • Renewal requires updated financial evidence each time

3 Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) — At a Glance

DNV

Who it's for: Remote workers employed by foreign companies, freelancers with foreign clients, and online business owners. Up to 20% of income can come from Spanish sources. Introduced under Spain's Start-Up Law in 2023.

Income Requirements (2026)

  • Single applicant: approx. €2,762/month (200% IPREM)
  • Per additional adult: + approx. €1,036/month (75% IPREM)
  • Per child: + approx. €345/month (25% IPREM)
  • Income must be from remote employment or freelance work
  • Bank statements + payslips / client invoices required

Key Facts

  • Remote work for foreign clients/employers permitted
  • Up to 20% income from Spanish sources allowed
  • 3-year initial residence authorisation
  • Renewable for 2 additional years (5 years total)
  • Beckham Law flat 24% tax rate available (apply within 6 months)
  • Leads to long-term residency after 5 years

Beckham Law — Special Tax Regime for DNV Holders

  • Flat rate of 24% tax on Spanish-source income up to €600,000 (vs. standard progressive rates up to 47%)
  • Foreign-source income generally exempt from Spanish tax (subject to double taxation treaty)
  • Available for up to 6 tax years
  • Must apply within 6 months of first social security registration — this window is critical
  • Filed via Modelo 149 with Agencia Tributaria — seek advice from a Spanish asesor fiscal

Pros

  • Can continue working remotely while living in Spain
  • Beckham Law provides significant tax advantages
  • 3-year initial validity (longer than NLV)
  • No requirement to stop working for Spanish companies entirely
  • Suitable for employed and self-employed applicants

Cons

  • Income threshold higher than NLV
  • Employer letter / client contract documentation is complex
  • Beckham Law window is easy to miss if not aware
  • Self-employed must register as autónomo and pay social security
  • Relatively new visa — some consulates less experienced with it

4 Student Visa (Visado de Estudios) — At a Glance

STUDENT

Who it's for: Non-EU nationals enrolling in language schools, universities, vocational programmes, or other accredited Spanish educational institutions. Popular for those who want to live in Spain as a pathway to longer-term residency.

Financial Requirements (2026)

  • Must demonstrate funds to cover living costs for study period
  • Typical requirement: approx. €7,200/year (600 IPREM/month)
  • Tuition fees must be paid or guaranteed
  • Health insurance covering Spain required
  • Evidence can be savings, parental support, scholarship

Key Facts

  • Acceptance letter from accredited Spanish institution required
  • Visa validity matches course duration
  • Can work up to 20 hours per week (part-time)
  • Cannot lead to permanent residency directly
  • Can switch to Job Seeker Visa or work permit after graduation
  • Applied for at Spanish consulate in home country

Pros

  • Lower financial threshold than NLV or DNV
  • Part-time work (20hrs/week) permitted
  • Good stepping stone into longer-term residency
  • Widely available visa with clear requirements
  • Family members (spouse/children) may apply as dependants

Cons

  • Must be genuinely enrolled in an accredited programme
  • Does not lead directly to permanent residency
  • Study visa time does not count toward 5-year residency requirement
  • Limited work rights (20 hrs/week maximum)
  • Must renew each year — tied to continuation of studies

6 Next Steps for Your Chosen Visa Route

If you're applying for the NLV:

  1. Confirm you meet the passive income threshold
  2. Purchase Spanish private health insurance (no copay)
  3. Order criminal record certificate (allows 8–12 weeks)
  4. Arrange Spain accommodation and obtain rental contract
  5. Get medical certificate + apostille + sworn translation
  6. Book consulate appointment (cita previa)
  7. Complete Form EX-01 and compile documents
  8. Attend appointment and submit application
  9. On arrival: register padrón and apply for TIE within 30 days

If you're applying for the DNV:

  1. Confirm remote income meets threshold (200% IPREM)
  2. Brief employer to prepare letter OR gather client contracts
  3. Purchase Spanish private health insurance (no copay)
  4. Order criminal record + arrange apostille
  5. Get medical certificate + apostille + sworn translation
  6. Commission sworn translations of all documents
  7. Submit via UGE (Spain) or consulate (abroad)
  8. On arrival: padrón → TIE → social security registration
  9. Apply for Beckham Law within 6 months of social security registration

If you're applying for a Student Visa:

  1. Choose and apply to your Spanish institution / language school
  2. Obtain acceptance letter (carta de admisión)
  3. Pay course fees and get receipt
  4. Purchase Spanish private health insurance
  5. Gather financial evidence (savings/support letter)
  6. Order criminal record + apostille
  7. Book consulate appointment
  8. Submit application with all documents
  9. On arrival: register padrón and apply for TIE within 30 days
