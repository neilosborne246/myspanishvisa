Spain Residency and Citizenship: Can You Hold Dual Nationality?

Can you hold dual Spanish citizenship? Learn Spain's dual nationality rules and how to maintain multiple nationalities.

One of the most important questions for those pursuing Spanish citizenship is whether you can hold dual nationality. Can you become a Spanish citizen while keeping your original nationality? The answer is complex and depends on your specific circumstances, your original nationality, and Spain's laws. This comprehensive guide explains Spain's dual nationality rules.

Spain's Official Position on Dual Nationality

Spain officially recognizes dual nationality. Spanish law does not require citizens to renounce previous nationalities. This means Spain will accept you as a Spanish citizen even if you retain your original nationality. However, this does not mean your home country will accept dual citizenship—that's determined by your original country's laws.

What Spain Allows

Spain permits Spanish citizens to hold multiple nationalities. Once you become a Spanish citizen, Spain will not force you to abandon your previous nationality. Your Spanish citizenship is valid and fully recognized regardless of whether you hold other nationalities simultaneously.

The Catch: Your Home Country's Laws

While Spain doesn't restrict dual nationality, your home country might. Some countries require citizens to renounce previous nationalities upon acquiring Spanish citizenship. Others recognize dual citizenship without issue. Still others fall in between—they don't recognize dual citizenship but don't actively enforce renunciation. Before pursuing Spanish citizenship, investigate your home country's stance on dual citizenship.

How Home Countries Treat Dual Nationality

Countries That Strongly Accept Dual Nationality

Many countries fully accept and recognize dual nationality, including the United Kingdom, Ireland, France, Germany, Canada, Australia, Israel, and several others. If you hold citizenship from these countries, you can maintain both your original nationality and Spanish citizenship without legal complications. Check your country's official position to be certain.

Countries That Prohibit Dual Nationality

Some countries, including China, Japan, and others, do not permit dual nationality. If you're a citizen of one of these countries, acquiring Spanish citizenship may be interpreted as automatic renunciation of your original nationality. However, enforcement varies. Some countries will cancel your citizenship upon learning you've acquired another nationality; others don't actively police dual citizenship.

Countries That Tolerate Dual Nationality

Many countries fall into a gray area—they don't officially recognize dual citizenship but don't prevent it either. If you become a Spanish citizen while holding citizenship from one of these countries, both nationalities may remain technically valid, even if your original country doesn't acknowledge the Spanish citizenship. This creates potential complications in edge cases.

Researching Your Country's Position

Before pursuing Spanish citizenship, contact your country's embassy in Spain or your country's nationality authority. Ask explicitly: "What is your country's position on citizens acquiring other nationalities?" Get a written response. Don't rely on assumptions or information from online forums—official guidance is essential.

Key Questions to Ask

Does my country permit dual nationality? If I acquire Spanish citizenship while maintaining my original nationality, will my original nationality be automatically revoked? Can I renounce my original nationality and later reacquire it? Are there any circumstances under which dual nationality is explicitly prohibited?

The Legal Reality: Spanish Citizenship and Your Original Nationality

If Your Country Accepts Dual Nationality

You can become a Spanish citizen and retain your original nationality without legal complications in Spain. You'll hold two valid passports (one Spanish, one from your home country) and two nationalities. Both are fully recognized and functional.

If Your Country Prohibits Dual Nationality

Acquiring Spanish citizenship may technically result in automatic loss of your original nationality according to your country's law. However, Spain will still recognize you as a Spanish citizen. You'll have one Spanish passport and one nationality (Spanish). Your original country may consider you to have lost your original nationality.

If Your Country Tolerates but Doesn't Recognize Dual Nationality

You might technically hold two nationalities, but your original country won't officially acknowledge the Spanish nationality. This creates practical complications: your original country might still issue you a passport (acknowledging your original nationality), while Spain recognizes you as Spanish. Travel, legal disputes, and property rights in your home country could be affected.

Practical Implications of Dual Nationality

Travel and Movement

If you hold dual nationality, you can use either passport to travel. Many people use their Spanish passport within the EU and their original passport for travel to other countries. This flexibility is advantageous. However, if your original country doesn't recognize your Spanish citizenship, complications can arise if you use your Spanish passport to travel to your home country.

Property and Inheritance

Dual nationality can complicate inheritance and property matters. Some countries have nationality-based inheritance laws or restrictions on property ownership by non-citizens. If you hold dual nationality, courts in both countries might claim jurisdiction over inheritance disputes or property rights. Estate planning becomes more complex.

Taxation

Some countries impose taxation based on citizenship, not residency. If you hold dual nationality, you might be liable for taxes in both countries. The US, for example, taxes citizens on worldwide income regardless of where they live. Before pursuing dual nationality, investigate taxation implications.

Military Service

Some countries require military service from citizens. If you hold dual nationality and one country has compulsory service, you might be obligated to serve. This is a serious consideration for some nationalities.

Employment and Government Access

Some government and sensitive positions require sole citizenship of that country. If you hold dual nationality, certain employment opportunities might be restricted. Additionally, background checks for security clearances become more complex.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I renounce one nationality and later reacquire it?
This depends on your original country's laws. Some countries allow repatriation (regaining citizenship after renunciation), while others don't. If you're concerned about losing your original nationality, research this before naturalizing in Spain.
What passport should I use when traveling?
Use your Spanish passport for travel within the EU. Use your original passport for travel to your home country and other destinations. If your original country doesn't recognize your Spanish citizenship, using a Spanish passport in your home country could create complications.
Does Spain recognize dual nationality with the USA?
Yes. The US accepts dual citizenship, and Spain allows it. American-Spanish dual nationality is straightforward and fully recognized by both countries. However, be aware the US taxes citizens on worldwide income.
What if my home country's laws change regarding dual nationality?
Laws can change, but your nationality status typically remains as it was at the time of acquisition. If your country changes its laws after you become Spanish, the change usually doesn't affect your existing status. However, future citizens might be treated differently.
Can I hold dual nationality and later move to another country?
Yes. Your dual nationality status is independent of where you live. You can move to any country while maintaining dual Spanish and original nationality (assuming both countries recognize the combination).
