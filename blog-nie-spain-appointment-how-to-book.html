NIE Spain Appointment: How to Book and What to Bring

Book your NIE appointment in Spain. Complete guide to the cita previa system, documents, timing, and what happens during your appointment.

Home / Blog / NIE Spain Appointment: How to Book and What to Bring

Booking a NIE appointment in Spain used to mean waking up at 5 AM to queue at the police station. Modern Spain has moved online. The cita previa system lets you reserve a specific time slot weeks in advance. But navigating it requires knowing which links to click, which documents matter, and what officials actually expect. Here's the complete guide.

Understanding Spain's Appointment System (Cita Previa)

Spain's cita previa is a government scheduling system. You book a time slot online, show up at that time, and avoid waiting in queues. For NIE appointments, you book through either the National Police website or your regional immigration office. The system has changed over time, so different regions use slightly different platforms.

How to Book Your NIE Appointment Online

Navigate to the Spanish National Police website (policía.es) or your region's immigration office website. Look for 'Cita Previa' or 'Agendar cita'. Select your province and the type of appointment (NIE for foreigners). Choose a date and time from available slots—popular times fill quickly, so book early. Confirm your appointment; you'll receive a confirmation email with your reference number.

The entire booking process takes 10 minutes if the site cooperates. Sites are sometimes slow during peak hours; try booking early morning or late evening for better speeds.

Alternative: Walk-In at the Police Station

Not all stations require appointments. In smaller cities, you can often walk in without booking. Arrive between 8 AM and 9 AM, take a number from the queue machine, and wait. This can take 1-4 hours depending on the station. It's riskier than booking an appointment, but it works if the online system fails or you need a NIE urgently.

Essential Documents to Bring

Bring your original passport (required; they won't accept a photocopy). Bring the completed Model EX-15 form (available at the police station, online, or downloadable from the website). Bring proof of your reason for the NIE: employment contract, property deed, university acceptance letter, or proof of self-employment. Bring official stamps (timbre) worth EUR 12-16, purchased at a tobacco shop. Bring 2-3 photocopies of each document.

Non-EU citizens should bring their visa or residence permit if they have one. Some regions require additional documents—call the police station 24 hours before your appointment to confirm.

How to Complete Form EX-15

The EX-15 form is straightforward but must be completed correctly. Fill in your full name, date of birth, nationality, passport number, and current address. Indicate your reason for the NIE: employment, self-employment, studies, retirement, or other. Sign and date the form. Most police stations have blank forms at the entrance; you can also download and print it beforehand.

What Happens During Your Appointment

Arrive 10 minutes early. Bring everything listed above. The officer will review your documents, ask questions about your reason for the NIE (answer honestly and clearly), take your fingerprints and photograph, and confirm your address. The entire process takes 15-30 minutes. Don't be late—missed appointments are rescheduled. Keep your appointment confirmation email on your phone as backup.

Timeline: When You'll Get Your NIE

In most cases, you receive your NIE number on the same day. The officer will print a letter (papel de asignación) with your nine-digit NIE. This letter is legally valid proof. Some regions mail you a physical card within 2-4 weeks, but the letter is sufficient for immediate use—open bank accounts, start jobs, and register with authorities with this letter.

If Your Appointment Gets Cancelled or Delayed

Appointments sometimes get cancelled due to staff shortages. The system will email you with a new proposed time. Accept the new time or book a different one immediately—slots fill fast. If the system crashes and you can't book, visit the station in person during morning hours and explain your situation. Keep records of your attempts.

Regional Differences in the NIE Process

Madrid and Barcelona have high demand; book 4-6 weeks ahead. Smaller provinces have appointments available within days. Some regions process NIEs through Oficinas de Extranjería (immigration offices) instead of police stations—check your specific region's requirements online before booking.

FAQ

Can I bring someone to translate at my appointment?

Yes, you can bring an official translator or a friend. Bring their identification. Some stations prefer official translators for non-EU citizens; call ahead to confirm.

What if I'm already in Spain without a visa?

You can still apply for a NIE. Bring proof of your reason (job offer, rental agreement, etc.). The officer may flag your situation, but non-residents can obtain a NIE.

How far in advance should I book my appointment?

Book 3-4 weeks ahead in major cities, 1-2 weeks in smaller areas. If you need it urgently, try walk-ins at small-town police stations early morning.

What if I miss my appointment?

Call the police station immediately if you cannot make it. You'll be rescheduled, usually within 1-2 weeks. Missing multiple appointments without notice may be flagged in your file.
